Compute efficiency

first seen 2026-W34 · 6 links · 3 min read

Compute efficiency asks how much intelligence a system produces for each dollar of hardware and electricity it consumes. Improvements come from two directions. On the model side, architectures that activate only a fraction of their parameters per token, better training data, and serving techniques that avoid recomputation all deliver the same quality for less compute. On the chip side, each hardware generation delivers more operations per watt. The two multiply, which is why the cost of a fixed level of capability has fallen so fast.

For an executive it is the lever that decides whether AI services can be profitable at current prices. A provider whose models become cheaper to run at fixed quality can cut prices and hold margin, and pricing alone cannot do that. It also has a darker side: because efficiency keeps improving, a capable attacker no longer needs a data centre, and a near-frontier model on a single high-end rig is now within reach of a well-resourced individual. And in the current shortage, re-examining which services are genuinely compute-intensive and consolidating the rest is the cheapest form of capacity.

Also seen as model efficiency gains

Deeper dive

What it measures. Compute efficiency is capability per unit of compute, and it can be decomposed into compute multipliers on the model side and the data side: how much less compute a better architecture needs, and how much less a better dataset needs, to reach a given loss or benchmark score. Recent analysis attributes most pretraining progress to the data side. On the serving side the measure is closer to Token efficiency and Performance per watt: how many useful tokens a system produces per joule and per accelerator-hour.

Where the gains come from. Mixture of experts architectures cut active parameters per token, which is the largest model-side saving. Serving research contributes the rest: repairing a stale KV cache after a document edit instead of recomputing it has been shown to need 13 to 21 times less recomputation while recovering most of the quality; characterising agents' resource use under concurrency reveals large wasted capacity; and GPU-to-GPU exchange protocols have delivered order-of-magnitude training speed-ups in specific settings. A separate argument holds that Recursive self-improvement, if it arrives, would show up first as models getting cheaper at a fixed intelligence level rather than smarter. Outside language models, learned weather forecasting replacing supercomputer-scale physics simulation is the same principle applied to a different workload.

Why it matters now. Efficiency is the second profit lever alongside pricing, and the one that makes price cuts sustainable; Revenue per gigawatt is the metric that captures it at data-centre scale. It shapes the competitive picture too: a fully usable small model that beats a larger frontier model on efficiency wins in manufacturing-style deployments where throughput per dollar decides. And it shapes the threat model, since the same gains lower an attacker's capital requirement to a bedroom-scale rig running a near-frontier model.

Trade-offs. Efficiency gains are partly consumed by demand growth, so total compute spend rises even as unit cost falls. Techniques that save compute, such as aggressive cache reuse or low active-parameter counts, can cost quality in edge cases. And efficiency measured on benchmarks may not transfer to agentic workloads whose cost is dominated by tool calls and CPU work rather than model inference.

What to watch. The rate of model-side multipliers versus chip-side gains, whether data-driven progress continues once high-quality data is exhausted, and how much of the efficiency dividend reaches customers as lower prices rather than staying with providers as margin.

Related terms

metric

Performance per watt

Performance per watt measures how much useful AI output, typically tokens or benchmark-weighted intelligence, a system produces per unit of electrical power.

11 linksmetric

Token efficiency

Token efficiency is the amount of useful capability a model delivers per token consumed or produced, whether measured as output tokens per task, accuracy per token, or the input-to-output ratio of a workload.

9 linkstechnique

Mixture of experts

Mixture of experts is a model architecture in which each token is routed to a small subset of specialised sub-networks, so a model with hundreds of billions of parameters activates only a fraction of them per step.

7 linksmetric

Revenue per gigawatt

Revenue per gigawatt is the annual revenue an AI operator earns per unit of electrical capacity it has built out, with power rather than chips or servers as the denominator.
