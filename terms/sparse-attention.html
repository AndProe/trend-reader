Sparse attention

first seen 2026-W36 · 6 links · 3 min read

In a standard transformer every token looks at every earlier token, so doubling the context quadruples the attention work and the memory that holds it. Sparse attention breaks that rule. Each token attends only to a chosen subset, such as nearby tokens, a few summary positions or the positions a learned selector picks. The model keeps most of the quality of full attention at a fraction of the cost.

For an executive the term is the reason long-context features have become cheap enough to offer. It is the long-context mechanism in a national frontier model and appears as a named component in recent Qwen releases, and it is one of the subquadratic families that serving hardware is now being designed around. The commercial effect is on price per token at long context and on how much conversation or document history a product can keep in view. The trade-off is that what the model skips it cannot use, so retrieval quality on very long inputs needs testing.

Also seen as sparse attention mechanisms

Deeper dive

How it works. Full attention computes a score between every pair of tokens, which costs time and memory proportional to the square of the sequence length and fills the KV cache with an entry for every position. Sparse variants restrict the pairs. Local or sliding-window patterns attend to a fixed neighbourhood; global or sink tokens give every position a few shared anchors; block-sparse patterns skip whole regions; learned or dynamic sparsity lets a lightweight selector choose which past positions each query reads. Modern designs combine several patterns across layers or heads so that some heads see locally and others see globally. The result is attention cost that grows roughly linearly with length, which is the enabling condition for Long context at serving scale.

Why it matters now. Sparse attention has moved from research into production models. A national frontier-scale model reports it as its long-context mechanism, and the Qwen family has shipped a named Qwen Sparse Attention variant in its Flash-Next line, which is where the Qwen releases compete on cost. Systems research now treats sparse and other subquadratic attention families as first-class targets when deciding how to disaggregate heterogeneous hardware, since the memory and compute profile differs from dense attention and the optimal split between prefill and decode hardware changes with it. In Sovereign AI models the technique is part of how a smaller programme reaches frontier context lengths without frontier budgets.

Trade-offs. Anything the pattern skips is invisible to that layer, so fixed patterns can miss long-range dependencies and learned selectors can miss what they were not trained to look for. Quality on needle-in-a-haystack retrieval and on tasks that need the whole document must be measured per model. Sparse attention also interacts with Mixture of experts routing and with cache management: fewer attended positions means less cache to read but a more complex access pattern, which hardware and kernels must support. Training a model with sparse attention from the start gives better results than retrofitting it, so the choice is made early and is hard to change.

What to watch. Whether frontier closed models disclose their attention patterns, how serving hardware specialises for subquadratic attention, and whether long-context benchmarks start reporting the attention pattern alongside the score.

Related terms

concept

Long context

Long context refers to model context windows of hundreds of thousands to a million tokens, which allow an agent or a user to place entire codebases, document collections or multi-hour recordings in front of the model at once.

KV cache

The KV cache is the per-token store of key and value vectors that a transformer keeps during generation so it does not recompute attention over the whole context on every step.

needed by 2 · 12 linkstechnique

Mixture of experts

Mixture of experts is a model architecture in which each token is routed to a small subset of specialised sub-networks, so a model with hundreds of billions of parameters activates only a fraction of them per step.

Sovereign AI models

Sovereign AI models are frontier-scale models trained by or for a state, in its own language and under its own control, as a hedge against depending on American frontier APIs.

Qwen is Alibaba's family of open-weight language and multimodal models, the most downloaded on the main model hub and the most common base for derivative models and academic research.
