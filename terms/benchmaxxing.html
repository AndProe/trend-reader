Benchmaxxing

first seen 2026-W33 · 11 links · 1 min read

Optimising a model release for industry-standard test sets, distinguished by Lambert from fraud: legitimate, expected, and a reason public scores overstate general capability.

Also seen as benchmark gaming, benchmark tuning

How the sources use it

  • Rahul Khedar, Mayank Malhotra, Avinash Karn (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-24) Cross-source (Interconnects, Zvi Mowshowitz); here the inverse case, where an under-specified prompt manufactures a capability gap that does not exist.
  • Daniel Eisner (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-24) Cross-source (Interconnects, Zvi Mowshowitz); generation rather than collection as the defence.
  • Nathan Lambert (Interconnects, 2026-09-22) Denain's overfitting test on the index lag, and Lambert on Chinese labs' benchmark incentives. Recurring;.
  • Latent Space (Latent Space, 2026-09-21) his reason for refusing public benchmarks, with the recollection that labs staffed teams to collect MMLU-shaped data. Cross-source (Interconnects, Frontier Labs, arXiv cs.AI).
  • Adrien Deliège, Claas Beger, Marc Van Droogenbroeck, Melanie Mitchell (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-18) Cross-source (Dwarkesh Patel, Frontier Labs, Interconnects, Latent Space); the shortcut-versus-rule ambiguity is the paper's opening problem, and the closing recommendation is a benchmark design that separates in-distribution from out-of-distribution rules.
  • Dwarkesh Patel (Dwarkesh Patel, 2026-09-11) Schulman's name for the easily verified environment distribution. Cross-source (Interconnects).
  • Ali Ansari, Haoran Sun, Andy Zeyi Liu et al. (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-11) cross-source (Dwarkesh Patel, Interconnects); the inverse case, that broken graders understate rather than inflate scores.

  • Google DeepMind (Frontier Labs, 2026-08-27) contamination is the involuntary version of what Lambert describes as deliberate tuning to public test sets. Cross-source (Interconnects).
  • Nathan Lambert (Interconnects, 2026-08-14) industry-standard test-set focus, distinguished from fraud.

Benchmarks

Vendor-reported model scores and the third-party tables (Artificial Analysis) that Berman reproduces with caveats; the opening argument of every release and, in his frame, less useful than cost per task.

Release cadence

How often labs ship models, treated by Lambert as the main competitive lever for Chinese labs (days versus months) and quantified by SemiAnalysis at one frontier release every 51 days in the agentic era.

Distillation attacks

Training a model on a rival's outputs to copy its capability; the fear behind access cut-offs, Anthropic's countermeasures in Fable 5.1, and Zuckerberg's call to legalise it domestically.

9 linksconcept

Evaluation awareness

Models detecting that they are under test and behaving differently, which undermines benchmarks and, in the OpenAI incident, did not stop the agents cheating anyway.

10 linksconcept

Open-closed capability gap

The measured distance between the best open and best closed models: 13 months to close in the scaling era, 8.5 in the reasoning era, about five in the agentic era.

15 linksproduct

ARC-AGI-3

An interactive-reasoning benchmark on which GPT-6 Astra claims 99.9 percent using OpenAI's continuous "Provider Adapter" harness (about $19,000, or $360 a game) against 62.7 percent for $26,000 on the standard harness, a result Chollet calls a step-function change.
