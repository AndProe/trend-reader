LLM-as-judge

first seen 2026-W36 · 6 links · 3 min read

LLM-as-judge means asking a model to grade another model's work. Instead of paying human raters to read every answer, an evaluator writes a rubric, hands the output and rubric to a model, and takes the score it returns. Panels of several models, often from different families and run blind, are used to reduce the bias of any single grader.

For an executive the technique is behind most of the numbers used to justify AI spend: benchmark leaderboards, internal quality scores, and the automated selection of training data all lean on it. The risk is shared error. A judge model tends to favour outputs that resemble its own, can be swayed by wording that a human would ignore, and grades replayed responses differently from live ones. Any procurement or deployment decision that rests on a model-graded score should ask who the judge was, whether it was one of the contestants, and whether a human sample confirmed it.

Also seen as model-as-judge, AI grader

Deeper dive

How it works. A judge prompt contains the task, the candidate output, a rubric and sometimes a reference answer; the judge returns a score, a ranking or a pass-fail verdict. Variants include pairwise comparison, panels of judges from multiple model families run blind, and calibrated judges used to scale a small set of expert ratings across a large corpus. The technique appears in three roles across the current evidence: as the grader of record for a benchmark, as a quality filter inside a data-selection pipeline, and as a classifier of behaviour, for example a lab using its own model to label automation levels in internal telemetry.

Why it matters now. Human grading does not scale to the volume of outputs that agentic systems and reinforcement learning produce, so model judges sit inside Benchmarks, Internal benchmarks, AI code review and training methods such as RLCD. That makes the judge a single point of failure. One paper reports a 12.5-point score gap on replayed versus live responses and attributes it to judge sensitivity rather than target behaviour. Another flags that its single fixed judge was one of the models being evaluated. A frontier lab using its own model to classify its own compute usage notes the shared-error problem explicitly: if judge and subject fail the same way, the score hides it.

Trade-offs. Cheaper judges are being proposed that match the agreement of large-model judges at a fraction of the cost, which helps volume but not independence. Multi-family panels reduce correlated bias but multiply cost and still cannot see errors common to all current models. Rubric design is the hidden variable: a vague rubric lets the judge substitute its own preferences, while an over-specified one rewards surface compliance, which is the same failure mode as Reward hacking. Self-verification, where a model checks its own output, is the degenerate case with the highest correlation between grader and graded.

What to watch. Adoption of blinded multi-family panels as a reporting norm, published judge-versus-human agreement rates alongside every model-graded score, and whether regulators or auditors accept model-graded evidence for claims about safety or productivity.

Related terms

technique

Internal benchmarks

Internal benchmarks are evaluations an organisation writes for its own workloads, testing a model or agent on the tasks it will actually perform rather than on public leaderboards.

needed by 1 · 13 linkstechnique

Self-verification

Self-verification is an AI system checking its own work before declaring it done, either inside the model's reasoning or through a harness that runs tests, reads telemetry, inspects screenshots and selects among candidate answers.

6 linkstechnique

AI code review

The use of language models to review code changes, from a single bot that comments on a pull request to multiple specialist agents that gate merges.

8 linkstechnique

Benchmarks

Benchmarks are standardised tests that score AI models on fixed tasks, from vendor-reported tables at each release to third-party leaderboards and research suites.

needed by 11 · 29 linkstechnique

RLCD

RLCD, reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions, is a training method that teaches a model to answer many typed questions about a single input with calibrated probabilities in one pass, instead of generating text.
