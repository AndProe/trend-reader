Zero Data Retention

first seen 2026-W34 · needed by 1 · 10 links · 3 min read

Zero Data Retention means the model provider processes a request and keeps nothing: no logs of prompts, no stored completions, no use of either to train future models. It began as a strict enterprise policy demanded by large customers and cited by companies as the setting that protects their clients. It is now a standard tier, priced as a surcharge over the ordinary rate, offered by OpenAI and contrasted with Anthropic's approach of inspectable safeguards with retention for oversight.

For an executive, ZDR is both a procurement requirement and a trade. It removes the data-leakage worry that held back adoption of some models, but it also removes the provider's ability to detect misuse; one lab reports a reseller abusing ZDR to hide gain-of-function biology traffic and argues retention is needed for observability in that domain. Buyers should decide which workloads need ZDR, what it costs, and whether they trust vendor promises about what improving models with customer data actually means.

Also seen as ZDR, zero data retention policy, no-retention tier

Deeper dive

How it works. A ZDR agreement commits the provider to stateless processing: request in, response out, nothing persisted beyond what is needed to serve the call, and nothing entering training data. It is typically an enterprise contract term or an API tier. Newer architectures reconstruct the property differently, replacing statelessness with server-side memory encrypted under keys the provider does not hold, so persistence exists but is unreadable by the vendor. ZDR is a component of Enterprise data governance and the baseline against which Two-tier data pricing sets its spread: the standard tier is a ZDR-like promise, the contributor tier waives it for a discount.

Why it matters now. Retention policy has become a competitive variable. One lab's retention walk-back was read as the most interesting move of its week and attributed to pressure from a rival offering ZDR; the barrier that ZDR removes is thought to explain a frontier model's 11 percent enterprise share. Retention is also priced explicitly as a surcharge, which turns a policy into a line item. Against this, a misuse report describes a reseller using ZDR to hide gain-of-function traffic and argues that biology work requires retention for observability, and a lab's inspectable safeguards are positioned as the alternative to blind statelessness. A separate question remains unanswered: what providers mean when they say they use customer data to improve models.

Trade-offs. ZDR protects customers from leakage and training exposure but blinds the provider to abuse, weakens incident investigation and can conflict with safety obligations in dangerous domains. Surcharges price privacy, which disadvantages smaller buyers. Encrypted persistence offers a middle path but requires trust in the key architecture. For regulated enterprises, Data sovereignty rules may demand more than ZDR, such as residency and auditability.

What to watch. Whether ZDR remains a surcharge or becomes default, how regulators treat retention-for-safety arguments in biology and cyber, and whether Frontier model pricing converges on retention tiers as a standard axis.
