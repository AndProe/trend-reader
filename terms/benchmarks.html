Every model release opens with a benchmark table: the vendor's scores on a chosen set of tests, usually with competitors named only by tier. Independent scoreboards re-run models under common conditions, and researchers publish new suites weekly, increasingly with constructed ground truth, deterministic controls or generated question sets that renew forever to avoid contamination. Reviewers reproduce vendor claims with caveats, and the more useful reading has shifted from rank to cost-versus-score plots.

For an executive, benchmarks are a screening tool with three limits. Scores depend on the harness and even the prompt envelope around the model, so the same model produces different numbers in different hands. Public test sets leak into training, deliberately or not, so scores overstate general capability. And a benchmark measures a task family, not your workflow, which is why cost per task on your own work is the better decision metric. Use vendor tables to shortlist, independent indexes to compare, and internal evaluation to decide.

Also seen as vendor benchmarks, benchmark table, leaderboards

Deeper dive

What it measures. A benchmark fixes a set of tasks, a grading rule and, ideally, the conditions of the run, then reports a score. The families in current use span knowledge and reasoning questions, coding tasks such as SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench, computer use such as OSWorld, job-relevant work such as GDPval, and the multi-step Agent benchmarks that grade a trajectory rather than an answer. Research suites increasingly control what earlier ones did not: deterministic baselines, ground truth constructed rather than observed, reference decisions for safety-critical choices, and generated corpora that remove contamination structurally. Vendors report their own scores; a third-party ranking such as the Artificial Analysis index supplies a common basis.

Why it matters now. Benchmarks are the opening argument of every release and the yardstick of every comparison, including the Open-closed capability gap. They are also the training signal: reinforcement learning environments are built from the same task families, so a benchmark's design shapes what models learn. The volume of new suites, several a week, reflects both saturation of older ones and a shift in what needs measuring, from answers to actions, trust and taste. Labs have begun citing third-party scores with numbers and named owners rather than only internal figures, and have argued that scores only mean something when the questions were unseen.

Trade-offs. Four weaknesses recur. Harness dependence: an uncontrolled prompt envelope can dominate a result, and the same model swings by tens of points across scaffolds. Contamination and tuning: public test sets leak into training, and Benchmaxxing optimises releases for them, so public scores overstate general capability. Grading errors: expert re-grading has found broken evaluations in leading suites, in some cases understating rather than inflating scores. And validity: a model can score well while its outputs lose validity in ways quality metrics do not see. Vendor tables add selective reporting, with competitors unnamed and charts occasionally misleading. The response is a movement toward Cost per task plots, internal benchmarks on real workflows, and double-blind external evaluation.

What to watch. Whether fixed-harness reporting becomes standard so scores are reproducible across labs. Whether renewable, generated benchmarks displace static test sets. And whether procurement shifts from public scores to task-based internal evaluation, which would make the vendor table a formality.

Related terms

metric

Cost per task

Cost per task is the dollar cost of getting a unit of work actually completed by an AI system, measured at a given quality level, rather than the price per token or the score on a benchmark.

needed by 1 · 30 linksproduct

Artificial Analysis index

The Artificial Analysis index is a third-party composite ranking of AI models, aggregating scores across standard evaluations into a single intelligence figure, with companion indexes for cost and for speech quality.

11 linksconcept

Benchmaxxing

Benchmaxxing is the practice of tuning a model release so it scores well on the industry-standard public test sets, which is legitimate and expected rather than fraudulent.

14 linkstechnique

Agent benchmarks

Agent benchmarks are evaluation suites that score an AI system on multi-step tasks inside an environment, such as repairing a code repository, operating a terminal or completing a task by voice, rather than on single-answer questions.

needed by 7 · 23 linksconcept

Open-closed capability gap

The open-closed capability gap is the measured lag between the best openly downloadable model and the best closed frontier model, usually expressed as the number of months it takes open models to reach a given capability level.

16 linksproduct

OSWorld

OSWorld is a benchmark of cross-application computer-use tasks run in controllable operating-system environments, on which agent scores rose from around 12 percent at its 2024 launch to the low-to-mid 80s by mid-2026, past an estimated human baseline of about 72 percent.
