AI research tools

first seen 2026-W35 · 6 links · 3 min read

AI research tools range from a model that gathers and summarises sources to an agent fleet that reads sequencing data and proposes experiments. Practitioners' verdict is consistent: useful for source gathering, unreliable for reasoning. The value sits in coverage, since a model can read every paper in a field, and in speed, since agents run instruments around the clock. Judgement about what matters remains human; one argument holds that the most accelerating AI in science is not a model trained on scientific data but one that helps a scientist decide what is worth doing.

2026 produced early published results: an enzyme finding from a lab-run agent fleet, with the human review step stated explicitly, and language-model agents on both teams competing on a Millennium Prize problem. Labs now describe speeding discovery in any domain with programmable instruments as a product goal. For an R&D executive the decisions are where to automate the pipeline, how to keep verification human, and how to stop the tools converging on the same hypotheses.

Also seen as AI scientist, vibe research, AI for research

Deeper dive

How it works. The stack has three levels. Reading tools search, retrieve and summarise literature, where language models are strong and the failure mode is fabricated citations, which is why practitioners restrict them to source gathering. Pipeline tools automate data processing, such as agentic frameworks for sequencing data, where agents select, configure and interpret while the analysis itself runs in conventional code. Workspace tools attempt the whole loop: a scientist workspace that plans experiments, drives programmable instruments, records notebooks per session, and iterates. These are AI agents applied to a domain where the verifier is an experiment rather than a test suite, which makes them slower and riskier than coding agents. Some research loops now include an explicit anti-convergence term so that automated exploration does not collapse onto the same candidate every run.

Why it matters now. The bottleneck has moved. When experiments are cheap and agents tireless, the scarce resource is taste about what to try, which is why the foundry-versus-navigator framing appeals: the foundry runs experiments at scale, the navigator decides where to go. Labs are building toward the foundry with agents that supervise and adjust experiments in real time, and offering scientific models such as weather systems as data products for researchers. The mathematical results of September show the loop working where verification is cheap; see AI mathematical discovery. The research agent that optimises itself is the boundary case where tool and researcher become the same object; see Recursive self-improvement.

Trade-offs. Coverage without judgement produces confident, plausible, wrong syntheses, so Human oversight of conclusions is not optional. Automation of pipelines removes the graduate-student labour that trained the next generation of scientists. Convergence is a real risk: agents exploring the same literature propose the same experiments. Reusable Skill library components help reproducibility but also spread the same defaults.

What to watch. Whether published results from agent fleets grow, whether journals require disclosure of agent involvement, and whether workspace tools move beyond fields with cheap verification.

AI agents

Software systems in which a language model plans, calls tools and acts over many steps toward a goal rather than answering a single prompt.

Automated alignment research

Automated alignment research is the use of AI systems to find and fix alignment failures in other AI systems, on the premise that AI researchers can do safety work faster and more cheaply than humans.

6 linksconcept

Human oversight

Human oversight is the practice of keeping a person in the loop over an agent's actions, with authority to approve, correct or stop them, and the review burden that this imposes.

15 linkstechnique

Skill library

A skill library is a store of reusable, named procedures that an agent can load and apply, whether taught by demonstration, written by hand as instructions, derived from failure diagnoses or compiled into executable artefacts.

9 linksconcept

AI mathematical discovery

Models producing new mathematical results rather than reproducing known ones, most visibly the prime-gap bounds attributed to GPT-6 Astra and the Navier-Stokes proof that followed a week later.
