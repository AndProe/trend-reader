Cost per task

first seen 2026-W35 · 20 links · 2 min read

Dollars per completed task rather than per token or per benchmark point; Berman's preferred economic benchmark, applied per effort level by Willison and now adopted in the labs' own charts.

Also seen as cost per task completed, cost per intelligence index task, cost per completed task

How the sources use it

  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-22) Berman's stated metric, and his explanation for the 20 versus 40 percent gap.
  • Trang Nguyen, Eulrang Cho, Bingqing Chen, Tim Dettmers (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-22) Cross-source (Frontier Labs, Latent Space, Tomasz Tunguz); per-rollout savings are the paper's headline unit.
  • Nikita Agarwal, Nivedit Jain (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-22) Cross-source (Frontier Labs, Latent Space, Tomasz Tunguz); the Terra-against-Sol comparison is stated as a cost substitution.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-21) the metric Berman applies before any benchmark, and the reason he flags Artificial Analysis on token usage.
  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-21) $0.0004 a case against $0.0304 and $0.0836, the unit in which Tunguz states the saving. Recurring (W38); cross-source (Berman, Willison, arXiv, Frontier Labs, Latent Space).
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-18) free output tokens and fractional-cent inputs as Berman's price lens.
  • Yining She, Lei Lin (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-18) Cross-source (Frontier Labs, Latent Space, Matthew Berman, Simon Willison, Tomasz Tunguz); the paper's optimisation target is the cost of evaluation, expressed as the share of the suite executed.
  • Run-Ze Fan, Zihao Zhang, Simin Ma et al. (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-17) cross-source; planning and a bash-only action space are reported as cost savers for stronger models.
  • Haozhe Liu, Tian Ye, Sensen Gao et al. (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-17) cross-source; the savings are reported per hour of agent operation rather than per task.
  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-17) $1.540 against $0.441 per resolved task. New for this source; cross-source (arXiv, Frontier Labs, Latent Space, Matthew Berman, Simon Willison).
  • Vishwas Sathish, Viresh Ranjan, Xinliang Zhu et al. (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-07) cross-source; the search-aware penalty and lower inference cost are efficiency claims.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-01) his core metric, contradicting the price-cut headline.
  • Simon Willison (Simon Willison, 2026-09-01) Berman's lens, applied per effort level.
  • Google DeepMind (Frontier Labs, 2026-09-01) the lab reports cost and accuracy per task together and claims a Pareto position.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-08-29) Berman's preferred economic benchmark (dollars per completed task, not just raw capability score).
  • Google DeepMind (Frontier Labs, 2026-08-27) the draft-then-finalise workflow prices iteration at a third of final-render cost.

Related terms

technique

Benchmarks

Vendor-reported model scores and the third-party tables (Artificial Analysis) that Berman reproduces with caveats; the opening argument of every release and, in his frame, less useful than cost per task.

needed by 11 · 29 linksconcept

Reasoning effort levels

The effort dial (low to max) that sets how much a model thinks per request, with a 33x cost range on one prompt in Willison's test and two settings apparently identical.

7 linksmetric

Token efficiency

Capability per token consumed or produced: the gap between intelligence rank and verbosity rank, the 30-to-1 input-to-output ratio of agentic work, capability per training token in A.X K2.

9 linksconcept

Price elasticity

How much demand responds to model price; Ramp's data show buyers do not pay premiums proportional to capability, the evidence behind Tunguz's 'who buys SOTA' argument.

4 linkstechnique

Smart model routing

Sending each request to the cheapest model that can handle it, frontier reserved for hard cases; Ramp's router, OpenRouter's marketplace and the mechanism behind local-model cost ceilings.

11 linkstechnique

Deployment qualification

Statistically qualifying an agent together with its oversight policy against a reliability target before deployment (READY), or a constrained-success metric that counts only acceptable completions; the answer to benchmarks that do not predict production.
