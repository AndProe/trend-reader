Cost per task

first seen 2026-W35 · 23 links · 3 min read

Model vendors quote prices per million tokens and publish benchmark scores. Neither tells a buyer what it costs to get a job done. A cheaper model that uses three times as many tokens, retries more often or needs a higher reasoning setting can cost more per finished task than an expensive one. Cost per task fixes the unit: dollars per resolved ticket, per merged pull request, per classified case or per completed benchmark item, at a stated accuracy. It has moved from an analyst's preferred metric to the axis the labs themselves now use in their launch charts.

For an executive it is the only sensible basis for comparing vendors and for setting budgets. The September releases show why. Anthropic's claim of a 40 percent saving for Opus 5.5 applies to typical workloads, not list price, and one estimate puts the real figure closer to 20 percent on some tasks. A harness swap changed the cost of a resolved coding task from $1.54 to $0.44 with no change of model. Any procurement or build-versus-buy decision should be made on measured cost per task on the organisation's own work.

Also seen as cost per task completed, cost per intelligence index task, cost per completed task

Deeper dive

What it measures. Cost per task divides total spend, including retries, tool calls, reasoning tokens and failed attempts, by the number of tasks completed to an agreed standard. It therefore captures Token efficiency, the effect of Reasoning effort levels, cache terms and harness overhead in one figure. Labs now report cost and accuracy per task together and claim positions on the resulting frontier, and independent evaluations apply the metric per effort level, since the same model can sit at several points on the curve.

Why it matters now. The metric has reorganised how the September price war is read. Same-day comparisons of Claude Opus 5.5 against GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna were drawn on a cost-per-task axis rather than list price, and the finding that Sol delivers the same capability as Fable 5 at a third of the cost is stated in this unit. The vendor qualifier that Opus 5.5's 40 percent saving applies to typical workloads is a cost-per-task statement, as is the observation that the saving on some tasks is nearer 20 percent. On the deployment side the figures are smaller but starker: a specialised decision model at $0.0004 a case against $0.03 and $0.08 for general models; a resolved coding task at $0.44 under one harness against $1.54 under another; an automated engineer priced under $6 an hour; and research claims of 63-fold practical savings from routing. Enterprise governance tools frame their crossover as cost per session, which is the same idea at a coarser grain, and Smart model routing exists to minimise it.

Trade-offs. The metric is only as good as the task definition and the quality bar. It is easy to lower cost per task by lowering the standard, and vendors' typical workloads may not resemble a buyer's. It also hides variance: a model that is cheap on average but occasionally very expensive can be worse in practice. Aggregate indices that ignore token usage can rank models in the wrong order, which is why flagging token consumption alongside scores has become a standard criticism. The contrast with Frontier model pricing is deliberate: list price is what the vendor controls, cost per task is what the buyer experiences, and Cache pricing often separates them.

What to watch. Whether labs publish cost-per-task curves as a standard part of releases, whether independent benchmarks adopt it as the primary axis, and how Price elasticity plays out as falling cost per task expands the set of tasks worth automating.

Related terms

technique

Benchmarks

Benchmarks are standardised tests that score AI models on fixed tasks, from vendor-reported tables at each release to third-party leaderboards and research suites.

needed by 11 · 29 linksconcept

Reasoning effort levels

Reasoning effort levels are the settings, typically low, medium, high, extra high and max, that control how much internal thinking a model does before answering a request.

9 linksmetric

Token efficiency

Token efficiency is the amount of useful capability a model delivers per token consumed or produced, whether measured as output tokens per task, accuracy per token, or the input-to-output ratio of a workload.

9 linksconcept

Price elasticity

Price elasticity is the degree to which demand for a model or AI service changes when its price changes.

4 linkstechnique

Smart model routing

Smart model routing sends each request to the cheapest model that can handle it, reserving frontier models for the steps that need them and often running a small local model as the dispatcher.

12 linkstechnique

Deployment qualification

Deployment qualification is the practice of statistically qualifying an AI agent, together with the oversight policy around it, against an explicit reliability target before it is put into production.
