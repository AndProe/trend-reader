Electricity prices

first seen 2026-W33 · 7 links · 3 min read

Electricity prices have become the most visible cost of the AI buildout. Tallies in the vault include a 6.9 percent rise in retail power prices attributed to data-centre demand, household bill increases of 17 to 24 percent in the PJM region of the eastern United States, and warnings that data-centre load could triple natural gas prices. The Ohio bill increases are described as the trigger for the wave of local moratoriums.

It matters now because the causal story is contested and the answer decides where the blame lands. One analysis attributes most of the PJM rise to the design of the capacity auction and to a modelling mistake that cost ratepayers around twelve billion dollars, rather than to data-centre load. If that is right, data centres are a scapegoat and the fix is market reform; if the load story is right, every new gigawatt raises bills. For an executive the exposure is in power contracts, siting decisions, and the reputational cost of being named on a utility bill.

Also seen as power prices, ratepayer bills, gas price shock

Deeper dive

How it works. Retail electricity prices carry three components: the energy itself, the transmission and distribution network, and in regions with a capacity market a charge that pays generators to be available at peak. Data-centre load can raise all three, by consuming energy, by requiring Grid interconnection upgrades, and by tightening the supply margin that the capacity auction prices. The 17 to 24 percent bill increases in PJM arrived through the capacity charge, which is why the mechanism matters: a capacity price is set by auction rules and forecasts, not by metered consumption alone.

Why it matters now. The bills landed on households at the same time as announcements of multi-gigawatt campuses, and the coincidence became the organising fact of the Data center backlash. Ohio's increases are credited with triggering its moratorium wave, local power prices are the stated public worry behind the industry's flexible-load alliance, and a 6.9 percent national rise has been tied to data-centre demand. A separate concern is gas: if new load is met by gas turbines, the forecast is for gas prices to as much as triple, which would feed back into every ratepayer's bill regardless of how the capacity market is designed.

Trade-offs and the dispute. The competing analysis argues that most of the PJM increase came from auction design and from a modelling error that wasted about twelve billion dollars of ratepayers' money, and that the grid operator proposes to repeat the approach. On that reading the data centres are absorbing blame for a regulatory failure, and moratoriums will not lower bills. The Hyperscalers have a stake in this argument being accepted, and are simultaneously hedging by moving to Behind-the-meter power that bypasses the grid, and by promising to curtail at peak. Whether curtailment is credible at scale is untested.

What to watch. The next capacity auction results and any reform of the forecast method, whether flexible-load commitments are enforced or merely announced, and whether improving Performance per watt reduces the load growth forecasts on which the auction prices depend.

Data center backlash

Data center backlash is the local, state and electoral opposition to large AI data centres, driven by concern over electricity bills, water use, emissions and grid strain.

Hyperscalers

Hyperscalers are the largest cloud providers, AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, together with Oracle and increasingly the frontier labs themselves, that buy most AI accelerators, issue most AI-related debt and build the gigawatt campuses.

needed by 1 · 16 linksmetric

Performance per watt

Performance per watt measures how much useful AI output, typically tokens or benchmark-weighted intelligence, a system produces per unit of electrical power.

11 linksconcept

Grid interconnection

Grid interconnection is the process, run through a grid operator's queue, by which a data centre or power plant is connected to the utility grid.

7 linksconcept

Data center jobs

Data center jobs are the employment created by the AI buildout in construction, power infrastructure and the skilled trades, set against the jobs AI is expected to remove.

5 linksconcept

Behind-the-meter power

Behind-the-meter power is electricity generation built on the data-centre site itself, bypassing the grid, its interconnection queue and its capacity markets.
