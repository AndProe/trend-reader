Performance per watt

first seen 2026-W34 · 11 links · 3 min read

Performance per watt is an efficiency ratio: tokens generated, or some measure of intelligence delivered, divided by the power consumed to produce it. It extends a long-standing computing metric, computations per joule, to language models and the hardware that serves them. Chip designers, including OpenAI with its Jalapeño chip, now design for this ratio directly, and hardware comparisons are increasingly argued in tokens per second per megawatt rather than per dollar.

For an executive the metric matters because most large AI deployments are capped by the power available at a site, not by capital. If a facility has a fixed number of megawatts, the only way to serve more demand is to squeeze more tokens from each watt, which determines revenue per site. It also affects consumer products: an on-device model on a phone or headset is limited by battery and heat, so extra model capability delivers less once the power envelope is fixed. Procurement should weigh energy efficiency alongside headline speed.

Also seen as intelligence per watt, tokens per megawatt, perf per watt

Deeper dive

What it measures. The numerator is output, which can be raw tokens per second, tokens weighted by model quality, or task completions; the denominator is power draw, measured at the chip, the rack or the whole facility. Rack-level figures of 125 to 135 kW for a doubling of performance illustrate how fast both sides of the ratio are rising. Related units include tokens per joule for individual operations and tokens per HBM wafer when memory rather than power is the scarce input. The metric depends heavily on workload: prefill-heavy agentic traffic, long-context decoding and low-latency interactive serving each give a different figure on the same hardware.

Why it matters now. Power is the constraint that Behind-the-meter power deals, grid interconnection queues and community opposition all express, so the industry has adopted an efficiency framing in which Revenue per gigawatt is the business metric and performance per watt is its engineering counterpart. Custom silicon is the main response: the Jalapeno chip was designed around tokens per watt rather than peak FLOPs, and vendor claims about per-watt gains are now contested in detail, with independent analysts disputing Nvidia's own numbers on tokens per second per megawatt. Research on disaggregated systems that split attention types across heterogeneous hardware reports its gains in tokens per joule for the same reason.

Trade-offs. Optimising for watts can cost latency, since the most efficient configurations batch heavily and serve each user more slowly, which collides with the demand for interactive agents. Efficiency gains at the chip level can be eaten by memory, networking and cooling overheads, so chip-level and facility-level figures diverge. In edge devices there are diminishing returns: more capable processors and models deliver less when everything is squeezed around a fixed battery, speaker or thermal budget. And the metric says nothing about whether the tokens were useful, which is why Energy efficiency arguments increasingly pair it with cost per task.

What to watch. Whether independent benchmarks for tokens per megawatt become standard in procurement, how custom chips compare with merchant GPUs once measured on real agentic workloads, and whether per-watt gains keep pace with the growth in demand for inference.

Jalapeno chip

Jalapeno is OpenAI's first in-house inference chip, built with Broadcom, designed from mid-2024 and taped out in about sixteen months.

Custom silicon

Custom silicon is the accelerator hardware designed by the companies that use it, such as Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium, Meta's MTIA and OpenAI's Jalapeño, rather than bought from Nvidia.

needed by 2 · 9 linksconcept

Behind-the-meter power

Behind-the-meter power is electricity generation built on the data-centre site itself, bypassing the grid, its interconnection queue and its capacity markets.

9 linksmetric

Energy efficiency

Energy efficiency, in the AI context, is the measured energy a system consumes per unit of model work, typically expressed as tokens per joule or GPU energy per task.

4 linksmetric

Revenue per gigawatt

Revenue per gigawatt is the annual revenue an AI operator earns per unit of electrical capacity it has built out, with power rather than chips or servers as the denominator.

8 linksconcept

Humanoid robot economics

Humanoid robot economics is the per-unit cost structure of general-purpose robots, dominated by the onboard compute, how much of the time it is used, and whether the planning model runs on the robot or in a data centre.
