Frontier model pricing

first seen 2026-W16 · needed by 2 · 18 links · 1 min read

Per-token list prices for top-tier models ($10 and $50 per million tokens for Astra, matching Fable exactly), rising with each release even as cost per task falls.

Also seen as model pricing, token prices, API pricing

How the sources use it

  • Forward Future team (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-22) Grok 4.7 held at Grok 4.6's price with $2 and $6 per million tokens.
  • Simon Willison (Simon Willison, 2026-09-22) a nine-model price table published on release day, with two vendors cutting prices within an hour of each other. Recurring and cross-source.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-21) $2 and $6 per million tokens against roughly 5x for Fable 5.1 and Astra.
  • Ben Thompson (Stratechery, 2026-09-21) the price umbrella argument: prices sit above where supply would put them, and training compute is what is not serving inference. Cross-source (Willison, SemiAnalysis).
  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-21) Sonnet-class $3/$15 as the baseline the decider is priced against, with the challenger's price possibly subsidised. New for this source; cross-source (Willison, Berman, Orosz, Technically, Zvi Mowshowitz, Frontier Labs).
  • Zvi Mowshowitz (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-09-12) the $10/$50 and cache-read comparison with Fable 5.1.
  • Gergely Orosz (Gergely Orosz, 2026-09-10) Anthropic's prices rising against cheaper open and OpenAI models. New for this source;.
  • Zvi Mowshowitz (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-09-05) unchanged headline price, discount delivered through cache reads.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-03) $10/$50 per million tokens.
  • Simon Willison (Simon Willison, 2026-09-03) Astra adopts Anthropic's Fable price point exactly.
  • Google DeepMind (Frontier Labs, 2026-09-02) an introductory price that doubles on a stated date, an unusual explicitness.
  • David Krevitt (Technically, 2026-08-09) the $3/$15 against $10/$50 comparison.
  • Will Raphaelson (Technically, 2026-04-16) the cost argument against calling the labs directly.

Related terms

concept

Frontier models

The strongest available models at any moment, now gated rather than openly sold and bypassed by most real usage; the tier the open models chase and the reference point for every pricing and access argument in the vault.

needed by 7 · 21 linksconcept

Price elasticity

How much demand responds to model price; Ramp's data show buyers do not pay premiums proportional to capability, the evidence behind Tunguz's 'who buys SOTA' argument.

4 linksconcept

Zero Data Retention

The enterprise contract norm that a vendor never retains or trains on customer prompts, now priced as a surcharge, offered by OpenAI and contrasted with Anthropic's inspectable safeguards; the term shared by Tunguz, Orosz and Berman.

needed by 1 · 10 linksconcept

Ultrafast mode

A paid high-speed inference tier (OpenAI's Cerebras-backed Ultrafast at 14 to 15x, Xiaomi's and Z.ai's high-speed tiers on TileRT) sold as a product; the market signal that users pay for latency.

5 linksconcept

Token share versus revenue share

Berman's split between volume dominance (open weights, especially Chinese models, winning tokens) and value capture (closed labs winning revenue), with a predicted market of cheap metered frontier access next to defensible proprietary-data inference.

8 linksconcept

On-device inference

Running models locally on the user's machine or on-premise, now competitive with cloud frontier models for most tasks at the cost of thinking time, and eliminating per-token metering for always-on agents.
