Frontier model pricing

first seen 2026-W16 · needed by 2 · 20 links · 3 min read

Frontier model pricing is the published rate card for the strongest models. The current anchor is ten dollars per million input tokens and fifty per million output, a point Astra adopted to match Fable 5.1 exactly, with discounts delivered through cheaper cache reads rather than a lower headline. Below the flagship, the workhorse tier sits around three and fifteen dollars, and challengers such as Grok 4.7 hold at two and six.

It matters now because the umbrella is being tested. On one day in September, Anthropic cut Opus 5.5 to about 40 percent of Fable 5.1's cost, the first cut in the Opus line's history, and OpenAI halved Sol and Luna within ninety minutes, calling the reduction permanent. One reading is that prices sit above where supply would put them because training compute is not serving inference. For an executive the decision is whether to pay the flagship rate for the thin tail of tasks that need it, and where to route the rest.

Also seen as model pricing, token prices, API pricing

Deeper dive

How it works. A frontier price card has an input rate, an output rate, and a cache-read rate for context that has been seen before, and the effective price a customer pays depends on the mix. The flagship anchor of ten and fifty dollars per million tokens has held across two vendors, with one delivering its discount through cache reads while keeping the headline unchanged. The tier below is where competition happens: the three-and-fifteen workhorse rate is the baseline against which challengers price, sometimes with subsidy, and Grok 4.7 was held at its predecessor's two and six. Introductory pricing that doubles on a stated date, and free output tokens, are new shapes the vault had not previously recorded.

Why it matters now. Two flagship-adjacent cuts landed on a single day: Claude Opus 5.5 at about 40 percent of Fable 5.1's cost, the first reduction in the Opus line's history, and GPT-6 Luna and Sol halved, with the vendor stating the cut is permanent and the announcements ninety minutes apart. Publishing a nine-model price table on release day has become the practitioner's habit. The structural reading is a price umbrella: list prices sit above where supply would put them because the compute that could serve inference is training the next model instead, so prices fall in steps when a lab decides to compete rather than continuously with cost. Meanwhile the number that matters to buyers, Cost per task, keeps falling as models finish work in fewer tokens, so rising list prices and falling bills coexist.

Trade-offs. Seat-based products built on per-token models face margin inversion when real usage exceeds the assumptions, which is the demand-side consequence of agentic workloads meeting flat subscription pricing. Enterprises on compliant tiers pay more for the same tokens. And Price elasticity cuts both ways: a halving can unlock workloads that were uneconomic, or simply transfer margin to customers who would have paid. Cache-read discounts reward customers who structure their prompts well, which is why Cache pricing has become a design concern rather than a footnote.

What to watch. Whether the flagship anchor itself moves, whether the challenger prices survive once subsidies end, and how much of the Capability overhang gets used once the mid-tier price war makes yesterday's frontier cheap.

Ultrafast mode

Ultrafast mode is a paid high-speed inference tier that serves the same model many times faster on specialised hardware, such as OpenAI's Cerebras-backed Ultrafast at roughly 14 to 15 times standard speed.

Token share versus revenue share

Token share versus revenue share is the distinction between which models process the most tokens and which capture the most money, with open-weight models winning volume while a few closed labs collect most of the revenue.

On-device inference

On-device inference means running a language model on the user's own hardware, whether a laptop, a phone, a robot or an on-premise server, instead of calling a cloud API.
