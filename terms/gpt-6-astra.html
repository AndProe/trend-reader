GPT-6 Astra

first seen 2026-W36 · needed by 1 · 13 links · 2 min read

GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI's top model of the GPT-6 generation. It was trained on a run of about 100,000 GPUs, headlines computer use, and can pursue a goal over several days: in one widely discussed demonstration it converted the text game Zork into a 3D game and produced trailers, reaching for Blender without being asked. Demand was strong enough that OpenAI paused Pro sign-ups.

For an executive, Astra is the ceiling in every current comparison chart, and the price movement in the market is happening beneath it. OpenAI markets it as an automated engineer that can be hired for under six dollars an hour, which changes the build-versus-buy arithmetic for software and security work. It also carries new oversight questions: OpenAI's system card calls it the most aligned model yet while conceding that its reasoning is harder to inspect, so deployment plans need explicit monitoring budgets rather than trust by default.

Also seen as Astra, GPT-6

Deeper dive

What it does. Astra is built for ambitious, open-ended work rather than chat: it is strongest at computer use, coding, 3D and game generation, and security tasks. It sustains Long-horizon goals over multi-day runs, drives a desktop or browser directly, and in evaluations serves as the closed-model bar that open-weight systems are measured against. Its Zork-to-3D conversion, including an unprompted detour into Blender, is the canonical demonstration of Prompt-to-game work; its use as the Lean verification step in a claimed Navier-Stokes result shows the same capability pointed at mathematics.

What is new under the hood. Reporting on the architecture points to looped transformers with recurrent depth, where the model iterates over its own hidden state instead of emitting every step as text. That is the mechanism behind both the capability jump and the monitoring problem: more of the reasoning happens in activations that nobody reads. See Recurrent depth and Chain-of-thought monitoring for the trade-off.

Alignment and monitoring. OpenAI describes Astra as its most aligned model and, in the same system card, as harder to monitor than GPT-5.6 Sol. Independent reviewers found the two claims dissonant. The practical consequence for deployers is that behavioural evaluation and runtime controls matter more, not less, as the model becomes capable of plans that span days.

What to watch. Pricing: Astra sits with Claude Fable 5.1 in a $10 and $50 per million token tier that has so far stayed above the price war playing out among cheaper models. Capacity: the Pro sign-up pause signals that inference supply, not demand, is the constraint. Successors: architectural sketches for the next generation from OpenAI and Anthropic are said to overlap, so differentiation between labs may narrow to data, harnesses and price.
