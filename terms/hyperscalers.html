Hyperscalers

first seen 2026-W13 · needed by 1 · 16 links · 3 min read

The hyperscalers are the handful of companies operating cloud infrastructure at global scale. Their AI businesses are growing 37 to 82 percent a year, they lock up the accelerator pipeline years ahead, and they are the gatekeepers of investment-grade capital for data centre projects, sometimes described as gigascalers to mark the shift to gigawatt campuses. They are also the buyers of behind-the-meter power capacity and the owners of the enterprise tiers, such as Bedrock and Vertex, through which regulated companies consume models.

For an executive, the hyperscalers are simultaneously supplier, competitor and financier. They now ration capacity rather than discount it, and they decline reservations they cannot fill, so cloud spend plans need earlier commitments. Their debt issuance makes them the implicit backstop for the AI build-out, which ties their credit to the sector's revenue. And their share of Nvidia's growth is decelerating relative to smaller buyers, a signal that demand is broadening and that the neo cloud alternative is becoming real.

Also seen as cloud providers, clouds, hyperscaler cloud growth rates

Deeper dive

Who they are. The core group is AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, joined by Oracle and, increasingly, the frontier labs that now build their own campuses. What distinguishes them from other providers is scale of capital: they can issue investment-grade debt, sign multi-year accelerator commitments, and absorb the cost of gigawatt sites that take years to energise. Ratings built for specialist GPU clouds have begun grading them alongside the Neo clouds, with Google Cloud promoted and Azure demoted in a recent round, which shows their AI offerings are now judged on the same terms as specialists.

What they control. Three things. Supply: they locked up the accelerator pipeline, which is the origin of the GPU access problem for everyone else, and they have recently moved from discounting to rationing, declining reservations they cannot fill and pushing up CPU pricing as agent workloads spread. Capital: most AI infrastructure debt is issued by them, and they act as the larger implicit backstop for the ecosystem's financing, including Vendor financing arrangements that route back to chip suppliers. Power: they are the buyers of Behind-the-meter power capacity and the parties most affected by Grid interconnection delays, with campuses placed deliberately beyond the reach of county moratoriums.

Trade-offs and tensions. Growth rates of 37 to 82 percent a year are used to argue that a 55 percent compound rate for the sector is plausible, but that growth depends on AI revenue materialising fast enough to service the debt. Their sequential share of Nvidia's growth is decelerating relative to smaller buyers, which either signals healthy broadening of demand or the leading edge of a slowdown. For customers, the enterprise tiers offer residency and fine-tuning but create lock-in to a vendor's cloud, which is the problem bring-your-own-cloud deployment models try to solve.

What to watch. Whether rationing eases as new capacity energises. How the hyperscalers' credit holds up if AI revenue lags the build-out. And whether the labs' own campuses turn them from hyperscaler customers into hyperscaler competitors.

AI infrastructure debt

The borrowing required to build AI data centres, estimated at roughly $4 trillion over five years, and the instruments that carry it: project finance at 65 to 75 percent leverage, off-balance-sheet vehicles, private credit, commercial paper, corporate and municipal bonds.

GPU access

GPU access is the question of who can obtain frontier compute and on what terms, from hyperscalers that lock up the supply pipeline to everyone outside it facing scarcity.

needed by 1 · 8 linksconcept

Edge AI

Edge AI is the running of model inference on local hardware, from a desktop Mac or a single GPU to the processor inside a robot or a pair of headphones, rather than in a data centre.

9 linksconcept

Vendor financing

Vendor financing is Nvidia using its own balance sheet to sustain demand for its chips, by investing in labs and neoclouds, guaranteeing leases and revenue floors, extending payment terms and financing data centres directly.

9 linksconcept

Object storage

Object storage is cloud storage for files and unstructured data, sold as a bottomless, cheap, separately billed service of which AWS S3 is the archetype.

4 linksconcept

Grid interconnection

Grid interconnection is the process, run through a grid operator's queue, by which a data centre or power plant is connected to the utility grid.
