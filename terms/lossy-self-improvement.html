Lossy self-improvement

first seen 2026-W37 · 7 links · 1 min read

Nathan Lambert's counter-thesis to recursive self-improvement: AI does accelerate AI, but the gain leaks away because automatable research is too narrow against the exponential costs in scaling laws, parallel agents show diminishing returns, and resource and political bottlenecks govern what gets built. On this model the frontier stays jagged, superhuman at goal-seeking in maths and software and limited in intuition and creativity, so serving efficiency improves faster than peak intelligence.
