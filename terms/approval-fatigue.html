Approval fatigue

first seen 2026-W42 · 10 links · 2 min read

Agent harnesses contain damage by intercepting any action that leaves the sandbox and asking a human first. The mechanism works, and that is the problem: an agent doing real work triggers many such prompts, and a person who is interrupted constantly stops reading them. Every production harness therefore ships an escape valve — standing allow-rules the user approves once, after which matching actions proceed silently.

That escape valve is the attack surface. If untrusted content can influence what permission the agent asks for, an adversary does not need to defeat the permission system; they need the user to widen it. Measured on shipping platforms, agents can be steered into requesting a plausibly justified but dangerously broad rule in a majority of attempts, and an approved rule then covers a large share of the capability space it nominally scoped. The security property that was promised — a human sees anything consequential — is lost at the moment the convenience feature is used as designed.

Also seen as approval prompts, permission fatigue

Deeper dive

How it works. A harness classifies actions as inside or outside the sandbox and blocks the latter pending approval. Allow-rules are patterns, which means their coverage is semantic rather than literal: a rule approved for one plausible purpose may match a wide set of unrelated actions. Published measurement puts that semantic reach at over half of category-specific capability tests for a single approved rule, and silent completion of an adversary's goal at four in five attempts once the rule exists.

Why it matters now. The same ladder is being adopted independently at very different scales — read before write, draft before send, ask before spend as personal-assistant guidance, and money movement as the boundary a retail bank draws for third-party agents. Those are both responses to Approval fatigue: they reduce the number of decisions a human must make by classifying actions in advance rather than asking per instance. The open question is whether pre-classification is more robust than per-action approval or merely less visible.

Trade-offs. Tightening approval raises the interruption rate until the agent stops being useful, so the usable design space is narrow. Rule expiry, scope narrowing and re-confirmation reduce blast radius at the cost of reintroducing the interruptions the rules removed. Logging and after-the-fact review shift the burden from prevention to detection, which only helps if someone reads the log — and the measured failure mode is specifically silent, giving the approver no further signal.

What to watch. Whether harnesses begin treating permission requests as untrusted output to be validated rather than as the agent's own judgement, whether allow-rules acquire expiry and scope limits by default, and whether regulated deployments converge on the money-movement style boundary as the standard rather than per-action approval.

Related terms

concept

Human oversight

Human oversight is the practice of keeping a person in the loop over an agent's actions, with authority to approve, correct or stop them, and the review burden that this imposes.

needed by 1 · 21 linksconcept

Prompt injection

Prompt injection is the attack in which instructions planted in content a model reads are treated as instructions from its operator.

10 linksconcept

Blast radius

Blast radius is the extent of systems, data and users that a change or an agent action can damage if it goes wrong, and it is the measure that decides how much review or isolation something needs.

7 linksconcept

Agent accountability

Agent accountability is the principle that every AI agent must be traceable to a named human or institution that answers for its actions, with the owner's permissions forming the agent's ceiling.

13 linksproduct

Claude Code

Claude Code is Anthropic's terminal-based coding agent: a harness that wraps a Claude model with file, shell and tool access so it can plan, edit, run and verify changes across a codebase on its own.

14 linksconcept

Agent security boundaries

Agent security boundaries are the permission, identity and network limits that separate one agent's reach from another's and from its operator's, applying zero-trust principles to agents with per-agent credentials, least privilege, isolation and monitoring.
