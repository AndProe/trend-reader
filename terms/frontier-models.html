Frontier models

first seen 2026-W32 · needed by 7 · 21 links · 3 min read

Frontier models are the top tier: the handful of models from a few labs that define what is possible at a given date. The tier resets often, with the frontier moving by three points or more on standard indices several times in a season, so it is a moving target that efficient and open models chase at a lag of months.

It matters now for three reasons. The frontier is gated: the strongest checkpoints are released through trusted-access programmes, withheld from some countries, and sometimes banned outright. It is bypassed: most real usage runs on cheaper tiers, and the frontier share of large-account consumption fell from 53 to 45 percent in a single month, a thin right tail of tasks where capability is decisive. And it is the reference point: pricing umbrellas, distillation disputes, export controls and superintelligence legislation all define themselves against it. For an executive the frontier is what to benchmark against and rarely what to run in production.

Also seen as SOTA models, state of the art models, frontier tier

Deeper dive

How it works. The frontier is defined by capability, not by vendor: the models that top the current evaluations across reasoning, coding, agentic tasks and multimodal work, produced by the labs with the compute to train them. The lead interval is set by training compute and architecture, which is why the term is reserved in some framings for training and architecture rather than production execution. The frontier resets whenever a lab ships a step change, and the vault records it moving by three or more points three times in a short period; Release cadence therefore decides how long any model stays at the frontier, and the Open-closed capability gap of roughly six months decides how long before an open model matches it.

Why it matters now. The tier has become gated rather than openly sold. Trusted-access programmes, nationality screening and government gatekeeping mean the strongest checkpoints reach a vetted minority, which is the mechanism tracked under Access rationing. At the same time the tier is economically thin: most real usage bypasses it, and the frontier share of large-account consumption slid from 53 to 45 percent in a month as buyers routed routine work to the middle. Analysts have at times declared a lab out of the frontier race and been proven wrong by its next release, which is a caution against reading the table as stable. The tier's relevance extends to defence: defensive capability has to keep pace with frontier attack capability, and frontier models now appear as the reasoning layer in robotics and the target class for offensive cyber training.

Trade-offs. Running the frontier buys capability and the highest Frontier model pricing, the greatest policy exposure and, on some tasks, no measurable advantage; benchmark studies find frontier models saturating leading tests while gaining points from taxonomy definitions rather than ability, and hallucinating on broad tool surfaces after being clean on narrow ones. The Capability overhang argument holds that the frontier already exceeds what most organisations can absorb, so the binding constraint is integration, not the model.

What to watch. Whether Lab consolidation shrinks the number of labs at the frontier, whether the gating hardens into law through superintelligence legislation, and whether the frontier's share of consumption keeps falling as the middle tier improves, which is the contrast with Open weights models that decides the market.

Related terms

concept

Access rationing

Access rationing is the practice of labs and governments deciding who may use the strongest AI models, through model withdrawals from distrusted platforms, gated frontier tiers, nationality screening and trusted-partner programmes.

needed by 1 · 15 linksmetric

Frontier model pricing

Frontier model pricing is the per-token list price of the top-tier models, anchored at ten dollars per million input tokens and fifty per million output for both Astra and Fable 5.1.

needed by 2 · 20 linksconcept

Release cadence

Release cadence is the rate at which a laboratory ships new models and point releases, measured in days between updates rather than in annual generations.

9 linksconcept

Open-closed capability gap

The open-closed capability gap is the measured lag between the best openly downloadable model and the best closed frontier model, usually expressed as the number of months it takes open models to reach a given capability level.

15 linksconcept

Lab consolidation

Lab consolidation is the expected shake-out of the frontier model market to a handful of labs and, on the buyer side, one or two vendors per enterprise workload.

8 linksevent

Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act

The Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act is a bill introduced by Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar that would pause advanced AI development until safety standards exist, backed by a corporate death penalty, prison terms of up to 20 years and a new cabinet-level agency.
