AI productivity

first seen 2026-W36 · 8 links · 3 min read

AI productivity is the question behind every 3x claim. Careful practitioners have revised what they think it is. One revision from writing: AI did not cut the effort a piece took, it raised the quality reachable at the same effort. Another from engineering: OpenAI's figure of 3.1 agent-workdays per human shift is better read as machine runtime than as better thinking, and since roughly half of long tasks need human intervention, delivered work about doubles rather than triples.

The practical reading for an executive is that the gain is real but is not a headcount multiplier. Experts get both better and more output when they bring judgement, taste and context the model lacks, and the effect on teams shows up as faster decisions rather than fewer people. Returns can be dramatic in narrow cases, such as a 32x return on infrastructure costing thousands of dollars. The wrong reading, that output per person triples without changing what people do, leads to headcount cuts the actual gain will not support.

Also seen as 3x AI productivity gain

Deeper dive

How it works. The gain decomposes into three parts. Machine runtime: an agent works while the person does something else, so the calendar time per task falls even though the person's attention is still required at intervals. Quality ceiling: with the model doing the drafting and checking, a person of fixed effort reaches an output they could not have produced alone. Throughput on well-specified work: tasks with clear specifications and cheap verification are where agents approach autonomy. The 3.1 agent-workdays figure mostly measures the first, and the observation that about half of long-horizon tasks need intervention means the Human oversight cost caps the multiplier. Work on what the person contributes identifies advantages such as judgement about what is worth doing, domain context, taste and accountability, which is why experts gain more than novices.

Why it matters now. Companies are setting budgets on productivity claims. The Cost per task of agent work is falling while the supervision cost is not, so the economics favour tasks with cheap verification and punish tasks where checking is as hard as doing. Where the gain is not extracted at all, which is common, the cause is organisational; see Capability overhang. Measurement is improving through task-based economic benchmarks such as GDPval and through studies of team-level outcomes such as decision speed.

Trade-offs. Optimising for runtime encourages shipping unreviewed output and accumulating Cognitive debt. Optimising for quality at constant effort produces no headcount saving, which disappoints finance. Fully agentic pipelines change the work rather than speeding it, which is the Agentic engineering bet. Any single multiplier hides which of these a company is getting.

What to watch. Whether intervention rates on long tasks fall, which would raise the effective multiplier, and whether companies report productivity as quality and speed rather than as headcount avoided.

Human oversight

Human oversight is the practice of keeping a person in the loop over an agent's actions, with authority to approve, correct or stop them, and the review burden that this imposes.

Persistent agents

Persistent agents are AI agents designed to run continuously and carry memory, identity and relationships across sessions, devices and tasks, instead of starting fresh on each request.

Cognitive debt

Cognitive debt is the loss of memory, understanding and judgement that builds up when people accept AI output as their own work without engaging with it, and the organisational equivalent when experienced staff are removed faster than their knowledge can be replaced.

6 linksmetric

Cost per task

Cost per task is the dollar cost of getting a unit of work actually completed by an AI system, measured at a given quality level, rather than the price per token or the score on a benchmark.

23 linksconcept

Agentic engineering

Agentic engineering is the discipline of building software with coding agents deliberately: engineers specify, constrain, verify and review while agents write most or all of the code, in contrast to unstructured vibe coding.
