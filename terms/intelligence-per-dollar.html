Intelligence per dollar divides a capability score by what it costs to obtain. It became the operative comparison once frontier models converged: when five models sit within five points on an index and differ by 3.8x in cost per task, the capability number stops discriminating and the ratio starts.

The analysis the vault records traces that spread largely to serving hardware, with the vendor furthest along on its own silicon cheapest — 74 percent below the most expensive comparable model. Two cautions attach. The measure depends entirely on which capability index is used and at what reasoning effort, so a single ratio is only meaningful with both stated. And subscription products break the calculation altogether: research in the same period found posted monthly prices carry almost no information because hidden usage meters, model mix and reset windows determine deliverable work, with one vendor's mid-tier plans worth roughly five times another's.

Also seen as intelligence per dollar, capability per dollar

Deeper dive

How it works. The numerator is a capability index; the denominator is a weighted cost per unit of work, aggregating input, cached, reasoning and output token prices across an evaluation suite at a stated effort level. Both halves are fragile: indices weight their component benchmarks by judgment, and Cost per task shifts with context length, cache-hit rate and the model's own default effort — one frontier model was observed self-selecting roughly thirty minutes of work on open-ended tasks unless instructed otherwise.

Why it matters now. The vault records a 41 percent fall in prices against 50 percent growth in token volume in a single quarter, frontier models' share of tokens dropping from 53 percent into the mid-40s as buyers defaulted to cheaper tiers, and decision models priced two orders of magnitude below general ones for routing and classification. In that market the procurement question is no longer which model is best but which ratio clears the threshold for a given workload, which is the practical content of Frontier model pricing becoming a commoditisation story.

Trade-offs. The ratio hides distribution: a model that is cheap on average can be expensive on the tasks that matter, and defect cost is absent from the numerator entirely — which is why one practitioner the vault records uses the most expensive model specifically where a defect is costly and small models everywhere latency dominates. It also ignores switching and integration cost, which accumulated context makes substantial.

What to watch. Whether any published ratio states its index, effort level and context size, and whether subscription-equivalent value becomes routinely reported.

Related terms

Cost per task

Cost per task is the dollar cost of getting a unit of work actually completed by an AI system, measured at a given quality level, rather than the price per token or the score on a benchmark.

Frontier model pricing

Frontier model pricing is the per-token list price of the top-tier models, anchored at ten dollars per million input tokens and fifty per million output for both Astra and Fable 5.1.

Artificial Analysis index

The Artificial Analysis index is a third-party composite ranking of AI models, aggregating scores across standard evaluations into a single intelligence figure, with companion indexes for cost and for speech quality.

10 linksmetric

Token efficiency

Token efficiency is the amount of useful capability a model delivers per token consumed or produced, whether measured as output tokens per task, accuracy per token, or the input-to-output ratio of a workload.

13 linksmetric

Gross margin

Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of serving, expressed as a share of revenue; in AI it is the number that says whether selling tokens is a business.

7 linksmetric

Performance per watt

Performance per watt measures how much useful AI output, typically tokens or benchmark-weighted intelligence, a system produces per unit of electrical power.
