Retainer equity grants

first seen 2026-W33 · 3 links · 3 min read

Most technology companies grant equity at hiring and then top it up each year with a refresher. Retainer grants are a departure from that routine: a one-off award made to someone who has handed in their notice, in the hope of changing their mind. During its 2026 engineering exodus Meta began making such grants, a practice new for the company, while the size of its annual refreshers became the single factor determining whether people stayed.

For an executive the practice is a warning sign rather than a tool. It rewards resigning, which teaches the rest of the organisation to resign, and it prices retention at the margin set by the most aggressive competitor. It is the compensation-side symptom of a mercenary culture in which loyalty is bought per cycle. The budget line it touches is stock-based compensation, and the risk it touches is that the people who accept a retainer are rarely the ones who would have stayed anyway.

Also seen as retention grants, equity refreshers

Deeper dive

How it works. Equity compensation at large technology firms has two moving parts: the initial grant vesting over several years, and the annual refresher that keeps total unvested value high enough to make leaving expensive. When refreshers shrink, as they did at Meta, the unvested balance falls and the cost of leaving drops with it. A retainer grant is the emergency response: a discretionary award to a specific resigning employee, sized to beat the offer they have accepted elsewhere. It is negotiated individually, arrives after the resignation, and is by construction visible to colleagues who did not resign.

Why it matters now. The practice surfaced during the Meta engineering exodus of August 2026, a resignation wave the company is described as having inflicted on itself, and the evidence for the term comes from that episode. Two facts were reported: one-off retention grants to resigning staff were new at Meta, and annual grants had become the decisive factor in whether engineers stayed. The backdrop is AI lab talent competition, in which AI laboratories offer packages that established firms cannot match through routine refreshers, so retention is fought one resignation at a time.

Trade-offs. A retainer grant can hold a key person through a critical project, and for a small number of irreplaceable engineers the arithmetic works. Applied broadly it inverts incentives: the reliable route to a raise becomes an outside offer and a resignation letter, which is the definition of a Mercenary culture. It also erodes internal equity, since two engineers doing the same work end up on different terms depending on who threatened to leave. The refresher lever is cleaner but slower, and cutting it is what created the problem in the first place.

What to watch. Whether other firms facing lab poaching adopt the same practice, whether Meta's refresher sizes recover, and whether the retainers retained anyone for longer than a vesting cliff.
