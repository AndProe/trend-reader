Grok 4.6

first seen 2026-W33 · 6 links · 2 min read

Grok 4.6 was xAI's mid-August release, an incremental step over Grok 4.5 rather than a new generation. It placed third on the Artificial Analysis index while priced well below the models above it, a result credited in part to coding data acquired with Cursor and to training on regenerated trajectories from its predecessor. It also became the model underneath Grok Bot, the Cursor and xAI agent product.

For an executive, Grok 4.6 is the moment xAI moved from a curiosity to a procurement option. Its price and speed became the baseline that Grok 4.7 held a month later, and its GDPval and legal benchmark scores are the comparison points for that successor. The commercial question it raises is whether a discount of this size is a durable strategy or a subsidy from spare compute, which affects how much to rely on it in cost models.

Also seen as Grok 4.6 (xAI)

Deeper dive

What it is. Grok 4.6 is an iterative release in xAI's Grok 4 line, shipped in mid-August 2026. On the Artificial Analysis index it reached third place, behind the top models from OpenAI and Anthropic, at a per-token price well below theirs. Reviewers treated it as the first evidence that xAI was a genuine third frontier lab rather than a fast follower.

How it was built. Two ingredients are credited. The first is coding data that came with the Cursor-xAI acquisition, which gave xAI a large stream of real developer interactions to train and evaluate on. The second is training partly on Grok 4.5's regenerated trajectories: the earlier model's outputs on tasks were regenerated, filtered and fed back as training data. That is a limited, practical form of the loop discussed under Recursive self-improvement, where a model's own outputs improve its successor, and it explains how a point release could move the index without a new pretraining run. The model also underpins Grok Bot, the chat-native agent product xAI and Cursor launched in the same period.

Why the price. xAI priced the model aggressively, and the discount is part of what made the release notable. One reading is that xAI has more compute than it can fill with frontier demand and is using price to buy share. Another is that the coding-data advantage lowers the cost of reaching a given capability. Either way, the price and speed of 4.6 became the anchor that Grok 4.7 held unchanged a month later, and its GDPval and legal-benchmark results are the baselines that successor is measured against.

What to watch. Whether the regenerated-trajectory approach keeps paying off or runs into diminishing returns. Whether xAI can hold the discount as demand grows. And whether the third-place position is stable or a snapshot between rival releases.
