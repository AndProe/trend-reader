Chinese AI chips

first seen 2026-W31 · 8 links · 3 min read

For several years the working assumption was that Chinese AI would be bottlenecked by access to Nvidia GPUs. That assumption is now in question. Huawei's Ascend accelerators and their successors are being used to train large models end to end, and Chinese serving stacks built around them handle very large volumes, with GLM 5.3 Flash reported serving 100 trillion tokens a day on domestic silicon. The next generation, the Ascend 960DT, has been pulled forward, and cluster claims run to a quarter of a million cards.

For executives this changes two calculations. On supply, a second full-stack accelerator ecosystem exists, and countries such as Malaysia are weighing Huawei chips for national projects, which means the market for AI infrastructure is splitting along geopolitical lines. On competition, Chinese open-weight models trained and served on domestic chips are cheap and capable, so the cost floor for AI services is being set partly outside the Nvidia ecosystem, with consequences for pricing everywhere.

Also seen as Chinese accelerators, Ascend, domestic Chinese silicon

Deeper dive

How it works. A domestic accelerator on its own is not enough; the chip, the interconnect that links thousands of them, and the serving software that schedules work across them must be designed together. That co-design is what has matured. Evidence includes LongCat-2.0 trained end to end on Ascend 910s, Alibaba's second-generation supernode as a training base, and GLM 5.3 Flash served at more than 100 trillion tokens a day at efficiency reported as comparable to Nvidia-class systems. The approach is a form of Custom silicon pursued at national rather than company scale, and it deliberately bypasses the CUDA moat by building its own software layer rather than trying to emulate Nvidia's.

Why it matters now. Export controls were designed to deny China the compute needed for frontier training. The counter-example now cited is a frontier-adjacent open model, GLM 5.3 Flash, trained and served on Chinese chips, which suggests controls slowed but did not stop the effort and may have accelerated domestic investment. The Ascend 960DT has been brought forward and a 256,000-card cluster has been claimed. Combined with the strategy of releasing Open weights models to build a global developer base, this creates an alternative stack that governments seeking Sovereign AI models can adopt without US dependence.

Trade-offs. Domestic chips still lag on per-chip performance and on manufacturing process, so matching Nvidia-class throughput takes more chips, more power and more engineering. Software maturity is narrower, and the ecosystem depends heavily on a few companies. For buyers outside China, adopting the stack means taking on political and sanctions risk in exchange for price and availability.

What to watch. Independent verification of the token-per-day and cluster-size claims, the Open-closed capability gap between models trained on domestic silicon and Western frontier models, whether more third countries follow Malaysia's lead, and whether export controls are tightened, loosened or bypassed further in response.

Related terms

concept

Custom silicon

Custom silicon is the accelerator hardware designed by the companies that use it, such as Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium, Meta's MTIA and OpenAI's Jalapeño, rather than bought from Nvidia.

needed by 2 · 9 linksconcept

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.

needed by 11 · 36 linksmodel

GLM 5.3 Flash

GLM 5.3 Flash is Z.ai's efficient open-weights model, with 320 billion total parameters and 18 billion active, which appeared anonymously as Ox-Alpha before its reveal.

4 linksconcept

Sovereign AI models

Sovereign AI models are frontier-scale models trained by or for a state, in its own language and under its own control, as a hedge against depending on American frontier APIs.

10 linksconcept

Open-closed capability gap

The open-closed capability gap is the measured lag between the best openly downloadable model and the best closed frontier model, usually expressed as the number of months it takes open models to reach a given capability level.

15 linksconcept

CUDA moat

The CUDA moat is Nvidia's software advantage: a mature programming stack and same-day framework support that make its GPUs the default target for every serving engine.
