Test-time training

first seen 2026-W34 · needed by 1 · 8 links · 3 min read

Most deployed models are frozen: their weights are set at the end of training and every query is answered from the same parameters. Test-time training breaks that rule by letting the model perform a small learning step on the prompt or task in front of it, then answer with the updated weights. The information that would otherwise sit in an ever-growing context is folded into parameters of fixed size.

For an executive, the relevance is economic and architectural. Long-context inference spends memory on a cache that grows with every token; a weight update of fixed size is a different cost curve. In robotics, a per-robot updated model is proposed as the way to keep a capable model on-device, with the per-robot state measured in the low hundreds of megabytes. The trade is that each unit's model diverges, which complicates versioning, evaluation and support, and the compute for on-the-fly updates is not free.

Also seen as TTT, learning at inference

Deeper dive

How it works. During inference the model runs a brief optimisation on a self-supervised objective derived from the current input, updating some or all of its weights, and then produces its output. The update is typically constrained: a task embedding, a subset of layers or a fast-weight memory. One recent variant on ARC-like tasks reorders the process, fitting a task embedding first and only then adjusting the backbone, which is a change of order rather than of objective. The practical contrast is with the KV cache: a cache stores every token's keys and values and grows linearly, while a weight update compresses the same information into a constant footprint.

Why it matters now. Two pressures push it forward. Long-context workloads are memory-bound and the cache dominates serving cost; a fixed-size alternative is attractive to anyone paying for Inference-time compute. And on-device deployment, particularly in humanoid robotics, cannot carry a datacenter-sized model, so a smaller model that adapts per unit is proposed as the route to autonomy, with per-robot state on the order of 151 megabytes cited as the serving cost. The general question of models that learn after deployment also connects to Continual learning and to the applied form, Test-time adaptation.

Trade-offs. Updating weights at inference costs compute per query and makes each deployed instance unique, which breaks the assumption that a model version is a fixed artefact for evaluation, safety testing and rollback. Self-supervised objectives can be led astray by adversarial or unusual inputs, and a model that learns from its own outputs can drift. Benefits are clearest on tasks with structure to exploit and weakest on short, one-off prompts.

What to watch. Whether frontier vendors ship any form of weight updates in production APIs, how Long context serving costs compare against fixed-size adaptation as context windows grow, and whether robotics and other edge deployments prove the economics before datacenter inference does.

Related terms

technique

Test-time adaptation

Test-time adaptation is the practice of adjusting an agent to a particular person, task or situation while it is being used, either through the context it holds or through small updates to its weights.

7 linksconcept

Inference-time compute

Inference-time compute is computation spent when a model answers, through longer reasoning, multiple candidate answers, verification or tool calls, rather than during training.

needed by 4 · 15 linksconcept

Long context

Long context refers to model context windows of hundreds of thousands to a million tokens, which allow an agent or a user to place entire codebases, document collections or multi-hour recordings in front of the model at once.

needed by 4 · 12 linksconcept

Humanoid robot economics

Humanoid robot economics is the per-unit cost structure of general-purpose robots, dominated by the onboard compute, how much of the time it is used, and whether the planning model runs on the robot or in a data centre.

6 linkstechnique

Continual learning

Continual learning is the ability of a model to keep learning from its deployment, updating on new tasks, users and information while retaining what it already knows, instead of being frozen at the end of training.

9 linkstechnique

KV cache

The KV cache is the per-token store of key and value vectors that a transformer keeps during generation so it does not recompute attention over the whole context on every step.
