Manager-to-IC moves

first seen 2026-W33 · 4 links · 3 min read

A manager-to-IC move is a director, engineering manager or head of engineering choosing to return to writing code and shipping work directly. Two motives dominate in current accounts: stability, since management layers are the first cut when companies flatten, and skills, since leaders who spent years in meetings want to learn how AI-assisted engineering actually works before they are asked to direct it.

For an executive the trend cuts both ways. It offers a way to keep experienced people who would otherwise leave, and it puts judgement close to the work at a time when agents produce code faster than reviewers can read it. But it also removes management capacity just as teams are being reorganised, and it signals anxiety: a wave of leaders asking to step back is a symptom of an organisation whose management roles feel unsafe. Compensation and status frameworks need to accommodate the move without penalising it.

Also seen as leaders returning to IC, manager to individual contributor

Deeper dive

What is happening. Engineering leaders are asking to return to individual-contributor roles, and the request is now treated as respectable rather than as a career failure. The reported motives are practical. Leadership positions have become precarious as companies remove layers, so an IC role looks safer. And the tools of the trade have changed: leaders who have not built with agents feel unable to judge their teams' work, and stepping back is the fastest way to learn. Some take an explicit career break first; others negotiate a direct move within their company.

Why it matters now. Org flattening is the structural driver. When a company decides it can run with fewer managers because AI raises what each engineer produces, the displaced managers either leave or become ICs. The parallel trend of Hands-on executives, senior leaders who use coding agents personally, normalises the idea that seniority no longer requires distance from the code. Leaders returning to the keyboard also address a review bottleneck: the volume of agent-written code needs experienced judgement, and a former manager reviewing agent output is arguably better placed than a junior engineer.

Trade-offs. The move preserves individuals but can hollow out Engineering culture if the people who mentored and shielded teams stop doing so. Compensation systems built on management ladders may cut pay for the move, which pushes people to leave instead. A returning IC can also crowd out junior staff who needed the same roles. And the underlying anxiety is not resolved by the move: an IC role built on agent-assisted output is itself exposed if the organisation later decides fewer ICs are needed.

What to watch. Whether companies publish dual-track ladders that price IC and management seniority equally, whether the leaders who moved back later return to management with better tools, and whether the trend spreads beyond engineering into product and design.
