GPU access

first seen 2026-W36 · needed by 1 · 8 links · 1 min read

Who can get frontier compute and on what terms: hyperscalers locking up the pipeline, scarcity for everyone outside it, surplus in one company meeting shortage in another. Berman's essay subject and the demand side of Nvidia's allocation decisions.

Related terms

concept

Neo clouds

GPU-rental clouds outside the hyperscalers (CoreWeave-type providers) that now drive most net-new data-centre revenue, serve open models at competitive prices, and, per SemiAnalysis's audits, mostly run insecure software stacks.

8 linksconcept

Hyperscalers

The largest cloud providers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, plus Oracle and increasingly the labs themselves) that buy most accelerators, issue most AI debt and grow 37 to 82 percent a year; their decelerating share of Nvidia's growth is a tracked signal.

needed by 1 · 16 linksconcept

Compute as currency

The view that once models commoditise, access to compute and energy rather than model access decides advantage, and that inference can be traded for training tokens rather than sold as a utility.

10 linksconcept

Vendor financing

Nvidia extending its balance sheet to sustain demand for its own chips: investing in labs and neoclouds, financing data centres directly, financing SpaceX's buildout.

9 linksconcept

Bullwhip effect

Supply-chain amplification in which demand swings grow larger upstream; Tunguz applies it to AI hardware as sequential multi-year shortages cascading from GPUs to memory to CPUs, storage and physical shells.

needed by 1 · 6 linksmetric

CPU pricing

The price and availability of server CPUs as agentic workloads push the CPU-to-GPU ratio up; Tomasz Tunguz cites Intel's server CPU average selling prices rising 27 percent year over year against falling volumes as one stage of his AI bullwhip.
