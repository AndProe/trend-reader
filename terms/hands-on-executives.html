Hands-on executives

first seen 2026-W34 · 6 links · 3 min read

The term describes senior people who returned to building as models improved: directors and VPs prototyping on weekends, founders shipping features and overriding their engineering leaders on the strength of what they built. Its popular name is founder mode. Those who study engineering organisations treat the shift as permanent rather than a phase, and new measures of AI leadership look at what leaders themselves do with the tools, judging, explaining, learning and coordinating, rather than what they sponsor.

For an executive this changes two calculations. The first is personal: fluency with AI tooling is becoming a condition of credibility at the top, not an optional skill. The second is structural: when leaders can build, the layers of management that once translated intent into work lose their purpose, which is one force behind flatter organisations and behind senior people choosing to step back to individual work. The risk is that hands-on leaders override teams on the basis of prototypes that do not survive production.

Also seen as founder mode, builder executives

Deeper dive

What is happening. Senior leaders are building again. As coding agents and general assistants became capable enough to produce working software from intent, founders and executives who had stopped writing code found they could ship prototypes, analyses and internal tools without a team. Reports from inside engineering organisations describe directors and VPs building on weekends, and founders overriding their leaders with artefacts rather than opinions. The pattern is part of a wider shift in Engineering culture toward judgement over delegation.

Why now. The cost of a first version has collapsed, so the leverage of being able to build is back in the hands of whoever has the clearest intent, which is often the person at the top. Research into AI leadership now measures what leaders do with AI directly: judge outputs, explain them, learn from them and coordinate work through them. That is the leadership equivalent of AI on the resume: evidence of use rather than sponsorship. The shift is widely treated as permanent because it follows from tool capability, not from fashion.

Trade-offs. Hands-on leadership shortens the distance between decision and evidence and removes translation layers, which is the mechanism behind Org flattening and part of why some senior engineers are making Manager-to-IC moves. The costs are real. A founder's prototype is not a product, and overriding a team on the strength of one can discard operational knowledge the prototype does not encode. Leaders who build also compete with their own teams for the interesting work, and a flood of executive-generated prototypes is its own form of AI slop if nobody is accountable for maintaining them.

What to watch. Whether AI leadership measures become part of executive assessment. How organisations set rules for when a leader's build becomes a team's responsibility. And whether the founder-mode pattern holds in large regulated firms or remains a startup phenomenon.
