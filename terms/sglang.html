SGLang

first seen 2026-W39 · 9 links · 2 min read

Serving a model is a software problem as much as a hardware one: batching, cache placement, scheduling and kernel choice determine how many tokens a given accelerator actually produces. SGLang is one of the open engines that does that work, and because it is open it has become a common denominator in published comparisons — configurations are reported as accelerator plus engine, not accelerator alone.

For a buyer, the practical consequence is that a dollars-per-million-tokens figure is meaningless without the engine named beside the chip. The same silicon moves substantially on throughput and cost depending on which runtime serves it, and the ordering between vendors can invert with the engine as well as with the latency target.

Deeper dive

How it works. SGLang schedules requests across a batch while managing the KV cache that dominates memory during decode, and it is the layer where techniques like cache overlap and sparse-attention kernels are implemented in practice. Its authors are credited with production designs that later appear in vendor benchmarks, which is the route by which an open project sets an industry baseline.

Why it matters now. Published serving economics are reported as pairs — accelerator and engine — and the engine term is load-bearing: two configurations on the same chip can differ by a wide margin in tokens per second per GPU and in dollars per million tokens. That makes the engine a first-class variable in Agentic inference workloads, where long contexts and heavy cache reuse magnify scheduling differences.

Trade-offs. An open engine is comparable but not always fastest; vendor-tuned stacks can lead on specific hardware, and closed measurement suites such as InferenceX report their own configurations. Choosing an open engine buys portability and auditability at the possible cost of peak throughput on any one accelerator.

What to watch. Whether serving-engine work becomes an explicit benchmark target rather than a background variable — recent agentic benchmarks already derive tasks from this project's own production changes. And whether the reported gap between open and vendor stacks narrows as sparse-attention and disaggregated designs mature.

Related terms

technique

KV cache

The KV cache is the per-token store of key and value vectors that a transformer keeps during generation so it does not recompute attention over the whole context on every step.

needed by 3 · 14 linkstechnique

Prefill-decode disaggregation

Prefill-decode disaggregation is a serving architecture that processes a request's prompt on one pool of hardware and generates its output tokens on another, transferring the KV cache between them.

Sparse attention

Sparse attention is a family of attention mechanisms in which each token attends to a selected subset of positions rather than to every previous token, so that the cost of long contexts grows less than quadratically.

7 linksconcept

Agentic inference workloads

Agentic inference workloads are the production traffic pattern created by coding and computer-use agents: many turns per task, context that accumulates toward a million tokens, very high reuse of shared prefixes, and bursts of parallel sub-agent calls.

needed by 1 · 17 linksconcept

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.

InferenceX

InferenceX is an independent inference benchmark suite that measures how well accelerators serve large models, with an AgentX extension for agentic workloads, run across Nvidia, AMD, Google TPU and custom chips.
