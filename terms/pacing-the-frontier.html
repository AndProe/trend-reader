Pacing the frontier

first seen 2026-W35 · needed by 1 · 14 links · 4 min read

Slowing frontier AI development in a coordinated, verifiable way, which Anthropic splits into pacing within a company (its own safety-over-speed choices) and pacing across the field, for which it calls for a lawful, verifiable coordination mechanism. Zvi records Roon's "pacing the frontier success story" and Pachocki's call for coordinated slowdown backed by mandated safety bars and third-party auditors as the frontier labs' versions of the idea.

Also seen as coordinated pacing, coordinated slowdown, pacing letter

Lab oversight capacity

Lambert's observation that labs are underwater on monitoring their own models and agents, which the OpenAI incident exposed and which any transparency demand presupposes.

8 linksconcept

Recursive self-improvement

AI improving AI: models regenerating their successors' training data, and, in Sottiaux's nuance, frontier models optimising the serving infrastructure and kernels they run on.

14 linkstechnique

Chain-of-thought monitoring

Reading a model's reasoning trace as an oversight mechanism, with the principle (Cotra's) that monitors stay separate from reward so the trace is not trained into a performance.

needed by 1 · 10 linksconcept

Preference cascade

A wave of public admissions by frontier-lab staff that AI might soon kill everyone and that no lab has a plan to align superintelligence, set off by Dean Ball's admission that he had held back and turned into an avalanche by Jacob Coxon's resignation from Anthropic.

8 linksmetric

P(doom)

A person's stated probability that AI kills everyone or causes an equivalent catastrophe, usually given with a time window.

8 linksconcept

Regulatory capture

The argument that safety rules proposed by leading labs would entrench those labs by raising compliance costs past what smaller competitors and open-model developers can bear.
