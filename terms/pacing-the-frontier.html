Pacing the frontier

first seen 2026-W35 · needed by 1 · 16 links · 3 min read

Pacing the frontier means deliberately slowing how fast the most capable models reach the public, in a way other labs and governments can verify. The idea has two levels. Within a company it is a choice to prioritise safety over speed on its own releases. Across the field it needs a lawful mechanism so that competitors can slow together without one of them defecting, hence calls for mandated safety bars, third-party auditors and possibly an antitrust exemption. It is explicitly not a pause.

For an executive this matters on three fronts. Regulation is the obvious one, as the proposal is now debated by governments and attacked by politicians who argue labs should not act unilaterally. Competition is the second, since coordinated slowdown among a few labs raises antitrust questions and, critics say, may favour incumbents. Capability is the third: pacing is expected to shift lab effort toward efficiency, speed and price rather than raw capability, which changes what buyers should expect from the next release.

Also seen as coordinated pacing, coordinated slowdown, pacing letter

Deeper dive

How it works. The core idea is a shared release rhythm for frontier models. A lab commits to hold back its unreleased frontier for a defined interval, keeps evaluating internally, and publishes measurements that let outsiders see how far ahead the internal frontier is, which is the visibility side of the proposal. Across labs, commitments become enforceable through mandated safety bars, audits by independent parties or agencies, and possibly an Antitrust exemption so that coordination is lawful. Proponents stress that the pace applies to the unreleased frontier, not to widening access to models already shipped. The measured lag between open and closed models, a few months, has been suggested as the natural unit for how far ahead a lab should be allowed to run.

Why it matters now. Within a few weeks the idea went from one lab's essay setting out a three-layer proposal to endorsements by several lab leaders, a co-signed cross-lab document, a stated willingness to hold risk talks with China, and a research agenda with archetypes ranging from moratorium to preparedness. The trigger was a Preference cascade in which staff across leading labs voiced high extinction estimates and a senior resignation turned private concern public. Government officials replied that labs should stop acting unilaterally; proponents answered with the collective action argument that no single lab can slow alone. Chinese state media read the call as protectionism, and open-weights advocates argued that pacing helps open models by narrowing the Open-closed capability gap.

Trade-offs. Pacing has no defined speed, which makes it hard to verify and easy to game, and measurement of internal progress depends on the labs' own reporting, which strains Lab oversight capacity. Some researchers object that the proposal assumes one training recipe, heavy reinforcement learning, and does not fit other model shapes. Critics warn of Regulatory capture, since incumbents write the safety bars, and note that slowing releases may create a Capability overhang where the unreleased frontier runs well ahead of what the public sees. Supporters reply that a coordinated slowdown leaves current capability intact and buys time for alignment work.

What to watch. Whether a legal coordination mechanism is actually created, whether release cadence visibly changes, and whether the measurements labs publish are independently audited or remain self-reported.

Lab oversight capacity

Lab oversight capacity is the ability of a frontier laboratory to monitor what its own models and agents are doing in deployment and in internal use.

Recursive self-improvement

Recursive self-improvement is the use of AI systems to accelerate the research, data, code and infrastructure that produce the next AI system, so that each generation shortens the time to the one after it.

Chain-of-thought monitoring

Chain-of-thought monitoring is the practice of reading a model's written reasoning trace, with automated or human oversight, to detect misbehaviour before it turns into action.

needed by 1 · 10 linksconcept

Preference cascade

A preference cascade is the moment when privately held views become public all at once after a few people speak first; in AI it names the September 2026 wave of lab staff admitting that advanced AI might kill everyone and that no lab has a plan to align superintelligence.

8 linksmetric

P(doom)

P(doom) is a person's stated probability that advanced AI causes human extinction or an equivalent irreversible catastrophe, usually given with a time window such as within a decade.

8 linksconcept

Regulatory capture

Regulatory capture, in the AI context, is the charge that safety rules proposed by leading laboratories would entrench those laboratories by raising compliance costs beyond what smaller competitors and open-model developers can bear.
