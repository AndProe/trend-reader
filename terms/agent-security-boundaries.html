Agent security boundaries

first seen 2026-W32 · needed by 1 · 15 links · 2 min read

The permission, identity and network limits that separate one agent's reach from another's and from its operator's: zero trust extended from employees to agents, per-agent credentials rather than shared logins. Grok Bot's shared computer is the counter-example the vault uses.

Also seen as zero trust for agents, agent permission model, agent control plane

How the sources use it

  • Gergely Orosz (Gergely Orosz, 2026-09-22) containment policies describing network, filesystem, UI and execution limits, plus ODR's reported position as a proxy that can inspect MCP payloads. Recurring and cross-source (METR, SemiAnalysis, Berman, Latent Space).
  • Forward Future team (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-22) a retailer blocking an outside agent under its terms of use.
  • Ivan Aleksandrov, German Kochnev, Sabrina Sadiekh, Yaroslav Rogoza (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-21) Cross-source (ChinaTalk, Latent Space, Matthew Berman, METR, Technically); the paper's claim is that the boundary is a property of the interaction, not of the model.
  • Fayeq Jeelani Syed, Rehan Ahmad, Ali Al Bataineh, Aakriti Adhikari (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-19) Cross-source (ChinaTalk, Gergely Orosz, Latent Space, Matthew Berman, METR, Technically, Tomasz Tunguz); the authors locate the boundary at the point where untrusted content shares a context with system instructions.
  • Diba Afroze, Xingli Zhang, Yazhou Tu et al. (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-17) cross-source (Tomasz Tunguz, Latent Space); the recommended enforcement layer checks tool permissions and vehicle state outside the model.
  • Laxmipriya Ganesh Iyer (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-16) cross-source (Tomasz Tunguz, Latent Space); the author's point is that gates and permissions cannot reject a call to a tool that does not exist.
  • Yunhao Feng, Ruixiao Lin, Ming Wen et al. (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-14) cross-source; the risks are grounded in agents' live access to terminals, browsers and file systems.
  • Seyedakbar Mostafavi (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-12) cross-source; the dissolving of classical perimeters when agents get execution authority.
  • David Krevitt (Technically, 2026-09-08) the frontend as untrusted, scoped keys, and authorization decisions kept away from the agent.
  • Nick Wentz (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-08) the author's requirement that agents be permissioned under defined controls. Cross-source, first use in Berman notes.
  • Dan McAteer (Latent Space, 2026-09-05) shared computer and logins across bots.
  • Irene Zhang (ChinaTalk, 2026-09-03) Huang's circuit breakers and insider-threat framing, Zhou's least privilege, network isolation and end-to-end monitoring. Cross-source (Technically, Tomasz Tunguz, Latent Space).
  • METR (METR, 2026-08-31) the stated organising principles, centralised identity, network isolation, minimal permission scoping and information barriers, and the personal instance that sat outside them. Cross-source (Berman, SemiAnalysis, Latent Space).
  • David Krevitt (Technically, 2026-08-25) the coding agent must not touch the production database; deployment does.
  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-08-07) Andy Triedman's corollary that zero-trust security must extend from employees to AI agents.

AI agents

Software systems in which a language model plans, calls tools and acts over many steps toward a goal, rather than answering a single prompt.

needed by 20 · 33 linksconcept

Agent accountability

The principle that every agent must be traceable to a named human or institution answerable for its actions, whose permissions become the agent's ceiling.

12 linksconcept

Shadow AI

Unsanctioned employee use of personal AI tools at work (bring your own AI); Berman's essay reframes it as an enablement failure to be solved with accountable agents rather than bans.

5 linkstechnique

Sandbox escape

An agent getting past its isolation: GET-based writes through an allow-listed proxy and the /etc/hosts bypass Willison documented, or the 13-year-old Chrome bug found by automation.

needed by 1 · 8 linksconcept

Enterprise data governance

The compliance regime (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, EU processing rules, financial and patient records) that decides what may leave the company's machines and on what terms, forcing enterprises onto the expensive tier and framing the ban-or-trust choice.

needed by 2 · 11 linksconcept

Vibe coding

Building software by prompting a coding agent and accepting what it produces without understanding the stack it chose; Technically's six-part series treats it as a practice whose failure modes (unexplained vendor choices, databases that fall over at scale, missing authorization) appear only when real users arrive, and argues the remedy is enough software-engineering vocabulary to ask the agent the right questions.
