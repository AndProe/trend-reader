Data flywheel

first seen 2026-W36 · 7 links · 3 min read

A data flywheel is a self-reinforcing loop: people use a model, their prompts, corrections and task outcomes become training signal, the model improves, and the improvement draws more use. The concept is distinct from a taste flywheel, which runs on human judgement about what good output looks like rather than on raw volume.

It matters now for two reasons. First, advertising-supported models obtain the loop cheaply by treating prompts as data rather than as a service to be paid for, which is how a company such as Meta can fund a frontier model without charging for tokens. Second, the loop is closing on itself: recent agent research has agents constructing the tasks that train their successors, so the flywheel no longer requires a human at every turn. For an executive the questions are who owns the data generated by staff using a vendor's model, whether that data is feeding the vendor's flywheel or the company's own, and what a zero-retention contract costs in lost improvement.

Also seen as usage flywheel

Deeper dive

How it works. Every interaction with a deployed model can be logged as an input, an output, and some signal about whether the output was good: an accepted suggestion, an edited draft, a completed agent task, a thumbs-down. Aggregated, those records become material for Post-training, where they are filtered, sometimes relabelled, and used to fine-tune or to construct reinforcement environments. The improved model then draws more usage and more records. The loop is the flywheel; its speed depends on volume, on the quality of the signal, and on how fast the lab can turn records into a new checkpoint.

Why it matters now. Two developments have changed the economics. The advertising model for prompts treats each query as data first and a service second, which lets a company monetise attention while collecting the training signal for free; this is the vertical integration that makes a free consumer assistant a rational investment. Separately, closed-loop research frameworks now have agents generating the tasks and trajectories that train their successors, so a portion of the flywheel runs on synthetic usage. That connects the concept to Reasoning traces as an asset class and to the broader AI data supply chain in which labelled trajectories are bought and sold.

Trade-offs. Volume is not the same as quality, and a flywheel fed by low-signal chat logs can degrade a model as easily as improve it. Enterprises face the mirror problem: the value they create by using a vendor's model may accrue to the vendor's flywheel unless contracts route it back, which is why Two-tier data pricing has emerged, with cheaper tokens for customers who allow training and dearer ones for those who do not. Zero-retention terms protect confidentiality at the cost of any improvement from one's own usage.

What to watch. Whether synthetic self-play closes enough of the loop that consumer volume stops being decisive, whether regulators treat prompts-as-data as a privacy or a competition question, and whether any enterprise builds a private flywheel on its own agent traffic that measurably outperforms the vendor default.

Related terms

technique

Post-training

Post-training is everything done to a model after its initial pretraining on raw text: supervised fine-tuning, preference optimisation and reinforcement learning on tasks and environments.

needed by 4 · 17 linksconcept

AI data supply chain

The network of sources that supplies training and labelling data to AI labs and who controls it: labelling vendors, state dataset programmes, corporate records bought from defunct firms, user behaviour on platforms, and harvested chat transcripts.

needed by 2 · 12 linksconcept

Reasoning traces

Reasoning traces are the recorded step-by-step thinking a model produces on its way to an answer, whether shown to the user, hidden, or summarised.

needed by 2 · 9 linksconcept

Compute as currency

Compute as currency is the view that once models become commodities, advantage is decided by access to compute and energy rather than by access to any model, and that compute is increasingly traded, for revenue shares or training tokens, rather than simply sold by the hour.

10 linksconcept

Two-tier data pricing

Two-tier data pricing is selling the same model at two prices: a standard tier where the vendor promises not to train on prompts and completions, and a much cheaper contributor tier where the customer grants training rights.
