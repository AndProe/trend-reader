Open weights models

first seen 2026-W16 · needed by 11 · 36 links · 3 min read

An open weights model is one whose trained parameters are published so that anyone with enough hardware can run it themselves. The licence may or may not permit commercial use, and the training data and code are usually not included, which is why the term is narrower than open source. Chinese labs have led the category with families such as Qwen, GLM and Kimi, while Western labs have responded with permissively licensed releases of their own.

For an executive the practical implications are cost and control. Open models now handle most production tasks and cost a fraction of closed frontier APIs, which surveys rank as the single largest cost lever in AI spending. They can be hosted inside the firewall, switched inside a harness without vendor lock-in, and fine-tuned on proprietary data. The risks are the mirror image: guardrails can be stripped by anyone, licence terms are tightening in China even as Western terms loosen, and regulators are weighing whether to restrict the strongest open releases.

Also seen as open models, open source models, open-weight models

Deeper dive

How it works. Publishing weights means releasing the checkpoint file, usually with an inference recipe, so third parties can serve the model on their own GPUs, quantize it for smaller hardware, or fine-tune it for a niche. The ecosystem around a released base grows through derivatives, and published intermediate checkpoints let researchers study training stage by stage. Inference providers host popular open models at prices well below closed frontier APIs, which is why open models carry a majority of gateway token volume at roughly an 86 percent discount.

Why it matters now. The capability distance to the closed frontier keeps shrinking, a trend tracked as the Open-closed capability gap, and open models are now the default substrate for research, for sovereign national programmes, and for bring-up of new inference silicon. Competition has changed shape: openness became a strategic tool for Chinese labs and a soft-power argument for Western ones, and the licence has become a business question, with Revenue-share licenses emerging as a way to monetise weights that were previously priced as a commodity. Enterprise adoption is measurable, with Open model adoption metrics showing rapid growth in the US usage share and large companies shifting workloads.

Trade-offs. The strengths are also the risks. Because anyone can run the weights, safety training can be removed by abliteration or fine-tuning, so the defensive burden moves downstream to the systems that deploy the model rather than the model itself. The same accessibility raises Distillation attacks concerns for closed labs and dual-use concerns for governments, which expect militaries to post-train open models for offensive use. Small teams have shown they can train frontier-level cyber models on open bases. Fair evaluation is another live issue: several studies find a fine-tuned open model matches frontier closed models once the comparison is controlled, which cuts against the premium paid for closed access.

What to watch. The trajectory of Open weights regulation risk after any deliberate misuse incident, whether Western labs keep releasing at the frontier or retreat to smaller models, and how licence terms evolve as labs try to fund open releases. The interaction with Chinese AI chips and domestic inference stacks will determine whether open models also become the substrate for a parallel hardware ecosystem.

concept

Open-closed capability gap

The open-closed capability gap is the measured lag between the best openly downloadable model and the best closed frontier model, usually expressed as the number of months it takes open models to reach a given capability level.

15 linksconcept

Revenue-share licenses

Revenue-share licences are open-model licences that permit free download, experimentation and small-scale commercial use but require a commercial agreement, security review or revenue share once the deployer passes a stated revenue threshold.

7 linkstechnique

Distillation attacks

A distillation attack is the training of a model on a rival's outputs in order to copy its capability without its cost.

9 linksconcept

Chinese AI chips

Chinese AI chips are the domestically designed accelerators, led by Huawei's Ascend line, together with the interconnect and serving software co-designed around them.

8 linksconcept

Neo clouds

Neo clouds are GPU-rental cloud providers outside the hyperscalers, such as CoreWeave and the inference hosts Together, Fireworks, Modal and Baseten, that lease accelerators and serve open models.

8 linksconcept

Commoditise your complement

Commoditise your complement is the strategy of making the product adjacent to your own cheap or free, so that demand for what you actually sell grows.
