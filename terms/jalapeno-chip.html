Jalapeno chip

first seen 2026-W35 · 7 links · 3 min read

Jalapeno is a custom processor that OpenAI designed for inference, the work of serving answers from a trained model rather than training it. Broadcom built it, the design process used AI-assisted tooling, and the programme went from start to a finished chip in sixteen months. OpenAI reports that on single-token prediction it produces more tokens per megawatt than Nvidia's Blackwell generation, while Nvidia remains its preferred partner.

The strategic point is leverage. A lab that can make part of its own silicon negotiates differently with Nvidia and with cloud providers, and pushes the cost per token of its products down where power is the constraint. The claims deserve caution: they are OpenAI's own, the fair comparator is Nvidia's newer Vera Rubin rather than Blackwell, and the figures cover single-token prediction only. Executives buying compute should treat this as pressure on pricing, not a change in supply.

Also seen as Jalapeño, OpenAI inference chip, OpenAI Broadcom chip

Deeper dive

What it is. Jalapeno is an application-specific chip for inference, the phase where a trained model serves requests, as distinct from training. Inference has a different profile from training: the decode step that produces each token is bound by memory bandwidth rather than arithmetic, and the prefill step that reads the prompt is the reverse, which is why Prefill-decode disaggregation runs them on different hardware pools. A chip designed only for this work can drop training-oriented features and spend its silicon and power budget on memory and interconnect. OpenAI started the design in mid-2024 with Broadcom as its partner, used AI-assisted tooling during the design, and taped out in sixteen months, which is fast for a first chip.

Why it matters now. The headline is OpenAI's claim that Jalapeno beats Nvidia's Blackwell on tokens per megawatt in single-token prediction. Power, not chips, is the binding constraint on new capacity, so Performance per watt has become the metric that sets how much revenue a data centre can produce. Reported alongside Apple's AI computers, the chip was read as pressure on Nvidia's pricing and on the margin it earns from its largest buyers. It also completes a pattern: the hyperscalers each have Custom silicon for their own workloads, and OpenAI now joins them while keeping Nvidia as its preferred partner for the general case.

Trade-offs. The results are OpenAI's own and were not independently reproduced at the time of the reports. The fair comparator is Vera Rubin, Nvidia's next generation, not Blackwell, and the single-token figure does not cover the full serving stack of batching, speculative decoding and long-context prefill that benchmarks such as InferenceX try to capture. A first chip also has a software cost: the kernels, compilers and serving stack that make Nvidia hardware easy to use must be rebuilt, and the volume OpenAI can deploy is bounded by Broadcom's and the foundry's capacity.

What to watch. Independent measurements against Rubin-class systems, whether OpenAI serves a meaningful share of its traffic on the chip, and whether the sixteen-month design cadence holds for a second generation.

concept

Custom silicon

Custom silicon is the accelerator hardware designed by the companies that use it, such as Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium, Meta's MTIA and OpenAI's Jalapeño, rather than bought from Nvidia.

needed by 2 · 9 linksmetric

Performance per watt

Performance per watt measures how much useful AI output, typically tokens or benchmark-weighted intelligence, a system produces per unit of electrical power.

11 linkstechnique

Prefill-decode disaggregation

Prefill-decode disaggregation is a serving architecture that processes a request's prompt on one pool of hardware and generates its output tokens on another, transferring the KV cache between them.

6 linksproduct

InferenceX

InferenceX is an independent inference benchmark suite that measures how well accelerators serve large models, with an AgentX extension for agentic workloads, run across Nvidia, AMD, Google TPU and custom chips.

5 linksproduct

Vera Rubin

Vera Rubin is Nvidia's next-generation GPU platform, including the Rubin, Rubin Ultra and NVL72 rack configurations, which ships with HBM4 and is reported to deliver several times the performance per megawatt of GB200.
