Vera Rubin

first seen 2026-W32 · 5 links · 3 min read

Vera Rubin is the platform Nvidia follows Blackwell with. It pairs a new GPU with HBM4 memory, comes in rack-scale configurations such as NVL72, and is claimed to deliver about 5.4 times the performance per megawatt of GB200, with the first agentic benchmark results on the NVL72 rack reporting large gains in performance per dollar. The transition from Blackwell is described as easier than the Hopper-to-Blackwell move, with changes concentrated in network speed and rack power.

For an executive, Rubin is the reference point for inference cost over the next planning cycle and the comparator against which custom chips such as OpenAI's Jalapeno are judged. Two facts shape the risk picture: the Rubin Ultra part has reportedly been redesigned with less memory than planned, cutting to 192 gigabytes per GPU, and the platform is strong at compute-heavy stages of inference but less effective at memory-heavy decode. Capacity plans built on earlier specifications may need revisiting.

Also seen as Rubin, Rubin Ultra, Vera Rubin NVL72

Deeper dive

What it is. Rubin is the GPU generation after Blackwell, named for the astronomer Vera Rubin, with the Vera CPU alongside. It ships with HBM in its fourth generation, in NVL72 racks and larger NVL576 systems, and Rubin Ultra follows as the higher-memory variant. The headline efficiency claim is about 5.4 times the performance per megawatt of GB200, which matters because power, not capital, is the binding constraint on new capacity. The first agentic inference benchmarks on NVL72 report gains framed in performance per dollar rather than raw throughput, reflecting the workload mix that now dominates.

Why it matters now. Rubin sets the price and power baseline that every inference cost model uses for the next two years. Cloud providers describe the transition as much easier than Hopper to Blackwell, with changes confined to network interface speed and rack power, so deployment should be quicker. Two developments complicate the picture. The Rubin Ultra HBM respec, cutting capacity to 192 gigabytes per GPU, has pushed labs and researchers to find memory savings in model architecture and in offloading, including DRAM and SSD codesign, rather than waiting for hardware. And analysis of data movement finds Rubin strong at the arithmetic-intense midfill stage but less effective at memory-intense decode, which reinforces the case for disaggregated serving and shapes Performance per watt comparisons.

Trade-offs. Rubin's efficiency gains are real but uneven across inference phases, and the memory cut narrows the gap that alternatives can exploit. It is the right comparator for Custom silicon such as the Jalapeno chip, and the comparison turns on memory bandwidth and capacity as much as compute. Buyers weighing Rubin against custom or competitor parts also weigh the CUDA moat: the software ecosystem remains Nvidia's strongest argument even where a rival chip wins a specification.

What to watch. Final Rubin Ultra memory specifications, independent agentic benchmark results beyond Nvidia's own, and whether four-high HBM stacks and offloading techniques change the memory economics that the respec disturbed.
