A Dot is an agent that keeps running when nobody is looking at it. Each one gets its own cloud computer, connects to a user's mail, calendar, documents and other accounts, and is expected to anticipate work rather than answer questions. It is reachable from the chat product and from workplace messaging tools, and it can drive the provider's existing coding and chat threads on the user's behalf.

The commercial point is position rather than capability. An agent that holds a person's preferences, obligations and history becomes the place work starts, and whoever operates it influences which other services get called. That is the same position a search box once held, which is why several companies shipped comparable products within weeks of one another. For a buyer the questions are narrower: what the agent is permitted to do unattended, how its usage is metered against existing subscriptions, and what happens to the accumulated context if the relationship ends.

Also seen as Dot, OpenAI Dots

Deeper dive

How it works. A Dot runs as a persistent process with its own environment rather than as a session inside a chat window, which is what distinguishes it from a conversational assistant. Connections to a user's accounts are granted once and reused, so the agent accumulates context over time; proactive behaviour follows from that context rather than from a standing instruction. Because the agent can call the provider's other products, its activity consumes the same quota those products do, which makes metering a visible design choice rather than an implementation detail.

Why it matters now. Several vendors converged on this product shape in the same quarter, which makes it a category rather than a feature. The strategic claim behind it is that accumulated context compounds into switching cost: an agent that knows how someone works is harder to replace than a model that scores better. That is the mechanism by which Personalized agents become a distribution position, and it is why incumbents whose revenue depends on being present at the moment of decision have begun restricting third-party agents from their own surfaces.

Trade-offs. Unattended action is the whole proposition and the whole risk. Everything the agent can reach is reachable without a human in the loop unless a boundary is drawn explicitly, which puts Agent identity and permission scoping at the centre of deployment rather than at the edges. Building the agent on top of existing products rather than as a standalone system also means its activity is billed against the user's other allowances, so the apparent price and the real cost diverge.

What to watch. Whether proactive suggestions prove useful enough to justify standing access, whether accumulated context is ever made portable, and whether advertising or commission arrangements appear once the agent is choosing between suppliers.

Personalized agents

Personalized agents are AI agents that adapt their behaviour to one person's preferences, history and situation rather than responding identically for every user.

OpenClaw

OpenClaw is an open-source, self-hosted agent harness that runs on hardware the user owns, connects any model to messaging apps such as Telegram and WhatsApp, and keeps memory in plain Markdown files alongside a skills system and MCP tool access.

8 linksproduct

Grok Bot

Grok Bot is the chat-native agent application from Cursor and xAI for general knowledge work: each user gets a persistent, named bot with its own hosted computer, taught skills, browser control and the ability to converse with other agents.

9 linksconcept

Agent identity

Agent identity is a verifiable, durable identity for a persistent agent: a stable named entity with its own account, credentials and history rather than a disposable session.

needed by 1 · 12 linksconcept

Ads in AI

Ads in AI refers to advertising placed inside AI assistants and chatbots as a revenue line, with OpenAI's reported $1 billion annual run rate as the reference figure.

Meta Muse

Meta Muse is Meta's personal AI agent, running on its own secure virtual computer and able to browse the web, send mail, book travel and make purchases across a user's connected accounts.
