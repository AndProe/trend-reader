Custom silicon

first seen 2026-W32 · needed by 2 · 9 links · 3 min read

The largest buyers of AI hardware have concluded that paying Nvidia's margin on every accelerator is too expensive at their scale, and each has designed its own chip. Google's TPUs are the most mature and are now sold to outside buyers, including through special-purpose vehicles that account the sales gross. Amazon has Trainium, Meta has MTIA, and OpenAI's first-generation Jalapeño is reported to compete with Nvidia's Blackwell rather than trail it. Alongside these are purpose-built inference systems from Cerebras, whose wafer-scale CS-4 targets the fastest serving tiers, and Groq.

For an executive the point is that the AI hardware market is no longer a single-vendor market. Hyperscaler cloud pricing increasingly reflects in-house chips, so the choice of cloud is becoming a choice of silicon. Nvidia's position remains strong because of its software ecosystem and supply, but the phrase pressure on Nvidia now appears in analyses of its own record quarters, and a buyer with long-term commitments should model a future in which custom parts take a growing share of both training and inference.

Also seen as custom hardware, in-house chips, ASICs

Deeper dive

How it works. A custom accelerator is an application-specific chip tuned to the matrix operations that dominate model training and serving. Because the designer knows its own workloads, it can drop general-purpose features, optimise memory and interconnect for its serving patterns, and buy manufacturing capacity directly. The saving is the vendor margin plus whatever the specialisation yields in Performance per watt. The cost is the engineering programme, the multi-year lead time and the software stack, which must be built and maintained without the ecosystem that Nvidia's CUDA moat provides for free.

Why it matters now. Three developments in the past two months moved custom silicon from strategy to evidence. Google is externalising TPU inference, selling full systems to third parties and to SPVs, and TPUs are described as its lead at the bottom of the stack. OpenAI's Jalapeno chip is a first-generation part reported to be better than Blackwell on the metrics that matter for its workloads, which is unusual for a debut. And purpose-built inference systems, Cerebras's wafer-scale rack and Groq's LPUs, are the alternative that Nvidia's own low-latency serving work competes against. Together these are tracked as the long-run threat to hyperscaler demand for Nvidia GPUs, even as Nvidia posts record quarters and ships Vera Rubin. The same logic at national scale produces Chinese AI chips.

Trade-offs. Custom parts win on cost and power for the workloads they were designed for and lose flexibility when workloads change; a chip optimised for dense inference may fit agentic workloads, with their heavy CPU and memory traffic, less well, which connects to CPU pricing pressure. Software lag is the persistent cost: every new serving technique arrives on Nvidia first. And building silicon ties up capital and talent that could go into models, which is why only the Hyperscalers and the best-funded labs attempt it.

What to watch. TPU sales to outside customers as a share of Google's cloud revenue, Jalapeño's second generation and whether OpenAI runs a meaningful share of inference on it, Cerebras's traction in ultra-fast tiers, and whether Nvidia responds on price or on software to hold share.

Related terms

product

Jalapeno chip

Jalapeno is OpenAI's first in-house inference chip, built with Broadcom, designed from mid-2024 and taped out in about sixteen months.

7 linksconcept

Chinese AI chips

Chinese AI chips are the domestically designed accelerators, led by Huawei's Ascend line, together with the interconnect and serving software co-designed around them.

8 linksmetric

Performance per watt

Performance per watt measures how much useful AI output, typically tokens or benchmark-weighted intelligence, a system produces per unit of electrical power.

11 linksproduct

Vera Rubin

Vera Rubin is Nvidia's next-generation GPU platform, including the Rubin, Rubin Ultra and NVL72 rack configurations, which ships with HBM4 and is reported to deliver several times the performance per megawatt of GB200.

5 linksmetric

CPU pricing

CPU pricing tracks the cost and availability of server processors as agentic AI workloads push the ratio of CPU to GPU work in a data centre upward.

8 linksconcept

CUDA moat

The CUDA moat is Nvidia's software advantage: a mature programming stack and same-day framework support that make its GPUs the default target for every serving engine.
