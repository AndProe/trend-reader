Custom silicon

first seen 2026-W32 · needed by 2 · 9 links · 1 min read

Accelerators designed by the companies that use them rather than bought from Nvidia: Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium, Meta MTIA, OpenAI's Jalapeño, Cerebras wafers for fast tiers. Tracked as the long-run threat to Nvidia's share and, in Jalapeño's case, as a first-generation part that already competes.

Also seen as custom hardware, in-house chips, ASICs

Related terms

product

Jalapeno chip

OpenAI's Broadcom-built inference chip, designed from mid-2024 and taped out in 16 months, beating Blackwell on tokens per megawatt with single-token prediction; Rubin is the fair comparator and the results are OpenAI's own.

7 linksconcept

Chinese AI chips

Domestically designed accelerators (Ascend and successors) and the co-designed chip, interconnect and serving stacks that now train and serve frontier-adjacent Chinese models at scale, shown by GLM 5.3 Flash serving 100 trillion tokens a day and LongCat trained end to end on Ascend.

8 linksmetric

Performance per watt

Tokens or intelligence per unit of power, the metric OpenAI designs Jalapeño for and Tunguz's Koomey-style framing; the right unit when power, not money, is the binding constraint.

11 linksproduct

Vera Rubin

Nvidia's next-generation GPU platform (Rubin, Rubin Ultra), shipping with HBM4, 5.4x the performance per megawatt of GB200, reportedly redesigned with less memory; the right comparator for Jalapeño.

5 linksmetric

CPU pricing

The price and availability of server CPUs as agentic workloads push the CPU-to-GPU ratio up; Tomasz Tunguz cites Intel's server CPU average selling prices rising 27 percent year over year against falling volumes as one stage of his AI bullwhip.

8 linksconcept

CUDA moat

Nvidia's software advantage: the CUDA stack and the day-0 framework support that make its GPUs the default target for every serving engine, which competitors must match before their hardware counts.
