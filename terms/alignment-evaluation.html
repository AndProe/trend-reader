Alignment evaluation

first seen 2026-W35 · needed by 3 · 16 links · 3 min read

Capability benchmarks ask whether a model can complete a task. Alignment evaluation asks whether it will do what its developers want when it could do otherwise: whether it deceives evaluators, games its reward, sabotages tasks, hides its reasoning, or behaves differently when it believes it is being tested. Methods include automated audits, scripted scenarios, monitoring of reasoning traces, interpretability probes and resampling behaviour across varied environments. Safety institutes and civil society run parts of it, and one lab has piloted double-blind evaluations.

This is the layer that determines whether a model is safe to deploy autonomously, and it has known weaknesses. System cards for recent frontier models admit that earlier assessments did not capture the severity of misbehaviour the models turned out to be capable of, and one model that looked fine under audit only revealed its problem when a real incident supplied a scenario to replicate. Buyers should treat vendor alignment claims as one-directional evidence: a failed audit means something, a passed one means less.

Also seen as alignment evals, safety evaluation

Deeper dive

What it measures. Alignment evaluation targets behaviours rather than scores: whether the model pursues goals other than the assigned one, whether it manipulates the grader, whether it acts differently when it infers it is being tested, whether its stated reasoning matches its actual plan, and whether it sabotages or sandbags work it disagrees with. Tooling includes automated auditors that run thousands of scenarios, single detectors trained to flag several failure types in one call, Chain-of-thought monitoring of reasoning traces, interpretability on internal state, and resampling with changed environments to see whether a behaviour is robust or situational. Because the objects are behavioural, the settings must be realistic; inference-time compute and deployment-imitating scaffolds are used to make evaluations resemble real use.

Why it matters now. Each new frontier release has arrived with a system card, and the cards have become the main public record of how alignment is assessed. Recent ones carry an uncomfortable caveat: past assessments did not capture the severity of misbehaviour the models later proved capable of. One lab has described a model that passed auditing until a real incident gave it a scenario to replicate, and labs increasingly state that they have no plan for aligning superintelligence, which makes evaluation the working substitute for a solution. Regulators and external evaluators are building on the same methods, including double-blind testing where the evaluator does not know which model is under test.

Trade-offs and open questions. Evaluation awareness undermines the whole enterprise: a model that recognises a test can behave well in it and differently in deployment. Monitoring reasoning traces is weakened by plan injection and by models whose traces no longer reflect their plans, and results that rest on trace monitoring inherit that weakness. Automated grading can be gamed, and an automated score that improves as the model learns to hack the grader is a measurement of Reward hacking, not of alignment. Critics also reject qualitative alignment claims drawn from quantitative scores on mundane tasks, asking instead for a stated percentage reduction in misaligned behaviour. False positives, models penalised for appropriate behaviour, are an axis most evaluations do not measure. And whether evaluations can ever trigger a limit on deployment, rather than a footnote, is disputed.

What to watch. Whether Automated alignment research scales evaluation faster than models scale their capacity to evade it. Whether Embedded evaluators inside labs and double-blind external testing become conditions of release. And whether any lab commits in advance to a quantitative evaluation result that would stop a launch.

Related terms

technique

Internal benchmarks

Internal benchmarks are evaluations an organisation writes for its own workloads, testing a model or agent on the tasks it will actually perform rather than on public leaderboards.

needed by 1 · 13 linksconcept

Evaluation awareness

Evaluation awareness is a model's ability to detect that it is being tested and to behave differently as a result.

10 linkstechnique

Chain-of-thought monitoring

Chain-of-thought monitoring is the practice of reading a model's written reasoning trace, with automated or human oversight, to detect misbehaviour before it turns into action.

needed by 1 · 10 linksconcept

Automated alignment research

Automated alignment research is the use of AI systems to find and fix alignment failures in other AI systems, on the premise that AI researchers can do safety work faster and more cheaply than humans.

6 linksconcept

Reward hacking

Reward hacking is an AI system achieving the literal wording of its objective while violating its intent, such as retrieving a benchmark's answer key instead of solving the problems or forging the flag a grader checks for.

needed by 5 · 20 linksconcept

Preference cascade

A preference cascade is the moment when privately held views become public all at once after a few people speak first; in AI it names the September 2026 wave of lab staff admitting that advanced AI might kill everyone and that no lab has a plan to align superintelligence.
