GPT-5.6 Sol

first seen 2026-W33 · 5 links · 2 min read

GPT-5.6 Sol was the strongest of the three GPT-5.6 tiers OpenAI sold before the GPT-6 generation, with Terra and Luna beneath it and government variants carrying the same names. It was OpenAI's priciest mainline model of its generation, yet it drew a larger share of spending than Fable 5 at a lower per-token price, and a Cerebras-backed Ultrafast option served customers who valued latency over cost.

For an executive, Sol is the reference point in a price war. Its price was the level Claude Opus 5.5 matched before OpenAI halved its own pricing on the GPT-6 tiers, so contracts and unit-cost models anchored on Sol are now overpriced. It also remains the baseline in many evaluations, which makes it a convenient yardstick when checking whether a newer model is cheaper per task rather than merely cheaper per token.

Also seen as GPT-5.6, Sol

Deeper dive

What it is. GPT-5.6 was OpenAI's mainline family before GPT-6 Astra, sold in three tiers named Sol, Terra and Luna, with matching government variants. Sol was the top tier: the most capable, the most expensive per token and, in independent evaluations that measured all three tiers, the strongest. A Cerebras-backed Ultrafast mode served the same model at much higher token speed for customers who prized interactivity.

Why it mattered commercially. Sol showed that the priciest tier of a family can still win on revenue share. Despite a lower per-token price than Fable 5, Sol captured more spending, which suggests that developers route the bulk of their paid tokens to whichever model is strongest at the tasks that matter, not to the cheapest option. That pattern is the recurring lesson of Token share versus revenue share: the model that draws the most revenue is not necessarily the one that produces the most tokens.

Its place in the price war. Sol's price became the reference that competitors targeted. Claude Opus 5.5 matched Sol's price point on release, and OpenAI responded by halving prices across its GPT-6 tiers in the same week, with GPT-6 Luna at the bottom of the ladder. Any procurement model that anchored on Sol's list price is now stale, and the Sol-to-Astra comparison has become a standard flip test in evaluations, where Astra is scored against Sol as its baseline.

What to watch. Sol remains a fixed point in benchmark tables: physics re-grading studies report corrected scores for it, and coding-agent research uses its tests as the upper reference for feedback quality. As the GPT-6 tiers absorb its role, the open question is how long Sol stays available and at what price, which is the practical concern behind Frontier model access.
