SynthID marks AI-generated output so its origin can be detected later. The newer biological variant guides amino-acid choice in designed protein sequences and adjusts atomic coordinates in predicted structures, so the signature survives into the synthesised molecule rather than existing only in a file. A public detection site now lets anyone check images, video and audio against participating companies' watermarks.

The practical argument is about triage rather than enforcement. DNA synthesis providers screen orders against databases of known threats, and historically an unfamiliar sequence could be assumed to be an undiscovered natural one — an assumption AI-designed sequences break, leaving exhaustive manual review as the fallback. A watermark lets a provider distinguish an AI design from an unknown natural sequence quickly. It does not stop a bad actor, who would simply use an unmarked model; the mitigation it offers is throughput for legitimate research.

Also seen as SynthID Bio, SynthID detector

Deeper dive

How it works. For sequences the method biases the choice among equivalent amino acids; for predicted structures it fine-tunes part of a structure-prediction model so the watermark is carried in the weights, which means the signature appears regardless of who runs the model. Wet-lab testing across three target proteins reported watermarked designs matching unwatermarked ones on hit rate, binding affinity and sequence diversity, and structure watermarking was reported to preserve prediction accuracy with near-perfect detectability and robustness to noise and minor coordinate changes.

Why it matters now. Embedding the signature in model weights rather than in a post-processing step is what distinguishes this from the AI text watermarking approaches the vault already tracks, because it removes the option of simply skipping the marking step. The context is a screening layer under strain: generative design has invalidated the heuristic that unfamiliar means natural, which the vault records as a direct consequence of the capability growth under AI-designed viruses.

Trade-offs. Watermarking is a provenance tool, not a control: it identifies designs from participating models and says nothing about designs from anywhere else, which is why the published framing places it as one layer in a defence with acknowledged gaps elsewhere. Function preservation is reported by the developer across three targets, not independently replicated at scale. And a marking scheme's value depends entirely on adoption, so a public detector covering several companies is more consequential than any single implementation.

What to watch. Whether synthesis providers actually incorporate watermark checks into screening, how many model makers join the detector, and whether independent groups reproduce the function-preservation result.
