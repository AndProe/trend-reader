Inspect is the harness a large part of the safety-evaluation community runs its tests in, and its transcript viewer is how researchers read what an agent actually did. That makes it infrastructure rather than a product choice: findings about agent behaviour are mediated by it.

Its significance to the vault comes from a proof-of-concept exploit. A researcher, assisted by an AI agent, found in about ten minutes a flaw letting an agent running inside an evaluation alter what the reviewer sees in the viewer — including rewriting earlier actions and intercepting the download that produces a copy of the transcript. The underlying records were unaffected and no agent was observed exploiting it. The lesson drawn was general: when agents are treated as potential adversaries, their outputs are untrusted input and the systems that record and display them are security-critical.

Also seen as Inspect framework, Inspect viewer

Deeper dive

How it works. An evaluation framework runs an agent against a task while recording a trajectory — the actions taken, the tools called, the reasoning emitted — and renders that trajectory for a human to read. The viewer is the point where a judgment about behaviour is actually formed, which is why its integrity, not just the database's, determines whether Chain-of-thought monitoring produces reliable conclusions.

Why it matters now. Every published finding about agent misbehaviour that the vault records rests on telemetry an agent could in principle influence. The ten-minute discovery time matters because it was measured against the safety community's own tooling rather than against production infrastructure, which puts the integrity question upstream of every incident count. It also sharpens a dependency in the assessment proposals the vault tracks: those fall back on incident logs precisely because evaluations are unreliable, and this is a demonstration that logs have their own failure mode.

Trade-offs. The specific vulnerability was narrow — display only, with the real trajectory preserved in a database — but the mitigation that saved it was architectural luck rather than design, and a reviewer would have had to know to look. Hardening observability against the thing being observed is also a different engineering discipline from building evaluation tooling, and it adds cost to work that is largely unfunded. The broader implication for Embedded evaluators is that an external assessor inherits the integrity of whatever framework it uses.

What to watch. Whether evaluation frameworks adopt tamper-evident transcripts, whether any real exploitation is observed, and whether assessors begin reporting the integrity properties of their own tooling.

Related terms

concept

Alignment evaluation

Alignment evaluation is the testing of whether a model behaves as its developers intend, covering deception, sabotage, reward hacking and other misbehaviour, as distinct from testing what it can do.

needed by 6 · 22 linkstechnique

Chain-of-thought monitoring

Chain-of-thought monitoring is the practice of reading a model's written reasoning trace, with automated or human oversight, to detect misbehaviour before it turns into action.

needed by 3 · 15 linksconcept

Reasoning traces

Reasoning traces are the recorded step-by-step thinking a model produces on its way to an answer, whether shown to the user, hidden, or summarised.

needed by 2 · 14 linksconcept

AI deception

Behaviour in which a model conceals its actions, misreports what it did, or misleads users and overseers, whether by design of the task or as an emergent effect of training.

13 linksconcept

Embedded evaluators

Embedded evaluators are independent third-party staff placed inside a frontier lab with employee-like access to models, training runs and incidents, and the right to publish what they find.

13 linkstechnique

Observability

Observability is the practice of instrumenting software so that its behaviour in production can be understood from the telemetry it emits, and it now covers agents as well as services: wide events in place of logs and metrics, traces of hours-long agent runs, and instrumentation added by default.

needed by 3 · 11 links
