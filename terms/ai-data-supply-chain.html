AI data supply chain

first seen 2026-W33 · needed by 2 · 12 links · 3 min read

The AI data supply chain is the answer to a simple question: where did the data that trained this model come from? Suppliers have multiplied. Labs buy labelled data from a handful of vendors, and Chinese labs buy from the same US vendors that supply Anthropic and government agencies. Governments position themselves as dataset suppliers. Companies buy failed firms for their customer and HR records, as Google did with a defunct airline. Platforms turn user behaviour directly into training signal, and labs state that de-identified usage data may improve their models.

The darker end of the chain is harvested conversation data: proxy operators who resell access to frontier models and then sell the transcripts, including sensitive corporate and state material, back to labs as training data. For an executive the exposure runs both ways. A company's own data may be flowing into someone's training set through a reseller, and the models it buys may carry provenance risk it cannot see. Data governance and vendor contracts are the controls.

Also seen as corporate data acquisition, China data sourcing, China training data, training data sourcing

Deeper dive

How it works. Frontier training draws on public web text, licensed corpora, human-labelled and model-generated data for post-training, and usage data from deployed products. Each has a supply chain. Labelling runs through vendors that serve many customers at once, which is why the same labelled sets reach US labs, government agencies and Chinese competitors; see Data labeling and the emerging RL data industry. Corporate records enter through acquisitions, including whole-company purchases made for the data. Platform data enters through vertical integration, where an ads model turns clicks and prompts into training signal. Usage data enters through terms of service that permit de-identified transcripts to improve models. Proxy operators add a grey channel: they resell frontier access, harvest the transcripts and sell them onward, which lab misuse reports in 2026 documented in detail.

Why it matters now. Post-training quality depends on scarce, expensive data, so labs compete for supply; see Post-training. States have noticed: Beijing is pushing to become a global supplier of training datasets, which raises questions of influence over what models learn. Provenance is now a live question after a major mathematical result, since it is unclear whether de-identified user data fed the model that produced it. And the supply side interacts with pricing, where Two-tier data pricing separates data that may be trained on from data that may not.

Trade-offs. Broad data access improves models but multiplies legal and geopolitical exposure; see AI copyright and Data sovereignty. Tight Enterprise data governance protects a company's data but removes it from the models it would like to benefit from. Vendors that serve everyone leak advantage across borders.

What to watch. Whether governments restrict labelling exports, whether labs disclose training-data provenance, whether proxy resale is prosecuted, and whether enterprise contracts routinely carry no-training clauses.

Data labeling

Data labeling is the human annotation of training examples, an industry of roughly ten billion dollars that supplies models with judgements they cannot yet make for themselves.

Post-training

Post-training is everything done to a model after its initial pretraining on raw text: supervised fine-tuning, preference optimisation and reinforcement learning on tasks and environments.

needed by 4 · 17 linksconcept

Data sovereignty

Data sovereignty is the set of enterprise and government requirements on where data and prompts may be processed and stored, and under whose legal jurisdiction.

8 linksconcept

AI copyright

The legal questions around training models on copyrighted material and around models reproducing protected text, voices or likenesses, together with the product guardrails labs add in response.

4 linksconcept

Two-tier data pricing

Two-tier data pricing is selling the same model at two prices: a standard tier where the vendor promises not to train on prompts and completions, and a much cheaper contributor tier where the customer grants training rights.

7 linksconcept

Platform risk

Platform risk is the exposure a company takes on when its product depends on a single AI lab's proprietary models, given that the lab can learn from the customer's usage and later compete with it directly.
