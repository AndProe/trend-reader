AI data supply chain

first seen 2026-W33 · needed by 2 · 12 links · 1 min read

Where training and labeling data comes from and who controls it: labs buying labeled data from the same vendors, states positioning as dataset suppliers, companies buying defunct firms for their records, and platforms vertically integrating user behaviour into training data. The supply side of the data question, as opposed to its pricing.

Also seen as corporate data acquisition, China data sourcing, China training data, training data sourcing

How the sources use it

  • Zvi Mowshowitz (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-09-15) resellers selling user transcripts and labs forwarding user queries to Claude. Cross-source (Frontier Labs).
  • Anthropic (Frontier Labs, 2026-09-10) harvested user transcripts sold by proxy operators to labs as training data, including sensitive corporate and state data. Cross-source (Berman, Tunguz).
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-09) OpenAI's statement that de-identified usage data may improve its models.
  • Simon Willison (Simon Willison, 2026-09-08) whether de-identified usage data fed the model that solved the problem.
  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-03) the chain from user behavior to training data, which Meta is vertically integrating.
  • Forward Future team (Matthew Berman, 2026-08-19) Google buying a defunct airline's full customer and HR dataset to train products and models.
  • Forward Future team (Matthew Berman, 2026-08-18) Beijing's push to become a global supplier of AI training datasets, raising influence concerns.
  • Forward Future team (Matthew Berman, 2026-08-10) Chinese labs buying the same U.S. labeling data used by Anthropic and government agencies.

Related terms

concept

Data labeling

Human annotation of training data, a roughly $10 billion industry that reasoning traces from real usage now bypass, and the work Meta reassigned engineers to before its exodus.

needed by 1 · 8 linkstechnique

Post-training

Everything done to a model after pretraining (RL on environments, fine-tuning, preference data), now the whole recipe for frontier gains because the public web is exhausted and where open-model activity has moved.

needed by 3 · 15 linksconcept

Data sovereignty

Enterprise and government mandates on where data and prompts may be processed and stored, which decide which model tiers a buyer can use at all.

8 linksconcept

AI copyright

The legal questions around training models on copyrighted material and around models reproducing protected text, and the product guardrails labs add in response.

4 linksconcept

Two-tier data pricing

Selling the same model at two prices: a Standard tier on which the lab promises never to train on prompts and completions, and a Contributor tier about 92 to 95 percent cheaper in exchange for training rights, as Meta did with Muse Spark 1.3 at $1.25/$4.25 against $0.10/$0.20 per million tokens.

7 linksconcept

Platform risk

The exposure of building a business on a single lab's proprietary models when that lab can learn from the customer's usage data and later compete with it directly.
