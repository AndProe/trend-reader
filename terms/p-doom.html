P(doom)

first seen 2026-W37 · 8 links · 1 min read

A person's stated probability that AI kills everyone or causes an equivalent catastrophe, usually given with a time window. Zvi Mowshowitz compiles frontier-lab staff estimates ranging from near zero to about 50 percent, and Matthew Berman cites Evan Hubinger's figure of over 10 percent within a decade.

Also seen as extinction probability

Related terms

concept

Preference cascade

A wave of public admissions by frontier-lab staff that AI might soon kill everyone and that no lab has a plan to align superintelligence, set off by Dean Ball's admission that he had held back and turned into an avalanche by Jacob Coxon's resignation from Anthropic.

8 linksconcept

Recursive self-improvement

AI improving AI: models regenerating their successors' training data, and, in Sottiaux's nuance, frontier models optimising the serving infrastructure and kernels they run on.

14 linksconcept

Pacing the frontier

Slowing frontier AI development in a coordinated, verifiable way, which Anthropic splits into pacing within a company (its own safety-over-speed choices) and pacing across the field, for which it calls for a lawful, verifiable coordination mechanism.

needed by 1 · 14 linksconcept

Alignment evaluation

Testing whether a model's behaviour matches what its developers intend, as distinct from testing capability; in the arXiv line the setting in which evaluation awareness and realism become problems.

needed by 3 · 13 linksevent

Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act

A bill introduced by Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar to pause advanced AI development until safety standards exist, carrying a corporate death penalty, prison terms of up to 20 years and a new cabinet-level agency.

6 linksconcept

Lossy self-improvement

Nathan Lambert's counter-thesis to recursive self-improvement: AI does accelerate AI, but the gain leaks away because automatable research is too narrow against the exponential costs in scaling laws, parallel agents show diminishing returns, and resource and political bottlenecks govern what gets built.
