Distillation attacks

first seen 2026-W33 · 9 links · 2 min read

Training a model on a rival's outputs to copy its capability; the fear behind access cut-offs, Anthropic's countermeasures in Fable 5.1, and Zuckerberg's call to legalise it domestically. Lambert downplays it as the explanation for Chinese parity.

Also seen as distillation hacking, model distillation (as attack), distillation legality, illicit distillation

Related terms

Post-training

Everything done to a model after pretraining (RL on environments, fine-tuning, preference data), now the whole recipe for frontier gains because the public web is exhausted and where open-model activity has moved.

needed by 3 · 15 linksconcept

Access rationing

Labs and governments restricting who may use the strongest models: withdrawing models from distrusted platforms, gating frontier tiers, nationality screening and trusted-partner programmes.

needed by 1 · 15 linksconcept

Open-closed capability gap

The measured distance between the best open and best closed models: 13 months to close in the scaling era, 8.5 in the reasoning era, about five in the agentic era.

15 linksconcept

Release cadence

How often labs ship models, treated by Lambert as the main competitive lever for Chinese labs (days versus months) and quantified by SemiAnalysis at one frontier release every 51 days in the agentic era.

9 linksconcept

Benchmaxxing

Optimising a model release for industry-standard test sets, distinguished by Lambert from fraud: legitimate, expected, and a reason public scores overstate general capability.

11 linksconcept

Open weights regulation risk

The risk that regulation sidelines open-weights models: David Sacks, relayed in Berman's daily, argues that industry-backed standards and post-release monitoring requirements would favour closed systems without any explicit ban, and Lambert expects an intentional hack using an open model to bring severe restrictions on stronger open models.
