Data labeling

first seen 2026-W34 · needed by 1 · 8 links · 3 min read

Data labeling means paying people to mark up training material: rating answers, ranking pairs of responses, writing reference solutions, tagging images or transcribing speech. For most of the past decade it has been a large services business, and it is the work to which Meta reassigned engineers before its resignation wave.

It matters now because the economics are shifting. Models deployed at scale generate reasoning traces and outcome signals from real usage, which is a cheaper and larger supply than paid annotation, and advertising-funded assistants collect it at no marginal cost. Meanwhile the growth segment has moved from labels to environments, where vendors build simulated tasks for reinforcement learning, and some of those vendors are reported to be delivering poor inputs. For an executive the practical questions are whether a labeling budget should become an environments budget, whether proprietary usage data can substitute for either, and how a company acquires the rare cases its models lack rather than more of the common ones.

Also seen as human labeling, labeling vendors, data labeling market

Deeper dive

How it works. Supervised fine-tuning and preference training both consume labelled examples: a prompt paired with a good answer, or two answers with a human preference between them. Annotation vendors recruit and manage the workforce, build the tooling, and sell the output to labs, which use it in Post-training. The market grew to around ten billion dollars on the assumption that every capability improvement needed proportionally more human judgement, and that assumption is the one now under pressure.

Why it matters now. Three developments are eroding demand for generic labels. First, Reasoning traces captured from deployed models, plus the implicit signal of accepted or rejected outputs, provide a stream that scales with usage rather than headcount; the advertising model for prompts prices that stream at zero. Second, the frontier of post-training has moved to reinforcement learning in constructed environments, which is a different product with different vendors, and reports from inside labs describe some environment suppliers as a broken input. Third, the labour itself has become a destination for redeployed engineers rather than a contracted service, which at Meta contributed to the Meta engineering exodus when senior staff were moved onto AI training work.

Trade-offs. Usage data is abundant but skewed towards common cases, and models improve least where data is thinnest. The contrasting strategy is deliberate collection of rare scenarios, as a ride-hailing fleet does when it is instrumented to capture unusual road situations rather than routine trips. That is still labeling in effect, but designed around what the model lacks rather than what the vendor can supply cheaply. Quality also matters more than volume at the frontier, which favours expert annotators over crowd work and pushes the industry towards the specialised RL data industry rather than away from humans altogether.

What to watch. Whether the large annotation vendors successfully reposition as environment builders, whether labs bring expert labeling in-house, and how much of the AI data supply chain ends up sourced from users who were never told they were annotators.

technique

Post-training

Post-training is everything done to a model after its initial pretraining on raw text: supervised fine-tuning, preference optimisation and reinforcement learning on tasks and environments.

Meta engineering exodus

The Meta engineering exodus is the wave of senior engineers resigning from Meta after layoffs, forced reassignments to data-labelling work and a plan to cut teams sharply on the grounds that AI made them unnecessary, with departures going to AI labs and rivals.

Project OT

Project OT, short for Organization Transformation, was Meta's plan to cut engineering team sizes by 60 percent on the expectation that AI would make up the difference, a plan cancelled before execution.

needed by 1 · 7 linksconcept

RL data industry

The RL data industry is the market of vendors that build and sell reinforcement-learning environments, task suites and agent trajectories to model labs for post-training.

10 linksconcept

AI data supply chain

The network of sources that supplies training and labelling data to AI labs and who controls it: labelling vendors, state dataset programmes, corporate records bought from defunct firms, user behaviour on platforms, and harvested chat transcripts.

Reasoning traces

Reasoning traces are the recorded step-by-step thinking a model produces on its way to an answer, whether shown to the user, hidden, or summarised.
