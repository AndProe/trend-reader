Ultra-low latency

first seen 2026-W31 · 6 links · 1 min read

Sub-millisecond to microsecond response as an engineering property: table stakes rather than the moat at trading firms, and the target of persistent-kernel and wafer-scale inference for the fast tiers labs now sell.

Also seen as low latency engineering, microsecond latency

How the sources use it

  • Alex Salkever (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-22) latency named as the remaining barrier for time-critical decisions. Recurring; Cross-source (SemiAnalysis).
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-18) millisecond decisions inside game loops and browser races as the product's premise. New to this source; Cross-source (SemiAnalysis).
  • Google DeepMind (Frontier Labs, 2026-08-26) "sub-second" streaming latency is the design target of the live variant. Cross-source (Orosz, SemiAnalysis).
  • Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis, 2026-08-19) Orosz's term (Optiver); here the inference tier "fast modes" sell.
  • Gergely Orosz (Gergely Orosz, 2026-08-11) now table stakes rather than the moat.
  • Will Raphaelson (Technically, 2026-07-28) latency now costs money in games and trading, the condition Raphaelson says made the old speed-versus-safety trade-off untenable.

Ultrafast mode

A paid high-speed inference tier (OpenAI's Cerebras-backed Ultrafast at 14 to 15x, Xiaomi's and Z.ai's high-speed tiers on TileRT) sold as a product; the market signal that users pay for latency.

Quantitative trading

Firms trading their own capital with latency-driven watch-decide-send loops, now moving from raw speed to models; in the vault as the engineering culture Orosz profiled at Optiver and as a recruiting rival to the labs.

5 linkstechnique

KV cache

The transformer's per-token memory that grows with context; capacity limits long-context serving on SRAM machines, reuse across turns is the systems problem agentic serving turns on, and test-time training is proposed as a fixed-size alternative.

Jev

TypeSafe AI's decision model, released 15 September 2026, which takes a state and a set of options and returns a typed, calibrated choice in one parallel pass instead of generating text.

Interactivity versus throughput

The Pareto trade-off every inference system sits on: raising per-user tokens per second costs aggregate tokens per GPU, roughly 30x throughput for 10x speed in SemiAnalysis's numbers.
