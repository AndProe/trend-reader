Humanoid robot economics

first seen 2026-W35 · 6 links · 1 min read

The per-unit cost structure of general-purpose robots: the onboard compute, its utilisation, and whether the planning model runs on the robot or in a datacenter. SemiAnalysis models it as a make-or-rent decision in which shared datacenter GPUs cost less per FLOP beyond about five robots, while Berman relays the deployment and price claims of Chinese and US humanoid makers.

Also seen as robot unit economics

How the sources use it

  • Ivan Chiam, Gianluca Bencomo and colleagues (SemiAnalysis, 2026-09-14) the TCO comparison of Jetson Thor onboard against B300 offload, with home-robot utilisation of 4 to 8 percent as the decisive term.
  • Dylan Patel, Ivan Chiam, Gianluca Bencomo, Zane Fong, Bryan Shan, Reyk Knuhtsen (SemiAnalysis, 2026-09-14) per-unit compute cost and utilisation as the economics of humanoids. Cross-source mention (Berman).
  • Forward Future team (Matthew Berman, 2026-08-25) China's humanoid programmes and their unit costs as an industrial-policy story.
  • Forward Future team (Matthew Berman, 2026-08-25) China's hardware lead versus the difficult unit economics of training data collection. New, quantified.

On-device inference

Running models locally on the user's machine or on-premise, now competitive with cloud frontier models for most tasks at the cost of thinking time, and eliminating per-token metering for always-on agents.

Edge AI

Running inference on local hardware (Macs, single GPUs, devices) rather than in a data centre, driven by cheap capable open models; Tunguz's mainframe-to-personal analogy.

Test-time training

Gross margin

Revenue minus cost of serving: Nvidia at 75 percent, Anthropic from minus 94 percent to 40 to 50 percent, SemiAnalysis's 60 to 85 percent inference-margin estimates.

Performance per watt

Tokens or intelligence per unit of power, the metric OpenAI designs Jalapeño for and Tunguz's Koomey-style framing; the right unit when power, not money, is the binding constraint.
