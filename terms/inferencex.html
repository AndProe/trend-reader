InferenceX

first seen 2026-W33 · 5 links · 3 min read

InferenceX runs the same models and serving stacks across competing hardware and reports tokens per second, cost per token and latency along the full curve from bulk throughput to high interactivity. AgentX does the same for agent workloads, which have different traffic patterns: long contexts, many short turns and heavy cache reuse. Recent rounds covered six Nvidia SKUs and AMD's MI355X, added Google's TPUv7, integrated power measurement, and served as the harness for experiments such as running accelerators with reduced HBM.

For an executive deciding on inference hardware or a cloud provider, InferenceX is the closest thing to an audited comparison. Vendor multiples such as a claimed 67-fold performance per dollar improvement are quoted at a fixed latency target, and the advantage changes along the curve, so the useful question is which point on the curve a workload occupies. Independent verification of a custom chip's numbers is also a signal about whether that chip is a threat to the incumbent.

Also seen as InferenceX suite, AgentX, AgentX benchmark

Deeper dive

What it measures. InferenceX deploys a fixed set of open models on each accelerator with the best available serving software and sweeps the load, recording throughput per chip and per dollar at each level of per-user speed. The result is a Pareto curve per hardware platform, which captures the Interactivity versus throughput trade-off directly rather than reporting a single peak number. AgentX extends this to Agentic inference workloads, where requests carry long prefixes, reuse cached context and arrive in bursts, and where prefill and decode balance differently from chat. Power measurement has been integrated so that performance per watt appears alongside performance per dollar.

Why it matters now. Hardware claims have become the currency of the AI build-out, and most are made by the vendor. InferenceX supplied the independent numbers behind the Vera Rubin NVL72 agentic-inference results, ran the reduced-HBM experiments that inform the argument for fewer memory stacks, and verified the serving figures for OpenAI's Jalapeno chip, which is the claim that a custom part can beat Nvidia's Blackwell. Its expansion to Google's TPUv7, timed with Google's move to sell TPU capacity externally, and its coverage of AMD's MI355X make it the venue where the CUDA moat is tested: if a rival chip serves the same model as well with a different software stack, the moat is narrower than assumed.

Limits. Results depend on serving software as much as silicon, and a vendor that optimises for the benchmark can move its curve without moving real workloads. The model set is open-weight by necessity, so closed frontier models are inferred rather than measured. Multiples quoted at one latency target can reverse at another, which makes headline figures easy to misuse. And an independent lab is only as independent as its funding and access, which for a benchmark that requires early hardware always involves the vendors.

What to watch. Whether AgentX becomes the standard for agent-serving comparisons. How TPU and custom chips place once full results are published. And whether power-normalised results shift procurement toward efficiency as electricity becomes the constraint.

Related terms

concept

Agentic inference workloads

Agentic inference workloads are the production traffic pattern created by coding and computer-use agents: many turns per task, context that accumulates toward a million tokens, very high reuse of shared prefixes, and bursts of parallel sub-agent calls.

Jalapeno chip

Jalapeno is OpenAI's first in-house inference chip, built with Broadcom, designed from mid-2024 and taped out in about sixteen months.

Interactivity versus throughput

Interactivity versus throughput is the trade-off every inference system sits on: raising the tokens per second a single user receives lowers the total tokens a GPU can serve, by roughly thirty times throughput for a tenfold gain in speed.

needed by 1 · 10 linksconcept

CUDA moat

The CUDA moat is Nvidia's software advantage: a mature programming stack and same-day framework support that make its GPUs the default target for every serving engine.
