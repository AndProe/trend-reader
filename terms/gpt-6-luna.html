GPT-6 Luna

first seen 2026-W39 · 11 links · 3 min read

GPT-6 Luna is the low-cost member of the GPT-6 line, sitting beneath Sol and Astra. Its release price is half what its GPT-5.6 predecessor charged after that model's own 80 percent cut in July, and OpenAI has said the reduction is permanent rather than promotional. It is also the only model of the 22 September pair that reaches Free and Go users in the desktop app.

The executive question is where Luna is good enough. On Frontier Code it scores 66.6 percent at roughly 22 cents a task, against Astra's $1.70 at low effort, which makes it the default candidate for high-volume coding and text work. On computer-use benchmarks the gap is wider, 53 against 73 percent on OSWorld, so agentic desktop workflows still justify the expensive tier. Budget owners should re-run cost-per-task comparisons rather than carry forward assumptions from the July price list.

Also seen as Luna

Deeper dive

What it is. Luna is the bottom tier of the GPT-6 family, positioned for volume rather than peak capability. It shares the generation's lineage with GPT-6 Astra but is served at $0.10 per million input tokens, $0.01 for cached input and $0.50 per million output tokens, and it is the only GPT-6 model available to Free and Go users in OpenAI's desktop app.

The pricing move. The 22 September release cut Luna's price 50 percent below its GPT-5.6 equivalent, which had itself been cut 80 percent in July. OpenAI framed the new level as permanent. The move landed the same day as Claude Opus 5.5, which matched OpenAI's mid-tier price, and it pushed the floor of Frontier model pricing down again: a price war at the bottom of the market while the top tier stays fixed.

Where it holds up and where it does not. The right comparison is Cost per task, not price per token. On the Frontier Code benchmark Luna reaches 66.6 percent at about 22 cents a task, while Astra at low effort costs $1.70 for a higher score; for many coding jobs Luna is the better trade. On OSWorld, the computer-use benchmark, the gap is the widest of any reported comparison, 53 against 73 percent, so tasks that require long sequences of desktop actions fail more often on the cheap tier and the retries eat the saving. Choosing Reasoning effort levels on the expensive model is the other lever, since Astra at low effort is already far cheaper than at high.

What to watch. Whether developers actually route volume to Luna or keep paying for Astra, which bears on the split between token share and revenue share across the family. Whether the permanent-price claim survives the next capacity crunch. And whether the cheap tier's computer-use gap closes with the next release, which would remove the main reason to keep the expensive tier in routine agent work.
