CPU pricing

first seen 2026-W34 · 8 links · 2 min read

Server CPUs were the one part of an AI data centre nobody expected to run short. GPUs did the model work, and CPUs sat alongside them doing housekeeping. Agents changed that: every tool call, sandbox, file operation and web request an agent makes runs on ordinary processors, so the amount of CPU work per unit of GPU work has risen sharply. Intel's server CPU average selling prices rose 27 percent year on year while unit volumes fell, and spot availability has since disappeared, with list prices up 10 to 20 percent and six-month lead times.

For executives running compute-intensive services the practical advice is to reserve capacity now, even at a high price, rather than wait for prices to normalise. For everyone else the lesson is that agentic AI has an infrastructure bill beyond accelerators, and budgets that model only GPU spend will be wrong.

Also seen as CPU shortages, server CPU average selling prices

Deeper dive

What it measures. CPU pricing covers the average selling price of server processors, the availability of spot capacity and the lead time on new orders. Together they act as a market signal of how tight supply is. Rising prices against falling volumes, which is what Intel reported, indicate demand pulling forward the higher-end parts, and the disappearance of spot supply means buyers can no longer fill gaps opportunistically.

Why it is happening. The driver is Agentic inference workloads. A chat request is dominated by GPU time; an agent request is a loop of model calls interleaved with tool calls, each of which runs on CPU. As agents take on longer tasks the CPU-to-GPU ratio in a serving cluster moves toward parity. This is one stage in the Bullwhip effect running through AI hardware, where the shortage moved from GPUs to HBM and has now reached CPUs, with each tier over-ordering to protect itself and amplifying the signal upstream.

Trade-offs. Reserving CPU capacity now protects service delivery but at prices that may look expensive in hindsight. Building around Custom silicon or Arm-based parts offered by the Hyperscalers reduces exposure to one vendor but creates its own migration cost. Re-engineering agents to make fewer tool calls, or to run them on cheaper hardware, is the most durable response but requires the agent product to be mature enough to optimise.

What to watch. Whether the 10 to 20 percent list increases hold or reverse when capacity catches up, whether the shortage propagates to networking, the last primitive not yet constrained, and whether hyperscalers begin to ration GPU access and CPU access together as a bundle. A sustained rise in CPU cost per agent task would push the industry toward more efficient harness design as a direct cost-saving measure.

Related terms

concept

Agentic inference workloads

Agentic inference workloads are the production traffic pattern created by coding and computer-use agents: many turns per task, context that accumulates toward a million tokens, very high reuse of shared prefixes, and bursts of parallel sub-agent calls.

GPU access

GPU access is the question of who can obtain frontier compute and on what terms, from hyperscalers that lock up the supply pipeline to everyone outside it facing scarcity.

HBM

High Bandwidth Memory is the stacked DRAM mounted beside an AI accelerator that feeds it data fast enough to keep the compute busy.

Hyperscalers

Hyperscalers are the largest cloud providers, AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, together with Oracle and increasingly the frontier labs themselves, that buy most AI accelerators, issue most AI-related debt and build the gigawatt campuses.

needed by 1 · 16 linksconcept

Custom silicon

Custom silicon is the accelerator hardware designed by the companies that use it, such as Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium, Meta's MTIA and OpenAI's Jalapeño, rather than bought from Nvidia.

needed by 2 · 9 linksconcept

Bullwhip effect

The bullwhip effect is a supply-chain pattern in which small swings in end demand grow larger at each upstream tier, because every buyer over-orders to protect itself.
