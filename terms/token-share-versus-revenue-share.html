Token share versus revenue share

first seen 2026-W34 · 9 links · 3 min read

Token share counts the volume of model usage; revenue share counts the money. The two have separated. Open-weight models, many of them Chinese, handle a growing share of tokens because they are cheap and good enough for routine work, while the closed labs at the frontier keep most of the revenue because the highest-value tasks still go to the most capable tier. One recent split puts volume at roughly 62 to 38 in favour of open models, with value still accruing to two labs.

For an executive, the framing separates two purchasing decisions. Routine, high-volume workloads are a commodity buy where price and portability matter. Differentiated workloads that combine the model with proprietary data are where vendors can charge, and where lock-in risk lives. Gateway data showing the most capable model taking under 4 percent of spend while frontier-tier models still handle 45 percent of large-account tokens suggests the middle of the market is where most budget actually sits.

Also seen as value-added tokens versus commodity tokens, token share, volume versus value

Deeper dive

How it works. Token share is a volume metric: which models process the most requests across gateways, clouds and self-hosted deployments. Revenue share is a value metric: which vendors are paid the most. Open-weight models win volume because they can be run anywhere at marginal cost, their capability trails the frontier by a shrinking margin, and Chinese labs release aggressively. Closed labs win revenue because the tasks worth paying a premium for cluster at the top of capability, and because enterprises pay for reliability, support and safeguards rather than raw tokens. The prediction that follows is a two-sided market: cheap, metered frontier access as a commodity, and defensible inference where the model is combined with proprietary data the buyer alone holds.

Why it matters now. The Open-closed capability gap has narrowed enough that volume can defect to open models without visible quality loss, and gateway statistics show the effect: the single most capable model captures only a few percent of spend, while roughly 45 percent of large-account tokens still go to the frontier tier as a class. A split of about 62 to 38 in token volume, with value concentrated in two labs, is the figure now used to characterise the market. This is the empirical basis for arguing that no single lab will be the only company left, and that the middle tier is the most important market.

Trade-offs. Volume without revenue is expensive for whoever serves it, which is why Neo clouds and inference providers compete on utilisation rather than margin. Revenue without volume risks losing the data flywheel and developer habits that volume brings. For buyers, chasing the cheapest tokens raises Platform risk if the open model's maintainers change course, while paying frontier prices for commodity work wastes budget.

What to watch. Whether Frontier model pricing compresses toward open-model rates, whether the two-lab concentration of revenue holds as Open weights models improve, and whether proprietary-data inference proves as defensible as predicted.

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.

Open-closed capability gap

The open-closed capability gap is the measured lag between the best openly downloadable model and the best closed frontier model, usually expressed as the number of months it takes open models to reach a given capability level.

Price elasticity

Price elasticity is the degree to which demand for a model or AI service changes when its price changes.

Complementary assets

Complementary assets are the scarce resources, chiefly compute, data and distribution, that end up capturing most of the value of a general-purpose technology instead of the people who invented it.

Frontier model pricing

Frontier model pricing is the per-token list price of the top-tier models, anchored at ten dollars per million input tokens and fifty per million output for both Astra and Fable 5.1.

Neo clouds

Neo clouds are GPU-rental cloud providers outside the hyperscalers, such as CoreWeave and the inference hosts Together, Fireworks, Modal and Baseten, that lease accelerators and serve open models.
