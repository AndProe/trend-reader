On-device inference

first seen 2026-W34 · needed by 1 · 13 links · 3 min read

On-device inference is the practice of running an AI model where the work happens rather than in a vendor's data centre. The model's weights are downloaded once and executed on local processors, which can be a workstation GPU, a laptop's neural engine, a phone chip or the onboard computer of a robot. Quality is now close to cloud frontier models for most everyday tasks, with the main cost being longer response times on smaller hardware.

For an executive this changes three budget lines at once. Always-on agents that sit idle most of the day become cheap when there is no per-token bill. Confidential workloads such as sequencing data or trading research can be run without the data leaving the building. The trade is capital expenditure on hardware, plus new engineering constraints around battery, heat and thermal limits on edge devices, so the decision is about which workloads justify owning the compute.

Also seen as local models, local inference, on-premise models

Deeper dive

How it works. A model is shipped as Open weights models and loaded into local memory, where a runtime such as an operating-system inference layer dispatches work across GPU, NPU and CPU using execution providers written by the hardware vendors. Fitting a capable model on a device usually depends on Quantization, which shrinks weights to lower precision, and on the rise of Small language models tuned for a narrow job. The harness, meaning the tooling that plans, calls tools and checks results, is almost always local; the model is local when it fits and falls back to a cloud endpoint when it does not, a pattern that overlaps with Smart model routing.

Why it matters now. Two forces converged in 2026. Open models reached parity with cloud frontier models on most tasks, so the quality penalty for staying local shrank to a latency penalty. At the same time agent workloads changed shape: Persistent agents that wait, poll and react spend most of their time idle, which makes per-token cloud pricing a poor fit and a flat hardware cost attractive. Vendors have responded with workstations sized for trillion-parameter local models, CPU-first model families, and consumer pitches built around private on-device assistants. Research groups have followed, building agent frameworks for DNA sequencing and quantitative alpha mining on the explicit grounds that the data must not leave the environment.

Trade-offs. Local inference exchanges operating cost for capital cost and engineering burden. A voice model on a headset brings processor, battery, thermal and microphone requirements with it, and those constraints cap model size far below what a data centre can serve. For robots, the comparison is costed per FLOP: onboard compute is expensive per unit of work but avoids network latency and dependence on connectivity. Updates, patches and safety controls also become the operator's responsibility rather than the vendor's, which is why on-device deployment sits close to Edge AI debates about who maintains the fleet.

What to watch. The gap between local and cloud thinking time, the maturity of vendor execution providers on consumer silicon, and whether energy per task on local hardware keeps falling. If open models continue to close the capability gap, the default for confidential or always-on workloads shifts from renting tokens to owning inference.

Related terms

concept

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.

needed by 11 · 36 linksconcept

Edge AI

Edge AI is the running of model inference on local hardware, from a desktop Mac or a single GPU to the processor inside a robot or a pair of headphones, rather than in a data centre.

9 linksconcept

Small language models

Small language models are models designed to be small from the start, typically from around one to fourteen billion parameters, rather than large models compressed after training.

8 linkstechnique

Quantization

Quantization reduces the numeric precision of a model's weights and activations after training, for example from 16-bit to 8-bit or 4-bit formats, so that it uses less memory, bandwidth and energy.

7 linksconcept

Persistent agents

Persistent agents are AI agents designed to run continuously and carry memory, identity and relationships across sessions, devices and tasks, instead of starting fresh on each request.

needed by 2 · 16 linkstechnique

Smart model routing

Smart model routing sends each request to the cheapest model that can handle it, reserving frontier models for the steps that need them and often running a small local model as the dispatcher.
