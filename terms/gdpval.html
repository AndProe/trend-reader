GDPval

first seen 2026-W33 · 9 links · 3 min read

GDPval measures a model's performance on tasks drawn from real occupations: documents, analyses, spreadsheets and other deliverables of the kind knowledge workers produce, judged against expert-quality outputs and reported as an Elo rating. Unlike a coding benchmark, it is meant to approximate the work of the broader economy rather than of engineers.

It matters now because release reviews have settled on it as one of two numbers to read from a model's table, alongside a terminal-agent coding score. Recent readings include Grok 4.6 posting the top score in August and Claude Opus 5.5 at 1,846 Elo against 1,735 for Fable 5.1 in September, a gap that suggests the newer, cheaper model outperforms the previous flagship on office work. For an executive it is the benchmark that speaks most directly to labour questions: which roles a model can plausibly cover, how fast that frontier moves, and how a vendor's claim of productivity gains should be discounted.

Also seen as GDPval 2.1

Deeper dive

What it measures. GDPval draws tasks from occupations weighted by their contribution to economic output, asks models to produce the actual deliverable, and has experts compare the result with professional work. The pairwise judgements are aggregated into an Elo rating, so a score is relative to the other models in the pool rather than an absolute percentage. The current version is 2.1. As a member of the family of Benchmarks, it is unusual in targeting general knowledge work rather than a technical skill, which makes it the counterpart to Terminal-Bench in the two-number reading of a release table: one score for agentic coding, one for office deliverables.

Why it matters now. The numbers have started to separate models in ways that matter to buyers. Grok 4.6 posted the top GDPval score in August, its strongest relative result, and in September Claude Opus 5.5 scored 1,846 against 1,735 for Fable 5.1, placing a cheaper model above the outgoing flagship on the benchmark most tied to office work. That makes GDPval the score to cite in the AI labor displacement debate and in claims about AI productivity, since it is the only widely reported number that attempts to measure the work most employees do.

Trade-offs. An Elo rating hides the absolute level: a model can lead the pool and still fall short of an expert on most tasks. The benchmark is owned by one lab, which is a conflict when that lab's models are scored on it, and expert judgement of deliverables is expensive, so the task set is small relative to the economy it represents. Like any public benchmark it is exposed to Benchmaxxing, and a strong score on polished deliverables says nothing about the reliability of the long agentic workflows that produce them in practice, nor about Cost per task.

What to watch. Whether independent parties reproduce GDPval rankings, whether the gap between the leading models and expert baselines closes, and whether the score begins to appear in enterprise procurement alongside the coding benchmarks that currently dominate.
