Claude Opus 5.5

first seen 2026-W39 · 11 links · 3 min read

Opus 5.5 is the model Anthropic released on 22 September 2026 with the pitch that it matches Fable 5.1 on quality while costing about 40 percent less on typical workloads and producing output more than 30 percent faster. The pricing is the headline: $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output, ending four consecutive Opus versions at $5 and $25, with cache reads cut 60 percent on top.

For an executive the release is a price-war event. It landed the same day as GPT-6 Sol at $2 and GPT-6 Luna at 10 cents, so the cost of frontier-grade coding dropped sharply across vendors at once. Two caveats apply. One reading of the system card is that this is a smaller Fable 5.1 rather than a new Opus generation, so the comparison should be run on the organisation's own tasks. And its safety classification is disputed: some analysts argue it should be classified as a higher-tier cyber model than Anthropic states.

Also seen as Opus 5.5

Deeper dive

What shipped. Opus 5.5 is the successor to Claude Opus 5 and the first model in the 5.5 series. Anthropic's claims are Fable 5.1-level quality at 40 percent lower cost on typical workloads, output more than 30 percent faster, and Cache pricing cut by 60 percent. The list price of $4 and $20 per million tokens is the first reduction in the Opus line, which had held at $5 and $25 across four versions. The model ships with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards covering cybersecurity, biology and distillation, and inherits the classifier-and-fallback mechanism from Claude Fable 5.1.

Why it matters now. The release turned a two-lab capability race into a price war. Same-day comparisons placed Opus 5.5 at $4 against GPT-6 Sol at $2 and GPT-6 Luna at 10 cents on a coding benchmark, and the argument that the most important market in AI is the middle, not the frontier, was written against this event. That reframes Frontier model pricing as a question of Cost per task rather than per-token rate, since cache terms and token efficiency now move the bill more than list price. Pre-deployment evaluation summaries use Fable 5.1 as the reference, and the finding that Opus 5.5 lands near it on capability supports the reading that Anthropic has produced a cheaper, smaller model of the same class rather than a step up.

Trade-offs. A smaller model at Fable-level quality is a strong product, but the system card raises two disputes. Its chain-of-thought controllability is low and its monitorability lines up with the earlier Opus trend rather than the Mythos one, which matters for oversight of agentic use. And the cyber classification is contested: critics call it a Tier 2 cyber model that Anthropic declines to classify as such, and doubt the reported sandbagging rates. Buyers get lower cost with somewhat less clarity on what the model can do in adversarial domains.

What to watch. Independent Alignment evaluation results as the capabilities reviews arrive, whether the $4 and $20 price holds or falls further in response to Sol and Luna, and whether Reasoning effort levels let customers tune Opus 5.5 down to Luna-class costs for routine work.

Claude Opus 5

Claude Opus 5 is Anthropic's previous flagship model, priced at $5 and $25 per million tokens like every Opus before it, and now the baseline against which Opus 5.5 and rival launches are measured.

Frontier model pricing

Frontier model pricing is the per-token list price of the top-tier models, anchored at ten dollars per million input tokens and fifty per million output for both Astra and Fable 5.1.

Cost per task

Cost per task is the dollar cost of getting a unit of work actually completed by an AI system, measured at a given quality level, rather than the price per token or the score on a benchmark.

23 linksconcept

Cache pricing

Cache pricing is the rate charged for reading prompt tokens that a model provider has already processed and stored, as opposed to the full price for fresh input tokens.

6 linksconcept

Reasoning effort levels

Reasoning effort levels are the settings, typically low, medium, high, extra high and max, that control how much internal thinking a model does before answering a request.

Pacing the frontier

Pacing the frontier is the proposal that leading AI labs slow the release of their most capable models in a coordinated, verifiable way, for example by shipping every two months rather than continuously, rather than pausing altogether.
