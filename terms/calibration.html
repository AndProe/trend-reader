Calibration

Accuracy answers how often a model is right. Calibration answers whether its confidence can be trusted — whether the things it says it is 90 percent sure about are right about nine times in ten. The two come apart routinely, and the gap matters the moment a system does something different depending on the confidence it receives.

This is the difference between a model used for advice and a model used as a component. A chatbot's miscalibration is an irritation the reader absorbs. A router that misjudges which requests need the expensive model, a guardrail that passes a borderline case because it was wrongly confident, or an abstention mechanism that declines the wrong cases all convert miscalibration into operational cost. Diagnostic evaluation is now showing this directly: models with near-identical accuracy differ substantially in the reliability of their high-confidence predictions, so selecting on accuracy alone selects blindly on the property that governs the behaviour.

Also seen as calibrated confidence, probability calibration

Deeper dive

How it works. Calibration is measured by comparing predicted probabilities to observed outcome frequencies, usually in bins, and is distinct from discrimination, which only asks whether the model ranks correct answers above incorrect ones. A system can rank well and be badly calibrated, or be well calibrated and rank poorly. Diagnostic suites separate these by scoring contrast groups — controlled variations of the same item — alongside probability ordering and empirical selective coverage, which is the share of items a model would answer at a given confidence threshold.

Why it matters now. Calibration has moved from a research nicety to a product property because of Decision model deployments, where the returned probability is the output the calling system branches on. Published diagnostics report the dissociation plainly: one system correctly ranking the positive member of a pair above the negative in 92.3 percent of pairs while answering both members correctly in only 74.1 percent. Reasoning effort also moves it — the same model has been measured at 93.5 percent contrast-group accuracy at high effort against 81.5 percent at its lowest — which makes calibration a function of serving configuration, not only of weights.

Trade-offs. Improving calibration can cost accuracy, and post-hoc recalibration on one distribution does not survive a shift to another, so a figure measured on a benchmark is weak evidence about production. Vendors in the decision-model category acknowledge jagged edges here, which is honest but leaves the buyer without a number to plan against. Self-reported confidence is also not the only route: Self-verification and ensemble disagreement give partially independent signals that do not depend on the model's own introspection.

What to watch. Whether calibration is reported alongside accuracy in model cards as a matter of course, whether it holds across reasoning-effort settings rather than only at the top, and whether it degrades on the long-tail inputs where guardrails and escalation decisions actually bind.

Decision model

A decision model is a model that returns a choice from a fixed set of options together with calibrated probabilities, rather than generating text.

LLM-as-judge

LLM-as-judge is the practice of using a language model, or a panel of them, to score outputs against a rubric in place of human graders.

Self-verification

Self-verification is an AI system checking its own work before declaring it done, either inside the model's reasoning or through a harness that runs tests, reads telemetry, inspects screenshots and selects among candidate answers.

needed by 1 · 9 linksconcept

Agent reliability

Agent reliability is whether an agent completes a given workflow acceptably often enough to be trusted with it, measured as a target such as success on nearly every run under a defined oversight policy and cost, rather than as a best-case capability score.

Benchmaxxing

Benchmaxxing is the practice of tuning a model release so it scores well on the industry-standard public test sets, which is legitimate and expected rather than fraudulent.
