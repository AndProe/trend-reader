AI fatigue

first seen 2026-W33 · 7 links · 2 min read

AI fatigue is what happens to engineers when AI adoption becomes a mandate rather than a tool. It shows up as burnout and loss of drive under usage quotas, as weariness with a hype cycle that oversells every release, as disgust at the low-quality output that floods review queues, and as fear about what the technology means for a career. Experienced engineering leaders separate these varieties because they call for different answers.

The common thread is that mandates backfire. Teams told to use AI for everything produce more code and less ownership, and their most capable people disengage or leave. The alternative described by practitioners is to run experiments: give teams a specific problem, measure what the tools do for it, and let adoption follow evidence. For an executive the risk is quiet: the metrics that mandates optimise, such as tool usage or lines generated, keep rising while the team's judgement and retention fall. AI fatigue is a leading indicator of both.

Also seen as AI burnout, hype fatigue

Deeper dive

What it looks like. Practitioners describe several strains. Slop fatigue is the exhaustion of reviewing large volumes of generated code and prose that is plausible but careless, the reviewer's side of AI slop. Hype fatigue is the reflexive scepticism that sets in after repeated claims that the next release changes everything, and it makes engineers dismiss real gains along with the marketing. Fear is the quieter strain: uncertainty about whether the craft one spent a career building still counts. Mandated usage compounds all three by removing the sense of choice that makes a tool feel like leverage rather than surveillance.

Why it matters now. Companies are setting usage targets and measuring token consumption as if volume were value, a pattern that shades into Tokenmaxxing. Under those conditions engineers stop reading what they ship, which accumulates Cognitive debt, and burnout rises even while output metrics improve. The paradox that fatigue coexists with a large Capability overhang, where the tools could do more than teams extract from them, is the point: the constraint is organisational, not technical.

Trade-offs. Letting adoption be voluntary risks leaving gains on the table and lets sceptics opt out indefinitely. Mandates get everyone using the tools but poison the culture. The middle path that experienced leaders recommend is bounded experiments with honest measurement, which is slower than a mandate and demands that managers understand the tools themselves. This sits inside Engineering culture and contrasts with AI on the resume, where the same technology appears as a career asset rather than a burden.

What to watch. Retention among senior engineers at companies with usage mandates, whether performance reviews start scoring judgement rather than tool usage, and whether the hype cycle's cadence slows enough for scepticism to relax.

Related terms

concept

AI slop

Low-effort AI-generated content that costs the reader more than it cost the sender: pitches, pull requests, code shipped by founders who cannot maintain it, drive-by contributions to open-source projects, mass-produced marketing.

8 linksconcept

Cognitive debt

Cognitive debt is the loss of memory, understanding and judgement that builds up when people accept AI output as their own work without engaging with it, and the organisational equivalent when experienced staff are removed faster than their knowledge can be replaced.

6 linksconcept

Tokenmaxxing

Tokenmaxxing is engineers inflating their AI usage, measured in tokens or tool invocations, to score well on adoption metrics rather than to do better work.

4 linksconcept

Capability overhang

A capability overhang is the gap between what already-deployed AI models can do and what their users and buyers actually get out of them.

9 linksconcept

Engineering culture

Engineering culture is how an engineering organisation actually works: the goodwill, autonomy, experimentation and internal sharing of practice that decide what gets built and how well.

needed by 10 · 17 linksconcept

AI on the resume

The expectation that engineers and engineering leaders show hands-on AI experience to stay employable, now described by senior practitioners as a mandatory career move rather than a differentiator.
