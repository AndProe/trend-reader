GLM 5.3 Flash

first seen 2026-W35 · 4 links · 3 min read

GLM 5.3 Flash is the small, efficient member of Z.ai's GLM 5.3 family. It is a mixture-of-experts model with 320 billion total parameters of which 18 billion are active per token, released under an open licence. It first appeared as an anonymous model called Ox-Alpha on a routing marketplace, where its quality drew speculation for days before Z.ai confirmed the identity and the release date.

It matters now on three counts. Capability per cost: it is described as matching a larger Anthropic model at a fraction of the active parameters. Scale: Z.ai confirmed it serving 100 trillion tokens a day on Chinese chips, evidence that domestic accelerators can run frontier-adjacent inference at volume. Risk: an uncensored derivative is being sold for offensive cyber work, and a preview claim that it reached Mythos level was later deflated. For an executive it is the current best case for an open model as a cost floor, with the usual caveats on provenance and jurisdiction.

Also seen as Ox-Alpha, Ox Alpha, GLM 5.3 Flash (Z.ai)

Deeper dive

How it works. GLM 5.3 Flash uses a sparse Mixture of experts design: 320 billion parameters in total, but only 18 billion participate in any given token, so inference cost tracks the small number while capacity tracks the large one. That ratio is what lets it match a larger dense-active closed model at much lower serving cost, and what makes it practical to run on domestic accelerators. It is released as open weights, in contrast with the flagship of the GLM 5.3 family, which carries a bespoke licence.

What happened. The model was launched in stealth. It appeared on a routing marketplace as Ox-Alpha with no attribution, accumulated usage and speculation as users noticed its quality, and was identified as Z.ai's model before the lab confirmed it and set an open-weight release date. The pattern has since been described as a playbook: gather real-world usage and word of mouth before the announcement, then reveal. Z.ai also confirmed the model serving 100 trillion tokens a day on Chinese AI chips, one of the few public volume figures for inference on non-NVIDIA hardware at that scale.

Trade-offs. Efficiency comes with caveats. A preview claim that the model reached Mythos level was deflated on closer examination, and its strengths are concentrated in the cost-sensitive middle rather than the frontier. Open weights also mean uncontrolled derivatives: an abliterated variant with safety training removed is reportedly sold for offensive cyber work, which is the kind of case that fuels regulation of open releases. Enterprise use raises the standard questions about training-data provenance, licence terms and jurisdiction, which are less settled for Chinese models than for Western ones.

What to watch. Whether the 100-trillion-token figure is sustained and independently corroborated, whether the stealth-launch playbook spreads to other labs, and whether GLM 5.3 Flash holds its position among Open weights models as the next DeepSeek and Kimi releases arrive.

Mixture of experts

Mixture of experts is a model architecture in which each token is routed to a small subset of specialised sub-networks, so a model with hundreds of billions of parameters activates only a fraction of them per step.

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.
