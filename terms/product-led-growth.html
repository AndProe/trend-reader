Product-led growth

first seen 2026-W34 · 3 links · 3 min read

Product-led growth, or PLG, means letting customers try, adopt and pay for software on their own. Instead of a sales team qualifying leads and running demos, the product offers a free tier, guides the user to value quickly, and converts them to paid plans as usage grows. The model works when onboarding is smooth enough that users succeed without help, which historically limited it to simple tools.

For an executive two developments matter. First, AI is removing the onboarding barrier: assistants inside the product can configure, explain and troubleshoot, so more complex software can grow product-led than before. Second, the free-to-paid pattern is being applied to model licensing, where open weights function as the free tier and commercial terms or revenue-share arrangements kick in above a usage threshold. The counterpoint is that for complex enterprise deployments the repeatable self-service motion is giving way to forward-deployed engineers, so PLG remains right mainly for sellers of tokens, bytes or physical goods.

Also seen as PLG, self-service growth

Deeper dive

How it works. A PLG business designs the product as the primary sales channel. A free or trial tier lowers the barrier to entry, in-product guidance drives users to a first success, usage data identifies accounts ready to expand, and pricing tiers convert them, often with a sales team engaging only once usage signals an enterprise opportunity. The economics depend on low cost to serve free users and a short time to value. AI shifts both: an embedded assistant can handle setup and questions that previously required a human, which lowers support cost and shortens onboarding, so products that once needed guided implementation can be self-served.

Why it matters now. The pattern is spreading into model distribution. Labs release open weights that function as a free tier, attract developers and derivatives, and then monetise through Revenue-share licenses or commercial terms above a threshold, which is the SaaS free-to-paid motion transplanted to the model layer. On the application side, PLG metrics such as free-to-paid conversion and expansion revenue still drive AI valuations, and self-service growth is how most AI developer tools reached scale. At the same time Enterprise AI adoption is proving harder than consumer or developer adoption, since integrating agents into a company's workflows requires bespoke work.

Trade-offs. The self-service motion breaks down when the product needs configuration to a customer's data, systems and policies, which is exactly the situation with enterprise agents. That has revived the forward-deployed engineer, whose job is to make a product work inside one customer, and it implies that the repeatable same-product motion remains right only for businesses selling commodities such as tokens, storage or hardware. PLG also exposes vendors to price competition, since self-serve buyers compare and switch easily, and free tiers of open models can undercut paid APIs. A hybrid, product-led acquisition with sales-assisted expansion, is the common compromise.

What to watch. Whether AI onboarding assistants materially raise free-to-paid conversion for complex products, how open-model licences evolve as a monetisation funnel, and whether forward-deployed engineering scales or remains a bottleneck that keeps enterprise AI sales-led.
