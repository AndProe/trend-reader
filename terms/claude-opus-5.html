Claude Opus 5

first seen 2026-W36 · needed by 1 · 5 links · 3 min read

Opus 5 was Anthropic's top general-purpose model before Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 arrived. It carried the Opus line's long-standing pricing of $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output, and was the model many enterprises standardised on for demanding tasks through mid-2026. It has moved from flagship to baseline.

That baseline role is why the term still appears everywhere. Reviews of GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 3.8 Flash and Opus 5.5 use it as the yardstick for price, speed and benchmark scores, and research papers cite it as the closed-model reference on their leaderboards. For an executive the practical questions are about migration: Opus 5.5 offers similar or better capability at 20 percent lower list price and much cheaper cache reads, so contracts and workloads pinned to Opus 5 should be reviewed. Some users also want Opus 5, rather than the older Opus 4.8, as the fallback model when safety classifiers fire.

Also seen as Opus 5

Deeper dive

What it is. Opus 5 is the fifth generation of Anthropic's largest model tier. It held the Opus line's unchanged pricing of $5 and $25 per million tokens, a stability that ended only when Claude Opus 5.5 launched at $4 and $20 on 22 September 2026. Through mid-2026 it was the closed-model comparator of choice: pre-deployment evaluations of later Anthropic models, benchmark reviews of GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 3.8 Flash, and academic papers on agent harnesses and refusal behaviour all use it as the reference row in their tables.

Why it matters now. Three events in September moved Opus 5 from current to reference. Claude Fable 5.1 took over as the broadly available frontier model on 1 September. Opus 5.5 then arrived priced below the line that Opus 4.5 and Opus 5 had held, with Anthropic claiming lower cost than Opus 5 and quality closer to Fable 5.1. And the Classifier fallback debate around Fable 5.1 raised Opus 5 as the preferred fallback: when a safety classifier fires, requests currently drop to Opus 4.8, and users have asked for the option to fall back to Opus 5 instead, since it is both more capable and more resistant to prompt injection.

Trade-offs. Staying on Opus 5 offers stability, well-understood behaviour and an established evaluation record, which matters for regulated workloads where re-qualifying a model is expensive. Moving to Opus 5.5 offers lower cost and faster output, but the newer model's system card is disputed on cyber classification and its chain-of-thought is less controllable, so the migration is not free of risk. Papers that anchor on Opus 5 will age quickly as the comparison set moves on.

What to watch. How long Anthropic keeps Opus 5 available and at what price once Opus 5.5 absorbs its workloads, whether it becomes the standard fallback model behind classifiers, and how its scores on established Benchmarks hold up as the reference row gets replaced by newer models.
