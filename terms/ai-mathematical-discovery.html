AI mathematical discovery

first seen 2026-W36 · needed by 1 · 9 links · 1 min read

Models producing new mathematical results rather than reproducing known ones: the prime-gap bounds OpenAI attributes to GPT-6 Astra, which Berman called the first model-discovered new knowledge he had seen, and the Navier-Stokes proof a week later. Berman ties it to recursive self-improvement on the grounds that a model that discovers new mathematics may discover how to improve itself, while Forward Future's daily and Willison frame it through credit attribution and verification.

Inference-time compute

Spending more computation at answer time (longer reasoning, more candidates, verification) rather than in training; OpenAI's strategic bet in Lambert's reading and the cost the arXiv verification papers try to reduce.

Navier-Stokes proof

OpenAI's announced resolution of the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the Millennium Prize Problems, produced by agents running an unreleased model more capable than GPT-6 Astra and verified in Lean with Astra, as reported by Willison and Berman.

9 linksconcept

Recursive self-improvement

AI improving AI: models regenerating their successors' training data, and, in Sottiaux's nuance, frontier models optimising the serving infrastructure and kernels they run on.

14 linksmodel

GPT-6 Astra

OpenAI's frontier model released 3 September 2026, aimed at computer use, coding and security, with multi-day goal runs and Fable-matching prices; reviewed by Berman, Willison and Latent Space in the same week.

11 linksconcept

Long-horizon goals

Agents pursuing one goal over days with billions of tokens of coherence, unlocked by Astra's goal command; the condition under which agents are most capable and, per the arXiv line, most brittle.

AI research tools

AI systems used to do research: gathering sources, running literature searches and, in the arXiv line, acting as scientist workspaces.
