Grok 4.7

first seen 2026-W38 · 8 links · 3 min read

Grok 4.7 is xAI's delayed successor to Grok 4.6, positioned for coding and knowledge work and shipped in the third week of September at unchanged pricing, $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output, with a 500K context window. Elon Musk had predicted it would reach the level of Claude Opus 5; independent assessment does not support that, placing it fifth on the Artificial Analysis index and reading it as one step off the frontier.

For an executive, the model is a good value option for coding and document-heavy work, with a large context window at a low price. The unchanged price is itself a signal: it suggests xAI has spare compute and no frontier model to fill it with, so the discount may persist but the capability gap may too. Buyers should compare cost per task against the cheap tiers from OpenAI and Google rather than assume Grok's headline price wins.

Also seen as Grok 4.7 (xAI)

Deeper dive

What it is. Grok 4.7 is xAI's coding and knowledge-work model, released in September 2026 after a delay. It keeps the Grok 4.6 price of $2 and $6 per million input and output tokens and offers a 500K context window, large enough to hold substantial codebases or document sets in a single call. The positioning is practical rather than record-setting: better at the work developers and analysts do daily, at the same bill.

Where it lands. Musk predicted the model would reach the level of Claude Opus 5. On the Artificial Analysis index it places fifth, behind the current OpenAI, Anthropic and Google leaders, and the independent reading is that it sits one step off the frontier rather than on it. Against its predecessor it improves on GDPval and legal benchmarks while holding price and speed. Since the gains come from Post-training on coding data from the Cursor-xAI acquisition and refined reasoning, rather than a larger base model, the ceiling is set by the underlying Grok 4 pretraining.

Why the price did not move. A lab that holds price on a new release is either confident in demand or short of it. The reading here is the latter: xAI has more compute than frontier-grade demand, so it keeps the discount to fill capacity. That makes Grok 4.7 a strong entry in Frontier model pricing comparisons on paper, but the correct measure is Cost per task: a cheaper model that needs more retries or higher Reasoning effort levels can cost more per completed job than a pricier one at low effort.

What to watch. Whether a Grok 5 base model arrives and closes the gap Musk promised. Whether the 500K window proves usable in practice or degrades on long inputs as many large windows do. And whether xAI's discount survives once its compute is fully subscribed.
