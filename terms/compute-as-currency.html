Compute as currency

first seen 2026-W33 · 10 links · 3 min read

Open-weight models have made frontier-adjacent capability available to anyone, which invites the conclusion that everyone gets superintelligence and no one has an edge. The compute-as-currency view rejects that. If models are interchangeable, the constraint moves to what runs them: GPUs, data centres and electricity. Whoever holds compute holds the advantage, and compute behaves less like a utility bought by the hour and more like money, something to be traded, lent and priced against future returns.

The evidence is in how compute deals are now structured. Nvidia offers GPU access to startups in exchange for a share of future revenue, on the premise that startups are short of compute rather than capital. Meta's pricing is read as trading inference for training tokens rather than selling it as a utility. For an executive this means two things: compute access is a strategic asset to be secured on long terms, not a line item, and the vendor relationships that grant it, including who finances what, are becoming as important as the model choice.

Also seen as compute as the real balance of power, compute is the moat

Deeper dive

How it works. In a market where models are plentiful, the scarce factor of production is the hardware and power needed to run them. Scarcity gives compute the properties of a currency: it holds value, it is accepted in exchange, and it can be lent at a return. Three transaction types show this. Compute is exchanged for equity or revenue share, as in Nvidia's programme of granting GPU access against a slice of a startup's future revenue. It is exchanged for data or training signal, as in the reading of Meta's pricing that treats inference served to users as payment for the training tokens those users generate. And it is financed, through Vendor financing arrangements in which the chip vendor helps fund the buyer.

Why it matters now. The argument arose as a rebuttal to optimism about Open weights models: releasing weights spreads capability, but not the ability to serve it at scale, so power concentrates with the Hyperscalers and chip vendors rather than diffusing. The advertising model for prompts, where inference is subsidised by ads and paid for in data, is the consumer version of the same exchange and a step toward vertical integration. Measured through Revenue per gigawatt, the productivity of compute becomes the metric that matters, and Complementary assets theory says that compute, as the scarcest complement, captures the value that model inventors cannot.

Trade-offs. Treating compute as currency favours incumbents with balance sheets and grid connections, and makes startups dependent on the terms their compute suppliers set. Revenue-share arrangements dilute future upside. And the view assumes compute stays scarce: rapid efficiency gains, custom silicon and new capacity could all lower the price and weaken the currency. Two-tier data pricing, where data is priced differently for those who bring compute, is one early sign of how the exchange rates may be set.

What to watch. The spread of revenue-share compute deals beyond Nvidia, whether hyperscalers begin to denominate contracts in compute units rather than dollars, and whether energy rather than chips becomes the binding constraint, which would shift the currency once more.

GPU access

Complementary assets

Complementary assets are the scarce resources, chiefly compute, data and distribution, that end up capturing most of the value of a general-purpose technology instead of the people who invented it.

4 linksconcept

Hyperscalers

Hyperscalers are the largest cloud providers, AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, together with Oracle and increasingly the frontier labs themselves, that buy most AI accelerators, issue most AI-related debt and build the gigawatt campuses.

needed by 1 · 16 linksmetric

Revenue per gigawatt

Revenue per gigawatt is the annual revenue an AI operator earns per unit of electrical capacity it has built out, with power rather than chips or servers as the denominator.

8 linksconcept

Vendor financing

Vendor financing is Nvidia using its own balance sheet to sustain demand for its chips, by investing in labs and neoclouds, guaranteeing leases and revenue floors, extending payment terms and financing data centres directly.

9 linksconcept

Two-tier data pricing

Two-tier data pricing is selling the same model at two prices: a standard tier where the vendor promises not to train on prompts and completions, and a much cheaper contributor tier where the customer grants training rights.
