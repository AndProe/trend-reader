Compute as currency

first seen 2026-W33 · 10 links · 1 min read

The view that once models commoditise, access to compute and energy rather than model access decides advantage, and that inference can be traded for training tokens rather than sold as a utility. Berman's rebuttal to open-weights optimism and Tunguz's reading of Meta's pricing.

Also seen as compute as the real balance of power, compute is the moat

How the sources use it

  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-03) the idea that inference is traded for training tokens rather than sold as a utility.
  • Gaurav Sharma (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-01) GPU access traded for a share of future revenue; the essay's premise is that compute, not capital, is what startups are short of.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-08-11) Berman's central counter-argument: once models commoditize via open weights, compute (and energy) access, not model access, determines advantage, undermining the essay's "everyone gets super intelligence" premise.

Complementary assets

Catalini's term for the scarce resources (compute, data, distribution) that capture a general-purpose technology's value instead of its inventors, used to argue that no single lab will be the only company left.

4 linksconcept

Hyperscalers

The largest cloud providers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, plus Oracle and increasingly the labs themselves) that buy most accelerators, issue most AI debt and grow 37 to 82 percent a year; their decelerating share of Nvidia's growth is a tracked signal.

needed by 1 · 16 linksmetric

Revenue per gigawatt

Revenue earned per unit of power capacity: Tunguz's $50 million per megawatt against $10 to 15 million of cost, SemiAnalysis's $100 billion-plus per gigawatt-year for frontier APIs, Nvidia's metric for owning more of each AI factory.

8 linksconcept

Vendor financing

Nvidia extending its balance sheet to sustain demand for its own chips: investing in labs and neoclouds, financing data centres directly, financing SpaceX's buildout.

9 linksconcept

Two-tier data pricing

Selling the same model at two prices: a Standard tier on which the lab promises never to train on prompts and completions, and a Contributor tier about 92 to 95 percent cheaper in exchange for training rights, as Meta did with Muse Spark 1.3 at $1.25/$4.25 against $0.10/$0.20 per million tokens.
