AI slop

first seen 2026-W33 · 8 links · 3 min read

AI slop is what happens when generation becomes free and attention does not. A pitch, a pull request, a report or a piece of code that took seconds to produce lands on a person who must spend minutes or hours evaluating it, and most of it is not worth the time. The defining property is the asymmetry: the sender's cost falls to nothing while the reader's cost stays fixed or rises, so the rational response of recipients is to stop reading.

The consequences are visible. Leading open-source projects have closed contributions or moved to invitation-only models because thousands of drive-by AI pull requests overwhelmed maintainers. Engineering teams report slop pull requests from product and design colleagues, founders ship AI-written code that nobody can maintain, and podcasts and publishers stop responding to pitches. The same pattern has reached state media, where AI-animated propaganda travels far for nothing. For an executive the issues are which channels to protect with friction, and whether the company's own output is becoming slop.

Also seen as slop, AI-generated slop, slop PRs, founder slop

Deeper dive

How it works. Generative models produce fluent output at near-zero marginal cost, and by default they produce work very similar to themselves: plausible, generic, and unweighted by any judgement about whether it was worth sending. When that output enters a channel designed for scarce, effortful contributions, such as an open-source review queue, an editor's inbox or a code base, the channel's implicit filter, that the sender bothered, stops working. Code slop has a specific form: agent-written code in languages such as Rust that compiles and passes tests but that no human on the team can maintain, and code shipped by non-engineers through Vibe coding that carries the same debt. The detection problem is hard enough that the most prominent demonstration of one new model in September was a tool for finding machine-written text; see AI text detection.

Why it matters now. The channels are closing. Top open-source projects moved to Closed contributions after being flooded, which cuts off the path by which new contributors used to join. Reviewers of prose and code develop the exhaustion described under AI fatigue. And the sameness of generated output, whether text or interface, becomes a recognisable signal of low effort, which is why AI design homogeneity is the visual counterpart. Some organisations respond with a No-AI writing policy for internal communication, trading speed for the assurance that a human thought about what was sent.

Trade-offs. Friction defends channels but excludes legitimate newcomers and slows real contributors. Banning AI output loses the genuine gains of drafting with a model. Selection is the better lever: the same tools can produce excellent work when a person selects hard against the default, which the best practitioners describe as the actual skill. The costs of slop are diffuse and the benefits to senders concentrated, which is why it persists.

What to watch. Whether detection tools become reliable enough to filter at scale, whether contribution models for open source stabilise, and whether reputational costs for slop start to bite on the senders.

Related terms

concept

Closed contributions

Closed contributions is the policy of an open-source project refusing external pull requests altogether, or refusing AI-written ones, because drive-by machine-generated submissions have overwhelmed its maintainers.

4 linkstechnique

AI text detection

Tools that classify a piece of text as machine-written or human-written from its statistical properties.

4 linksconcept

AI design homogeneity

The tendency of AI-generated software, interfaces and visual output to converge on the same default look, layouts and choices, so that AI-built products become recognisable by their sameness.

3 linksconcept

AI fatigue

Burnout, loss of drive and reflexive scepticism among developers who work under mandated AI use and constant AI hype.

7 linksconcept

Vibe coding

Vibe coding is building software by prompting a coding agent and accepting what it produces without understanding the stack, vendors or architecture it chose.

13 linksconcept

No-AI writing policy

A no-AI writing policy is a publication's rule that every published sentence is written by a person, with AI permitted only for research, fact-finding and typo checks.
