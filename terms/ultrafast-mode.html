Ultrafast mode

first seen 2026-W33 · 5 links · 3 min read

Ultrafast mode is a premium tier where the vendor runs its model on hardware tuned for speed rather than throughput and charges more for the result. OpenAI's version, backed by Cerebras systems, runs GPT-5.6 Sol roughly 14 to 15 times faster at added cost; Chinese vendors including Xiaomi and Z.ai sell comparable high-speed tiers built on GPU runtime engineering. Inside OpenAI the tier is reserved mostly for external customers and used internally for incident response.

For an executive, the tier answers a question that hardware vendors had only argued in theory: whether latency is worth money. It is, in specific cases. The tier works best on generation-heavy workloads such as drafting and code, and less well on tool-call-heavy agents where the model waits on external systems. Buyers should reserve it for user-facing interactive flows and time-critical operations, and keep bulk work on standard pricing.

Also seen as fast mode, ChatGPT Ultrafast, high-speed tier, UltraSpeed, HighSpeed

Deeper dive

How it works. Standard inference maximises throughput by batching many requests across GPUs, which keeps hardware busy but adds queuing and per-token delay. A fast tier reverses the priority. Wafer-scale systems keep model weights in on-chip memory and remove the memory-bandwidth bottleneck of decode; GPU-side approaches such as persistent runtimes keep kernels resident and cut dispatch overhead to reach similar interactivity on commodity hardware. The tier is the Interactivity versus throughput trade-off sold as a product: fewer tokens per second per dollar of hardware, but far fewer milliseconds per token for the user paying for it.

Why it matters now. OpenAI's Cerebras-powered Ultrafast for GPT-5.6 Sol, priced above standard inference at roughly 14 to 15 times the speed, is the market signal that a segment of demand values latency over cost. Reports from inside the lab describe it as reserved mostly for external customers, used internally for incident response and most effective on generation-heavy rather than tool-call-heavy work. Chinese vendors selling high-speed tiers on GPU runtimes show the pattern is not tied to one hardware supplier. Together with Ultra-low latency demands from voice, games and real-time agents, fast tiers give inference hardware makers a product to sell into rather than a benchmark to argue about.

Trade-offs. The tier costs more per token and depends on scarce specialised capacity, so it cannot serve all traffic. Its advantage shrinks for agentic workloads dominated by tool latency and for reasoning modes that generate long chains regardless of speed. Committing interactive products to a fast tier creates dependency on a single vendor's hardware partnership. And a model that answers faster does not answer better; the tier changes experience, not capability.

What to watch. Whether fast tiers spread across Frontier model pricing as a standard option, how GPT-5.6 Sol and successors are positioned across speed tiers, and whether GPU runtime engineering closes the gap with wafer-scale hardware enough to make the premium fall.
