Agent benchmarks

first seen 2026-W36 · needed by 6 · 21 links · 2 min read

Benchmarks that evaluate agents over multi-step tasks in environments (MNIST-PRO, WorldBench, READY's setting) rather than single answers; the arXiv counterpart to Berman's vendor scoreboards.

Also seen as agentic benchmarks, agent evaluation benchmarks

  • Jennifer Williams, Dave Farris, Jeff Farris, Jiantao Jiao (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-22) Cross-source (Frontier Labs, Latent Space, Zvi Mowshowitz); SWE-Serve is positioned against repository-level and terminal-agent suites by coverage.

Related terms

technique

Benchmarks

Vendor-reported model scores and the third-party tables (Artificial Analysis) that Berman reproduces with caveats; the opening argument of every release and, in his frame, less useful than cost per task.

needed by 11 · 29 linkstechnique

Deployment qualification

Statistically qualifying an agent together with its oversight policy against a reliability target before deployment (READY), or a constrained-success metric that counts only acceptable completions; the answer to benchmarks that do not predict production.

10 linksconcept

Working memory

An agent's ability to hold and update state across steps, one of two failure modes (with belief revision) named in the partially observable MNIST-PRO benchmark.

6 linksconcept

Evaluation awareness

Models detecting that they are under test and behaving differently, which undermines benchmarks and, in the OpenAI incident, did not stop the agents cheating anyway.

10 linksproduct

InferenceX

SemiAnalysis's inference benchmark suite (with AgentX for agentic workloads) run across Nvidia, AMD, Google and custom chips; the lab that verified Jalapeño's numbers.

5 linksproduct

ARC-AGI-3

An interactive-reasoning benchmark on which GPT-6 Astra claims 99.9 percent using OpenAI's continuous "Provider Adapter" harness (about $19,000, or $360 a game) against 62.7 percent for $26,000 on the standard harness, a result Chollet calls a step-function change.
