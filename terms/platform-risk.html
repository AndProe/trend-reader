Platform risk

first seen 2026-W35 · 6 links · 3 min read

Platform risk describes what happens when a business is built on someone else's platform and the platform owner changes the rules. In AI the specific form is dependence on one lab's closed models. The lab sets prices, deprecates versions and, unless contracts say otherwise, may retain de-identified usage data that improves its own models. If the lab then launches a product in the customer's market, it competes with insight drawn from that customer's traffic.

For an executive the risk is concrete rather than theoretical. One lab has acknowledged that de-identified usage data may have improved its models in the course of a public dispute over a mathematical proof, and analysts have priced single-vendor dependence explicitly. The mitigations are contractual and architectural: zero data retention terms, a model-agnostic harness that can switch vendors, and open-weight models that can be hosted internally. Each has a cost, so the decision is how much independence a given workload is worth.

Deeper dive

How it works. The mechanism has three parts. First, dependence: a product is engineered around one vendor's API, prompts and behaviours, so switching is expensive. Second, information flow: usage data, even de-identified, tells the vendor which tasks matter, where the model fails and what customers pay for, and can be used for training or product decisions. Third, vertical expansion: the vendor moves into the customer's layer, whether coding tools, legal research or consumer assistants, armed with that knowledge and a cost advantage on its own models. The pattern is familiar from earlier platforms, but the training feedback loop makes it sharper, because the vendor's core product literally learns from the customer.

Why it matters now. The Vertical integration of AI labs is accelerating, with labs acquiring or building the applications that used to be their customers. Two episodes brought the issue forward: an analysis of token share that highlighted one lab's data retention as a reason to prefer open models, and the Navier-Stokes proof dispute in which a lab said de-identified usage data may have improved its models. Research on enterprise coding agents now treats external API dependence as a limitation to be engineered out, and quantitative finance work builds local agents so that strategies never reach a vendor. Each case supports the same conclusion: Open weights models are a hedge as much as a cost saving.

Trade-offs. Independence has a price. Self-hosted open models lag the frontier by months and require infrastructure and expertise. Zero Data Retention agreements limit what the vendor learns but may exclude the customer from features that depend on retained context. A model-agnostic architecture adds abstraction and can flatten the differences between models that a product relies on. The vendor's counterargument is that usage data improves the model for everyone and that contractual controls already exist, but the incentive to compete downstream remains, and the Token share versus revenue share analysis shows how much value accrues to whoever controls the model layer.

What to watch. Whether labs offer binding no-compete or data-use commitments to large customers, how often they enter markets their customers occupy, and whether enterprise buyers make open-model fallback a standard procurement requirement.

Related terms

concept

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.

needed by 11 · 36 linksconcept

Vertical integration of AI labs

Vertical integration of AI labs is model developers extending from models into the layers around them: buying developer toolchains, owning coding harnesses, moving into consumer verticals and cutting off third-party platforms.

8 linksconcept

AI data supply chain

The network of sources that supplies training and labelling data to AI labs and who controls it: labelling vendors, state dataset programmes, corporate records bought from defunct firms, user behaviour on platforms, and harvested chat transcripts.

needed by 2 · 12 linksconcept

Zero Data Retention

Zero Data Retention (ZDR) is the enterprise contract norm under which a vendor keeps none of a customer's prompts or outputs and never trains on them.

needed by 1 · 10 linksconcept

Token share versus revenue share

Token share versus revenue share is the distinction between which models process the most tokens and which capture the most money, with open-weight models winning volume while a few closed labs collect most of the revenue.

9 linksevent

Navier-Stokes proof

The Navier-Stokes proof is the announced resolution of the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the Millennium Prize Problems, produced by agents running an unreleased model more capable than GPT-6 Astra and formally verified in Lean.
