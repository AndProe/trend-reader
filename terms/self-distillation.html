Self-distillation

Distillation normally means a smaller model learning from a larger one's outputs. Self-distillation removes the larger model: the system generates its own training signal, filters it, and trains on what survives. This sounds circular, and whether it works depends entirely on whether the filter adds information the generator did not have — a verifier, an execution result, a majority vote across samples, or a representation available in one modality but not another.

Its commercial significance is about access rather than efficiency. When a frontier lab blocks extraction of its reasoning, or when a licence forbids using a model's outputs to train a competitor, or when an evaluation explicitly bars external teachers, self-distillation is what remains. It is therefore the technique that determines how far an organisation can improve a model using only what it already has, which makes it the quiet dependency behind both autonomous-research results and the question of how much the open ecosystem relies on frontier outputs.

Deeper dive

How it works. The pattern is generate, filter, train. A model produces many candidate outputs; some signal separates better from worse — execution success, a solver, agreement across samples, or a stronger internal representation — and the survivors become supervised training data. Where the filter is a genuine verifier the gain is real; where it is only the model's own preference the procedure can amplify existing biases, which is why the technique is usually paired with an independent check. One variant distils a representation rather than an output, aligning text embeddings to visual ones so that capability available with an image becomes available from a description.

Why it matters now. Autonomous post-training results depend on it: agents given a raw base model, public data and no external teacher reached parity with an officially tuned release by combining supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, cross-checkpoint weight merging and rounds of self-distillation. Separately, the claim that open models depend heavily on frontier outputs — and would fall back if extraction defences hold — is a claim about whether self-distillation is a sufficient substitute. The same technique therefore underwrites both Recursive self-improvement measurement and the strategic question of distillation dependence.

Trade-offs. Without an external information source the gains are bounded, which is what Lossy self-improvement names: recombining what a model already contains improves its expression of that content rather than extending it. Repeated rounds risk narrowing diversity and entrenching errors the filter does not catch. Compute cost is also front-loaded into generation, which makes the technique expensive relative to simply using a better teacher where one is available.

What to watch. Whether published self-distillation gains survive on held-out suites the generating system never saw, whether the filters used are genuine verifiers or model preference, and whether open-weight model quality holds if frontier extraction defences continue to tighten.

Related terms

technique

Post-training

Post-training is everything done to a model after its initial pretraining on raw text: supervised fine-tuning, preference optimisation and reinforcement learning on tasks and environments.

needed by 6 · 22 linksconcept

Lossy self-improvement

Lossy self-improvement is the thesis that AI does accelerate AI research, but that the gains leak away rather than compounding into a runaway, because automatable research is narrow, parallel agents show diminishing returns, and compute, capital and politics govern what gets built.

8 linksconcept

Recursive self-improvement

Recursive self-improvement is the use of AI systems to accelerate the research, data, code and infrastructure that produce the next AI system, so that each generation shortens the time to the one after it.

17 linksconcept

Reasoning traces

Reasoning traces are the recorded step-by-step thinking a model produces on its way to an answer, whether shown to the user, hidden, or summarised.

needed by 2 · 14 linksconcept

Vision-language models

Vision-language models are models that take images and text together and reason over both, serving as the perception layer for computer-use agents that read screens and as the basis of visually grounded reasoning research.

10 linkstechnique

Distillation attacks

A distillation attack is the training of a model on a rival's outputs in order to copy its capability without its cost.
