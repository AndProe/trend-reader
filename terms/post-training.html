Post-training

first seen 2026-W33 · needed by 4 · 17 links · 3 min read

Post-training is the second half of building a model. Pretraining teaches a model language and general knowledge by predicting text; post-training shapes it into something useful by fine-tuning on demonstrations, adjusting it toward preferred answers, and running reinforcement learning on tasks with verifiable outcomes such as code that passes tests or maths that checks out. Modern recipes chain these stages, often with a mid-training phase in between, and reinforcement learning on a growing set of environments has become the main engine of progress.

For an executive post-training is where an AI budget can create proprietary advantage. Fine-tuning an open base on internal data is the route large customers take past list prices, and companies now post-train open models on their own data routinely. It is also where risks arise: the wrong objective produces models that give up early, lose diversity of behaviour or game their reward. The build-or-buy question for AI has largely become a post-train-or-subscribe question.

Also seen as post-training data, post-training ecosystem, post-training scaling, fine-tuning

Deeper dive

How it works. A typical recipe runs supervised fine-tuning on curated demonstrations to establish format and basic behaviour, then some form of preference optimisation, then reinforcement learning against verifiable rewards or learned judges. Group-based policy optimisation methods such as GRPO have become the workhorse for the reinforcement stage, and the design of Reinforcement learning environments rather than the choice of algorithm is now the main variable. Frontier recipes are organised as many sub-teams contributing data and environments, with mid-training stages to inject domain knowledge before the reinforcement phase. Newer task types such as RLCD, which trains calibrated decisions rather than text generation, sit alongside human-preference and verifiable-reward training as distinct objectives.

Why it matters now. Most recent capability progress is attributed to reinforcement learning after pretraining, and post-training data is the input that drives it now that the public web is exhausted. That has created the RL data industry, with vendors selling environments and trajectories to labs on both sides of the US-China divide. Open models have made post-training the centre of academic and enterprise activity, since fine-tuning a published checkpoint is far cheaper than pretraining one, and the same accessibility underlies Distillation attacks on closed models. Papers report that the ordering and interleaving of stages, or the composition of the instruction set, matter more than new objectives, and that a distillation stage often buys schema compliance rather than capability.

Trade-offs. Post-training can remove as well as add. Studies locate the loss of behavioural variance at the first supervised stage and find later stages do not restore it, which matters for any use of models to simulate populations. Reinforcement objectives create side effects, such as length penalties that cause a model to give up early, and generative safety guards can inherit objective flaws from their training. The forgetting problem, where new training erodes earlier capabilities, constrains Continual learning and is one reason analysts doubt that models can automate their own post-training soon.

What to watch. Whether environment design becomes a standardised, tradable input, how much of frontier progress remains attributable to post-training versus test-time compute, and whether enterprises keep the post-training loop in-house or outsource it back to the labs.

Related terms

Reinforcement learning environments

Reinforcement learning environments are synthesised, verifiable task settings, such as sandboxed code repositories, simulated devices or games, in which an agent acts, receives a graded reward and is trained on the result.

Reasoning traces

Reasoning traces are the recorded step-by-step thinking a model produces on its way to an answer, whether shown to the user, hidden, or summarised.

needed by 2 · 9 linksconcept

Data labeling

Data labeling is the human annotation of training examples, an industry of roughly ten billion dollars that supplies models with judgements they cannot yet make for themselves.

needed by 1 · 8 linkstechnique

Distillation attacks

A distillation attack is the training of a model on a rival's outputs in order to copy its capability without its cost.

9 linksconcept

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.

needed by 11 · 36 linksconcept

RL data industry

The RL data industry is the market of vendors that build and sell reinforcement-learning environments, task suites and agent trajectories to model labs for post-training.
