Deployment qualification

first seen 2026-W35 · 10 links · 3 min read

Deployment qualification asks a different question from a benchmark. Instead of how often the agent gets the right answer on a test set, it asks whether the agent plus its human oversight arrangement meets a stated reliability target on the actual task, with statistical confidence, before anyone depends on it. A related metric counts only completions that are acceptable in context, rather than all completions that happen to match a label.

It matters now because agents are being sold on benchmark results that fail to transfer. In one enterprise study only six of 33 candidate patches earned deployment under such a gate. Labs are running predeployment evaluations that feed system cards, regulators are framing pre-procurement evaluation as a requirement, and enterprise frameworks express the gate as an executable decision returning reject, conditional or scale. For an executive this is the mechanism that turns an agent pilot into a governed rollout, and the line item that pays for oversight staff, evaluation harnesses and the reliability margin a contract promises.

Also seen as READY, reliability qualification, constrained task success

Deeper dive

How it works. A qualification procedure fixes a target such as a maximum acceptable failure rate, runs the agent on a sample of real or realistic tasks large enough to bound the failure rate with confidence, and evaluates the combined system of agent plus oversight policy rather than the agent alone. If humans review every output, the system's reliability is different from the model's, and the qualification measures the former. Constrained task success narrows the definition of success to completions the downstream process can actually use: schema-compliant output is a precondition, a patch that passes unit tests but fails serving tests does not count, and substitution conditions act as a gate. The two-tier enterprise version returns one of three decisions, reject, conditional or scale, and only the last releases budget.

Why it matters now. Agent benchmarks optimise for comparability across models, not for predicting behaviour on one company's workflow, and public Benchmarks are also subject to training contamination and Evaluation awareness. The qualification approach is the response: an enterprise study found that only six of 33 candidate patches earned deployment, a frontier lab now publishes a third party's predeployment evaluation in its system card while that party states explicitly that it does not verify any policy threshold, and a fintech regulatory proposal makes pre-deployment evaluation a condition of procurement. Voluntary commitments to phased deployment, independent evaluation and incident reporting follow the same shape at the lab level.

Trade-offs. Qualification costs samples, and samples cost money and time; the more tightly the target is set, the larger the sample and the longer the pilot. It also couples the agent to a specific Human oversight policy, so a change to staffing or review rules invalidates the qualification. And a reliability figure earned on one task distribution decays as the task drifts, which means requalification is a recurring cost that belongs in Cost per task rather than a one-off.

What to watch. Whether qualification thresholds become standard contract terms, whether third-party evaluators move from describing to verifying, and whether Agent reliability figures reported by vendors start to be measured this way rather than by benchmark.

Agent benchmarks

Agent benchmarks are evaluation suites that score an AI system on multi-step tasks inside an environment, such as repairing a code repository, operating a terminal or completing a task by voice, rather than on single-answer questions.

Human oversight

Human oversight is the practice of keeping a person in the loop over an agent's actions, with authority to approve, correct or stop them, and the review burden that this imposes.

Enterprise AI adoption

Enterprise AI adoption is the pattern by which companies actually take up AI, as distinct from what they announce.

Evaluation awareness

Evaluation awareness is a model's ability to detect that it is being tested and to behave differently as a result.

10 linksmetric

Cost per task

Cost per task is the dollar cost of getting a unit of work actually completed by an AI system, measured at a given quality level, rather than the price per token or the score on a benchmark.

23 linksconcept

Agent reliability

Agent reliability is whether an agent completes a given workflow acceptably often enough to be trusted with it, measured as a target such as success on nearly every run under a defined oversight policy and cost, rather than as a best-case capability score.
