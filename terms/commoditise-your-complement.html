Commoditise your complement

first seen 2026-W34 · 8 links · 3 min read

Every product has complements, the things customers buy alongside it. If the complement gets cheaper, demand for your product rises, so a company with a strong position in one layer has an incentive to drive the price of the neighbouring layer toward zero. In AI the model itself has become the complement that several large players want commoditised. Nvidia releases open models and training data because cheap, abundant models sell more GPUs. Meta pushes tokens into the market at prices that hurt companies whose business is selling tokens. OpenAI's leadership describes a version of the same logic from the middle: partners above it commoditise the model, while OpenAI works to commoditise the chip below it.

For an executive this is the lens for reading any free or open release. The question is not why a company is being generous but which layer it is trying to protect. It also warns that a supplier whose product is someone else's complement, such as a pure model vendor, faces structural price pressure regardless of how good the model is.

Also seen as commoditize the complement, flooding the zone with tokens

Deeper dive

How it works. Demand for a product and demand for its complements move together, and the sum of what a customer will pay is roughly fixed. If a company can push the complement's price down, more of the customer's budget flows to its own layer. The classic examples were operating systems and hardware; the AI version has models, chips, data and distribution as the layers. The strategy only works for a company with a defensible position in the layer it is protecting, since otherwise it is simply giving things away.

Why it matters now. Open weights models are the visible form. Nvidia's Nemotron models and released datasets make capable models cheaper for everyone, which raises demand for the accelerators Nvidia sells; the same logic underlies its Revenue-share licenses and Vendor financing arrangements, which put compute in customers' hands on terms that grow hardware demand. Meta's release strategy is read as flooding the zone with tokens to weaken token-sellers whose revenue depends on scarce access to frontier models. And the interview framing from OpenAI's president, who does not adopt the word but describes the shape, puts the lab in the middle: the application partners above it want the model commoditised, and OpenAI wants the chip commoditised, so it pursues custom silicon and multiple suppliers. The Capability overhang, where models have improved past what customers will pay extra for, makes the model layer easier to commoditise than it was a year ago.

Trade-offs. The strategy is cheap for the company that owns the protected layer and expensive for everyone whose product is the complement. Open releases also lose control of the artefact, which can be fine-tuned by competitors or adversaries. And the layer being protected can itself become a complement to something further up the stack, so the game repeats; the counter-theory of Complementary assets holds that value ends up with whoever controls the scarcest input, which is not always the one doing the commoditising.

What to watch. Whether Nvidia's open models reach frontier quality, whether Meta sustains token pricing below cost, and whether OpenAI's chip programme succeeds in making the accelerator the commodity, which would reverse the current balance of power with its hardware supplier.

Related terms

concept

Open weights models

Open weights models are AI models whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download, run and modify, in contrast to closed models reachable only through a vendor's API.

needed by 11 · 36 linksconcept

Vendor financing

Vendor financing is Nvidia using its own balance sheet to sustain demand for its chips, by investing in labs and neoclouds, guaranteeing leases and revenue floors, extending payment terms and financing data centres directly.

9 linksmodel

Nemotron

Nemotron is Nvidia's family of open-weights models, ranging from a single-GPU variant to a flagship of over a trillion parameters, backed by a reported 26-billion-dollar programme.

3 linksconcept

Revenue-share licenses

Revenue-share licences are open-model licences that permit free download, experimentation and small-scale commercial use but require a commercial agreement, security review or revenue share once the deployer passes a stated revenue threshold.

7 linksconcept

Open-closed capability gap

The open-closed capability gap is the measured lag between the best openly downloadable model and the best closed frontier model, usually expressed as the number of months it takes open models to reach a given capability level.

15 linksconcept

Capability overhang

A capability overhang is the gap between what already-deployed AI models can do and what their users and buyers actually get out of them.
