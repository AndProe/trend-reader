Token efficiency

first seen 2026-W34 · 9 links · 3 min read

Token efficiency asks how much work a model gets done per token. Two models can rank similarly on capability while one produces far more output to reach the same answer, and agentic workloads consume roughly thirty input tokens for every output token, so the total bill depends on verbosity, context handling and reasoning effort as much as on the price per million tokens.

For an executive, this is the metric that turns a per-token price into a cost per task. Vendors now trade it explicitly: higher reasoning effort buys score at the cost of more tokens per task, and several launches lead with token reductions on video understanding and execution rather than accuracy gains. Enterprise buyers comparing models should compare tokens per completed task, with caching and input pricing weighted heavily, and should expect effort settings to be a hidden driver of budget overruns.

Also seen as input-to-output token ratio, verbosity, tokens per task

Deeper dive

What it measures. Token efficiency has several readings that move independently. Verbosity is output tokens per task, and a model's rank on it can diverge from its rank on intelligence; intelligence density is the informal name for capability divided by tokens. Workload mix is the input-to-output ratio, around 30 to 1 for agentic work, which makes input pricing and Cache pricing the dominant cost levers. Training-side efficiency is capability per training token, a separate lever reported for some open models. Research on harness design, skill compilation and length control reports efficiency gains alongside accuracy, with claimed reductions of 38 to 89 percent in execution tokens in one compilation approach and up to 88 percent fewer tokens per video task in a lab release.

Why it matters now. The frontier price war has made list prices converge, so differences in tokens per task become the differentiator. Reasoning models complicate the picture: Reasoning tokens and effort levels let a vendor concede more tokens for higher scores, and reports on one model's efficiency have been mixed because effort settings and possible bugs inflate output. One release's claimed efficiency was set against a maximum-effort run that exhausted a 128,000-token output limit, which illustrates how far the spread can run. The result is that Cost per task rather than price per token has become the buyer's comparison unit, and agentic products are marketed on it.

Trade-offs. Cutting tokens can cut quality; difficulty-aware length control tries to spend reasoning only where it helps. Sparse architectures such as Mixture of experts improve compute per token but not necessarily tokens per task. Efficiency measured on a benchmark may not transfer to a customer's workload, where tool outputs and retrieved context dominate input.

What to watch. Standard reporting of tokens per task alongside scores, effort defaults in vendor APIs, and whether Agentic inference workloads keep the input-heavy ratio as caching and compaction improve.

Related terms

metric

Cost per task

Cost per task is the dollar cost of getting a unit of work actually completed by an AI system, measured at a given quality level, rather than the price per token or the score on a benchmark.

23 linksconcept

Reasoning tokens

Reasoning tokens are the internal deliberation a model generates before producing its visible answer, billed and counted like any other output.

6 linksmetric

Compute efficiency

Compute efficiency is the amount of useful capability delivered per unit of compute, combining gains on the model side, such as fewer active parameters and cheaper inference, with gains on the chip side.

6 linkstechnique

Mixture of experts

Mixture of experts is a model architecture in which each token is routed to a small subset of specialised sub-networks, so a model with hundreds of billions of parameters activates only a fraction of them per step.

7 linksconcept

Agentic inference workloads

Agentic inference workloads are the production traffic pattern created by coding and computer-use agents: many turns per task, context that accumulates toward a million tokens, very high reuse of shared prefixes, and bursts of parallel sub-agent calls.

needed by 1 · 16 linksconcept

Cache pricing

Cache pricing is the rate charged for reading prompt tokens that a model provider has already processed and stored, as opposed to the full price for fresh input tokens.
