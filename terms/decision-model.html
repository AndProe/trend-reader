Decision model

Most of what an AI system does internally is not writing — it is deciding. Should this request go to the expensive model or the cheap one? Does this output violate policy? Should this incident be escalated? A language model can answer each of these, but it answers by generating tokens, which means paying for a full forward pass and a text response when what the calling system needs is a typed value and a confidence number.

A decision model is the specialisation that follows. It takes the same pretrained understanding and replaces the generative head with one that returns probabilities over the available choices. The commercial claim is a step change in speed and cost rather than in intelligence, with output often priced at zero because it is a single value. The commercial risk is calibration: a fast, cheap classifier whose confidence numbers do not track reality is worse than a slow one for exactly the escalation and guardrail decisions that make the category attractive.

Also seen as decision models, universal classifier

Deeper dive

How it works. The architecture is a tuned base model with a modified head that emits the probability distribution over a fixed option set instead of sampling tokens. Training optimises for calibrated probabilities rather than for human approval of generated text, which is the distinction RLCD names. Because the answer is a value rather than a string, the caller receives a type it can branch on, which is what makes the category suitable for system-to-system interaction rather than for conversation.

Why it matters now. Decision models displace two things at once: the long tail of bespoke classifiers that each required model selection, labelled data and a training cycle, and the generative calls currently spent on bounded choices. Early adoption has concentrated at the analytics layer and inside Smart model routing, where the decision is the product. The cost argument is strong enough that it becomes a procurement argument — a universal pretrained classifier that matches a purpose-built one removes a build step entirely.

Trade-offs. The category inherits the interpretability problem of the underlying model: a percentage is produced, and the question of why is harder to answer than for logistic regression or a decision tree whose training data can be inspected. Calibration is the measured weakness, and diagnostic work shows it does not follow from accuracy — models separated by a couple of points on aggregate scoring can differ by tens of points on controlled variations, and correct ranking of options does not imply correct answers on individual cases. There is also an unresolved question of whether a general decision model beats one trained for a specific problem.

What to watch. Whether calibration improves with architecture and data rather than remaining a jagged edge, whether decision models move from the analytics layer into agent orchestration where the decisions carry consequences, and whether the zero-priced output survives once the category has customers who depend on it.

Related terms

concept

Small language models

Small language models are models designed to be small from the start, typically from around one to fourteen billion parameters, rather than large models compressed after training.

needed by 1 · 11 linkstechnique

Smart model routing

Smart model routing sends each request to the cheapest model that can handle it, reserving frontier models for the steps that need them and often running a small local model as the dispatcher.

13 linkstechnique

Guardrails

Guardrails are the controls that limit what a model or agent may do, from safety training and output classifiers to permission checks on tool calls and actions.

15 linkstechnique

RLCD

RLCD, reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions, is a training method that teaches a model to answer many typed questions about a single input with calibrated probabilities in one pass, instead of generating text.

11 linksmetric

Calibration

Calibration is the agreement between a model's stated confidence and the long-run frequency with which predictions at that confidence turn out correct.

8 linksmodel

Jev

Jev is TypeSafe AI's decision model, released on 15 September 2026, which takes a state and a set of options and returns a typed, calibrated choice with a confidence score in a single parallel pass instead of generating text.
