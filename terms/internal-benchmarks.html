Internal benchmarks

first seen 2026-W33 · needed by 1 · 13 links · 3 min read

Internal benchmarks, usually just called evals, are the tests a company writes for its own use of AI. Where a unit test checks deterministic code, an eval checks non-deterministic model behaviour against a set of cases drawn from the company's real work, with thresholds the company sets. They are run when a new model is considered, when prompts or tools change, and increasingly on every release of an agent.

The standing advice to enterprises is to build these rather than trust vendor benchmark tables, because public scores are tuned for and do not predict performance on a specific workload. Evals are named as one of the three ingredients of a good agent harness, and they are the mechanism by which model switching becomes a cheap decision rather than a leap of faith. The budget implication is that evaluation is a product function that needs ownership, cases with known outcomes and a link to production telemetry.

Also seen as evals, private evals, company benchmarks, Box AI evals

Deeper dive

How it works. An internal benchmark is a set of cases with expected outcomes, a way to run a model or agent against them, and a scoring rule. The cases come from the organisation's own traffic: past tickets, documents, emails or decisions where the right answer is known. Scoring is exact where outputs are structured and uses an LLM-as-judge or a human panel where they are prose. Because model output is non-deterministic, results are reported as rates over repeated runs rather than pass or fail on one. The best suites are built from known outcomes rather than annotator agreement, so the ground truth is what actually happened rather than what raters believed. Tooling now exists to construct such suites as executable acceptance pipelines, and one production study followed an evolving agent through 574 runs of its own benchmark history.

Why it matters now. Public Benchmarks are the subject of deliberate tuning by labs, so their predictive value for a specific workload is weak, and enterprise studies find that the meaningful unit of evaluation is the organisation's own use case with its own thresholds. Evals also underpin Deployment qualification: whether a new model version is allowed into production is decided by the suite, not by the vendor's release notes. Even external assessors report their own task sets side by side with a lab's internal trend line, because neither alone is trusted.

Trade-offs. Building evals costs engineering time and requires someone to own case quality, and a suite that is never refreshed drifts from production reality. Judge models introduce their own bias, and cases with known outcomes are scarce for open-ended work. The expected direction is a merger of tests, evals and Observability: production telemetry feeds new cases, and evals become the regression suite for behaviour.

What to watch. Whether eval construction becomes a standard product category rather than in-house craft, and whether Verification loops inside agents reduce the need for external suites or simply move the problem inside the harness.

Related terms

technique

Observability

Observability is the practice of instrumenting software so that its behaviour in production can be understood from the telemetry it emits, and it now covers agents as well as services: wide events in place of logs and metrics, traces of hours-long agent runs, and instrumentation added by default.

needed by 2 · 8 linkstechnique

Deployment qualification

Deployment qualification is the practice of statistically qualifying an AI agent, together with the oversight policy around it, against an explicit reliability target before it is put into production.

10 linkstechnique

LLM-as-judge

LLM-as-judge is the practice of using a language model, or a panel of them, to score outputs against a rubric in place of human graders.

6 linkstechnique

Verification loops

Verification loops are pipelines in which an agent's change is automatically tested and, if it fails, retried until it passes a defined check.

needed by 3 · 14 linksproduct

SWE-bench Verified

SWE-bench Verified is the human-validated subset of SWE-bench, a benchmark in which a coding agent must resolve real GitHub issues from open-source repositories and pass the hidden tests.

14 linksconcept

Causal grader

A causal grader is a scorer that checks how an agent reached its answer and rejects a result obtained by an illegitimate route, rather than accepting anything that matches the answer key.
