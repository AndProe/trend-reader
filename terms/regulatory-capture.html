Regulatory capture

first seen 2026-W38 · 9 links · 1 min read

The argument that safety rules proposed by leading labs would entrench those labs by raising compliance costs past what smaller competitors and open-model developers can bear. It is the standing objection to pacing and licensing proposals, raised against Amodei's essay by investors and reviewers alike.

Also seen as capture, incumbent capture

How the sources use it

  • Zvi Mowshowitz (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-09-22) the charge Zvi argues against on two grounds: the labs lobbied against light-touch rules until recently, and the stocks fell when they warned.
  • Ben Thompson (Stratechery, 2026-09-21) his closing position: the safety arguments coincide with Anthropic's need for time to build a moat, and the demand arrived when OpenAI held the frontier. Cross-source (Matthew Berman, Tomasz Tunguz).
  • Zvi Mowshowitz (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-09-17) Tabarrok's lecture and the Roon, Kokotajlo and Ball rebuttals. Cross-source (Matthew Berman, Tomasz Tunguz); new to this source.
  • Zvi Mowshowitz (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-09-16) Tabarrok's stock-price argument and Burry's cartel thesis. Cross-source (Matthew Berman, Tomasz Tunguz); new to this source.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-15) his reading of Amodei's second proposal: coordination among labs under an antitrust waiver would concentrate the frontier and eventually reach open source.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-15) Berman's, LeCun's, Gurley's and Sweeney's reading of the call for regulation.
  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-14) one of five camps answering the pacing call, quoting David Sacks against the labs and Lina Khan for existing law.
  • Tomasz Tunguz (Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-14) Sacks's and Khan's objection that new rules protect incumbents.

AI regulation

Government and quasi-government rules for AI: voluntary pre-release testing frameworks, state retraining programmes, watermarking mandates and the organisations lobbying for or against them.

Open weights regulation risk

The risk that regulation sidelines open-weights models: David Sacks, relayed in Berman's daily, argues that industry-backed standards and post-release monitoring requirements would favour closed systems without any explicit ban, and Lambert expects an intentional hack using an open model to bring severe restrictions on stronger open models.

Frontier models

The strongest available models at any moment, now gated rather than openly sold and bypassed by most real usage; the tier the open models chase and the reference point for every pricing and access argument in the vault.

Embedded evaluators

Independent evaluators placed inside a frontier lab with employee-like access to models, training runs and incidents, and the right to publish what they find.
