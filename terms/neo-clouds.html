Neo clouds

first seen 2026-W16 · 8 links · 3 min read

Neo clouds are the specialist cloud companies that rent out GPUs and serve models without being one of the large general-purpose clouds. Some, such as CoreWeave, build large data centres and lease capacity to labs and enterprises; others, such as the inference providers, host open-weights models and sell tokens at competitive prices. The category has grown fast: one operator reports a 4.2-gigawatt footprint and a backlog above one hundred billion dollars, and non-hyperscaler buyers now account for the majority of new data-centre revenue.

For an executive the tier matters as both opportunity and risk. It gives enterprises bargaining power against a two-lab oligopoly, since several competing hosts serve the same open models. It also offers GPU access when hyperscaler allocation is scarce. Against that, independent audits find most neoclouds weak on security, and their financing depends on backstops from the chip vendor that make non-investment-grade operators bankable. Choosing one is a due-diligence task covering security posture, financial durability and rating.

Also seen as neoclouds, neo-clouds, GPU clouds, GPU rental providers

Deeper dive

How it works. A neocloud buys accelerators, often financed against long-term customer contracts, houses them in leased or owned data centres and rents them out by the hour or serves models on them. The pure GPU renters sell raw capacity to labs, Neolabs and enterprises; the inference providers layer serving software on top and sell tokens for Open weights models. An industry rating system for the tier assesses operators on reliability, tooling and security, and in its latest round awarded top marks to 19 of 77 reviewed operators out of 323 tracked, noting that the bar has risen. The inference providers are described as far better than the labs at the specific craft of serving, which is why labs sometimes route their own traffic through them.

Why it matters now. GPU access outside the hyperscalers is the tier's reason to exist, and it now drives the majority of net-new data-centre revenue. For buyers of open models it is the competitive market that keeps token prices low: several hosts serve the same weights, so the price of a model is set by competition rather than by the lab that trained it. Smart model routing across those hosts turns that competition into procurement leverage. The chip vendor's Vendor financing backstops make operators with weak balance sheets bankable, which accelerates build-out but ties the tier's health to one supplier.

Trade-offs. Security is the recorded weakness: audits find most neoclouds running insecure stacks, and the tier is named as a likely next target for a rogue agent swarm, since it holds both compute and other customers' models. That feeds AI cybersecurity skepticism about the whole inference supply chain. Financially, backlogs are large but customer concentration is high, and a downturn at a few labs would propagate quickly. Compared with Hyperscalers, neoclouds offer lower prices and faster access but thinner compliance and support.

What to watch. Rating-system results over time, security incidents at the tier, the durability of chip-vendor backstops, and whether hyperscalers reclaim share by matching neocloud prices for open-model inference.

Vendor financing

Vendor financing is Nvidia using its own balance sheet to sustain demand for its chips, by investing in labs and neoclouds, guaranteeing leases and revenue floors, extending payment terms and financing data centres directly.

Smart model routing

Smart model routing sends each request to the cheapest model that can handle it, reserving frontier models for the steps that need them and often running a small local model as the dispatcher.

Hyperscalers
