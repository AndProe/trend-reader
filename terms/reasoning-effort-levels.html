Reasoning effort levels

first seen 2026-W36 · 7 links · 1 min read

The effort dial (low to max) that sets how much a model thinks per request, with a 33x cost range on one prompt in Willison's test and two settings apparently identical.

Also seen as effort dial, thinking levels, low/medium/high/xhigh/max

How the sources use it

  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-22) medium beating max on Frontier Code at a fifth of the cost.
  • Simon Willison (Simon Willison, 2026-09-22) "max" on Opus 5.5 consumed the output budget in reasoning and returned nothing twice; Willison concludes he does not trust it for longer work. Recurring and cross-source.
  • Matthew Berman (Matthew Berman, 2026-09-21) a 13-point CursorBench spread between low and extra high.
  • Jaejun Shim, HyunJin Kim, Young Jin Kim et al. (arXiv cs.AI, 2026-09-17) cross-source (Simon Willison, Frontier Labs); the learned NoThink-versus-Think choice is the model-internal counterpart of the user-set effort dial.
  • Gergely Orosz (Gergely Orosz, 2026-09-10) Uber defaults to Medium effort for the best cost-to-output ratio. New for this source;.
  • Zvi Mowshowitz (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-09-05) an 11x usage range across levels.
  • Google DeepMind (Frontier Labs, 2026-09-02) the lab's own advice to drop effort levels when the model's extra work costs too much. Cross-source (Willison).
  • Simon Willison (Simon Willison, 2026-09-01) five settings with a 33x cost range on one prompt, and two of them apparently identical.

Reasoning tokens

The internal deliberation tokens a model spends before answering; Tunguz quantifies how a smaller model spends more of them to close the gap with a larger one.

Reasoning traces

Records of a model's step-by-step reasoning; as training data they are the asset Meta buys cheaply through prompt pricing, as oversight they are what monitors read, and as evidence they are what Willison inspects per effort level.
