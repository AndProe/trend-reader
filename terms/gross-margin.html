Gross margin

first seen 2026-W32 · 5 links · 3 min read

Gross margin measures what is left of a dollar of revenue after paying the direct cost of delivering the product. For a chipmaker that means wafers and packaging; for a model provider it means the compute that serves inference, before training costs and distribution revenue share. The reported numbers vary widely: Nvidia runs near 75 percent, estimates for frontier inference range from 60 to 85 percent, and one lab's margin has been tracked from minus 94 percent to 40 to 50 percent as scale and efficiency improved.

For an executive, gross margin is the bridge between AI revenue and AI valuations. Debt raised to build data centres is serviced from gross profit, so a 60 to 70 percent margin turns an interest bill into a revenue requirement. It also decides where suppliers allocate capacity: Nvidia favours data centre wafers over edge parts because the margins differ. Buyers should expect vendors with thin margins to raise prices or cut service; vendors with fat margins have room to start price wars.

Also seen as inference gross margins, serving margin

Deeper dive

What it measures. Gross margin is revenue less cost of goods sold, divided by revenue. For inference the cost of goods is the compute used to serve requests: GPU depreciation or rent, power, networking and the engineering that keeps utilisation high. It excludes training, research and sales, which sit below the gross line. That boundary matters because a headline figure above 80 percent for a lab's inference business, before distribution revenue share and before training costs, says little about whether the company is profitable.

Why the numbers differ. Nvidia's roughly 75 percent margin reflects a near-monopoly on the accelerator and a wafer allocation policy that favours high-margin data centre parts over lower-margin edge chips such as Jetson. Model providers sit one layer down and must buy that silicon, so their margins depend on Compute efficiency: batching, caching, quantisation and routing to cheaper models. Independent estimates put frontier inference margins at 60 to 85 percent, while one lab's tracked margin moved from minus 94 percent during heavy subsidy to 40 to 50 percent. The comparison of 38 against 75 percent on identical revenue is the difference between a business that rents compute and one that sells the chip. Harnesses and applications sit higher still and capture a margin wedge that the raw token price does not.

Why it matters now. Data centre build-outs are financed with debt, and gross profit is what services it, so margins convert directly into the AI revenue requirement behind each gigawatt. Revenue per gigawatt and gross margin together determine whether a campus pays for its capital, and AI valuations rest on assumptions about where margins settle as Frontier model pricing falls. Custom silicon programmes at the hyperscalers are, in accounting terms, an attempt to move Nvidia's margin onto their own income statement.

What to watch. Whether price cuts across the model tiers compress inference margins or are offset by efficiency gains. Whether labs disclose margins net of distribution revenue share. And whether Nvidia's allocation policy shifts as custom silicon reduces its pricing power.

Revenue per gigawatt

Revenue per gigawatt is the annual revenue an AI operator earns per unit of electrical capacity it has built out, with power rather than chips or servers as the denominator.

AI valuations

Revenue multiples for AI companies, public and private, and the gap between them and the rest of software: 100x-type multiples returning at the $100 million revenue milestone while public software has compressed from a 100x ceiling to about 34x.

5 linksmetric

Compute efficiency

Compute efficiency is the amount of useful capability delivered per unit of compute, combining gains on the model side, such as fewer active parameters and cheaper inference, with gains on the chip side.

6 linksmetric

SpaceX AI revenue

SpaceX AI revenue is the projected income from the company selling compute capacity, forecast to overtake its entire space business by the fourth quarter of 2026 and modelled to reach three hundred billion dollars of annual recurring revenue by the end of 2027.

3 linksconcept

Humanoid robot economics

Humanoid robot economics is the per-unit cost structure of general-purpose robots, dominated by the onboard compute, how much of the time it is used, and whether the planning model runs on the robot or in a data centre.
