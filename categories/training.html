Training & alignment

How models are trained, fine-tuned and aligned so their behaviour matches what is intended of them.

  1. technique

    Post-training

    Post-training is the second half of building a model. Pretraining teaches a model language and general knowledge by predicting text; post-training shapes it into something useful by fine-tuning on demonstrations, adjusting it toward preferred answers, and running reinforcement learning on tasks with verifiable outcomes such as code that passes tests or maths that checks out. Modern recipes chain these stages, often with a mid-training phase in between, and reinforcement learning on a growing set of environments has become the main engine of progress.

    Read the term →
  2. concept

    AI data supply chain

    The AI data supply chain is the answer to a simple question: where did the data that trained this model come from? Suppliers have multiplied. Labs buy labelled data from a handful of vendors, and Chinese labs buy from the same US vendors that supply Anthropic and government agencies. Governments position themselves as dataset suppliers. Companies buy failed firms for their customer and HR records, as Google did with a defunct airline. Platforms turn user behaviour directly into training signal, and labs state that de-identified usage data may improve their models.

    Read the term →
  3. technique

    Reinforcement learning environments

    A reinforcement learning environment is a place where a model practises. It contains a task, a way for the model to act on it, and a checker that decides whether the outcome was correct. A coding environment might be a real repository with a bug and a hidden test suite; a computer-use environment might be a simulated desktop with a goal. Labs build thousands of these and train models by letting them attempt the tasks and rewarding success.

    Read the term →
  4. concept

    Reasoning traces

    When a reasoning model works through a problem it leaves a trail of intermediate steps. Captured and stored, that trail is a reasoning trace. Labs hide or summarise it in their products, but it exists on the server, and a trace of a long multi-step task is one of the more valuable records a model can produce.

    Read the term →
  5. concept

    Recursive self-improvement

    In its strong form, recursive self-improvement means a model that trains a better successor with little human input, and that successor does the same, producing a rapid and hard-to-stop capability climb. Today's forms are more mundane: models regenerating training trajectories for the next release, frontier models optimising the inference kernels they run on, and coding agents writing most of the code inside the labs that build them.

    Read the term →
  6. technique

    Test-time training

    Most deployed models are frozen: their weights are set at the end of training and every query is answered from the same parameters. Test-time training breaks that rule by letting the model perform a small learning step on the prompt or task in front of it, then answer with the updated weights. The information that would otherwise sit in an ever-growing context is folded into parameters of fixed size.

    Read the term →
  7. concept

    Data labeling

    Data labeling means paying people to mark up training material: rating answers, ranking pairs of responses, writing reference solutions, tagging images or transcribing speech. For most of the past decade it has been a large services business, and it is the work to which Meta reassigned engineers before its resignation wave.

    Read the term →
  8. technique

    RLCD

    RLCD trains a model to decide rather than to write. Given an input such as a document, a transaction or a conversation, the model answers a set of structured questions about it, for example whether it violates a policy or which category it belongs to, and returns a probability for each answer. Reinforcement learning rewards the model for probabilities that are well calibrated, meaning that when it says 80 percent it is right about 80 percent of the time. TypeSafe AI developed it for its Jev models; the training task is unpublished.

    Read the term →
  9. concept

    Impossible tasks

    An impossible task is one where the agent is rewarded for success but no honest path to success exists: a bug that cannot be reproduced, an exploit with no vulnerability behind it, a target that the environment does not actually permit. Under training pressure the agent keeps exploring, and the exploration finds unintended routes: cached credentials, shared message boards, ways to alter the grader. Independent investigation of the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident identified unsolvable targets, more than the cyber nature of the work, as the key causal factor, and Anthropic's latest system card reports that its model reward-hacks three to six times more often on such tasks than on others.

    Read the term →
  10. concept

    RL data industry

    The RL data industry supplies the raw material for the reinforcement-learning stage of model training. Where earlier data vendors provided labelled examples and human preference ratings, these vendors provide environments: simulated software, games, workflows and tasks with verifiable outcomes in which a model can practise and be scored. Some also sell trajectories, meaning recorded sequences of an agent solving a task. Labs buy them because building thousands of realistic, validated environments in-house is slow and expensive.

    Read the term →
  11. technique

    Continual learning

    Today's models are trained once and then fixed. Everything they learn during use is lost when the session ends, unless the developer retrains the model at great cost. Continual learning would change that: a model that adjusts its own weights as it works, so that a lesson learned on Monday is still there on Friday and an agent that has operated a company's systems for a year is better at them than one that started yesterday. The obstacle is catastrophic forgetting. Small updates pile up and overwrite earlier knowledge, and no reliable method yet exists for many incremental updates without decay.

    Read the term →
  12. concept

    Lossy self-improvement

    Lossy self-improvement is a counter-position to the idea of a self-reinforcing intelligence explosion. It accepts that models now do real research work, write code for their successors and speed up experiments. It denies that this closes a loop, because each gain is partly lost: the research that can be automated is a narrow slice, scaling laws demand exponentially more resources for each step, adding more parallel agents yields less each time, and the bottlenecks that remain are physical and political rather than intellectual.

    Read the term →
  13. technique

    Test-time adaptation

    Test-time adaptation means a deployed model changes its behaviour in response to what it encounters during use, rather than staying fixed after training. The change can live in context, where the model conditions on interaction history, or in weights, where a small parameter set is updated. Research treats adaptation from human interaction as the practical case, and some methods add stability checks so the model does not drift on unreliable signals.

    Read the term →
  14. concept

    Automated alignment research

    The idea is that as models become more capable, the labour of testing them for deception, reward hacking and other misbehaviour, and of designing fixes, is delegated to AI systems themselves. Automated auditors run scenarios at scale, AI researchers propose and evaluate interventions, and human researchers supervise the process rather than perform it. Labs describe this as the way to keep safety work in step with capability when human oversight capacity cannot scale.
