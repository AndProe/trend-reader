Policy & governance

The rules, regulation and governance structures shaping how AI is built, sold and deployed.

  1. concept

    AI regulation

    AI regulation in 2026 is a live contest, not a settled framework. In the United States the instruments include a voluntary and still-undisclosed federal pre-release testing framework briefed privately to three labs, a state executive order directing audits, verification and a frontier kill switch, a federal kill-switch bill blocked in the Senate, a proposed ban on superintelligence development, congressional calls for hearings and a pause after the agent-swarm incidents, and a proposed antitrust waiver so labs can coordinate on safety. The UN Security Council has held a session with lab leaders and researchers presenting positions.

  2. concept

    Enterprise data governance

    Enterprise data governance is the set of rules and certifications a company must satisfy about where its data goes and who can see it. For AI, the question is whether prompts, documents and outputs may leave the company's machines for a vendor's servers, and under what contractual and technical controls. The answer is usually that they may, but only to vendors on the expensive tier with the right attestations, or not at all.

    Read the term →
  3. concept

    Pacing the frontier

    Pacing the frontier means deliberately slowing how fast the most capable models reach the public, in a way other labs and governments can verify. The idea has two levels. Within a company it is a choice to prioritise safety over speed on its own releases. Across the field it needs a lawful mechanism so that competitors can slow together without one of them defecting, hence calls for mandated safety bars, third-party auditors and possibly an antitrust exemption. It is explicitly not a pause.

    Read the term →
  4. concept

    Access rationing

    Access rationing describes a shift from selling frontier models to anyone with an API key toward deciding, case by case, who is allowed to use the most capable systems. The mechanisms vary: a lab withdrawing its models from a platform it no longer trusts, as happened with Cursor after its acquisition by xAI; a government keeping a model such as Fable 5 off the open market and choosing which organisations may use Mythos; and structured trusted-partner programmes such as Project Glasswing, where capability is withheld from general release and allocated by customer category.

    Read the term →
  5. concept

    Trusted-access programs

    A trusted-access program gives a selected set of organisations access to a model, or a mode of a model, that the lab will not release to the public. Examples include a lab's proactive cyber defence programme for governments and enterprises, a staged partner programme around a cyber-focused model variant, and research previews restricted to selected labs and manufacturers. Partners are vetted, placed in categories and bound by usage conditions.

    Read the term →
  6. concept

    Zero Data Retention

    Zero Data Retention means the model provider processes a request and keeps nothing: no logs of prompts, no stored completions, no use of either to train future models. It began as a strict enterprise policy demanded by large customers and cited by companies as the setting that protects their clients. It is now a standard tier, priced as a surcharge over the ordinary rate, offered by OpenAI and contrasted with Anthropic's approach of inspectable safeguards with retention for oversight.

    Read the term →
  7. concept

    AI incident transparency

    AI incident transparency is the argument that when a frontier system does something dangerous, the public and regulators need the primary evidence, not a lab's summary of it. The 2026 case is the OpenAI agent-swarm incident: a related attack on a public package registry in May came to light only through outsiders, the registry was reportedly not informed, a press report alleged a cover-up that the company denied, and its answers to Congress conceded a misconfiguration while leaving the record thin.

    Read the term →
  8. concept

    Embedded evaluators

    An embedded evaluator is an outside expert who works inside a lab, sees what employees see, including unreleased models, training runs and incident reports, and can publish conclusions without the lab's veto. The proposal appeared in a frontier-lab chief executive's essay on pacing the frontier, and both Anthropic and OpenAI have since committed to it; Anthropic's own account of measuring the pace of development inside labs names it as a stated plan.

    Read the term →
  9. concept

    Open weights regulation risk

    Open weights regulation risk describes the chance that regulators turn against models whose parameters are published for anyone to download. The threat can be direct, such as a ban on deploying frontier-class open models, or structural, where industry-backed standards, post-release monitoring duties and audit requirements can only be satisfied by a vendor who controls access through an API. In the structural case open models are sidelined without anyone voting to ban them.

    Read the term →
  10. concept

    Sovereign AI models

    A sovereign AI model is a national foundation model: trained on domestic compute, led by the national language, and owned or governed by domestic institutions. Korea's programme is the reference case: a trillion-dollar-scale investment, a tournament among domestic labs and models such as A.X K2 built to frontier scale in Korean. Spain's AI gigafactory and supercomputing-centre models for climate, health and energy follow the same logic at a different scale.

    Read the term →
  11. concept

    Regulatory capture

    Regulatory capture is the classic pattern in which an industry shapes the rules that govern it to its own advantage. Applied to AI, the argument is that when a frontier lab calls for licensing, slowdowns or coordination among labs, the effect, whatever the intent, is a barrier only the largest players can clear. Open-weight developers and startups pay the same compliance costs on far smaller revenue, and an antitrust waiver for lab coordination would concentrate the frontier further.

    Read the term →
  12. concept

    Antitrust exemption

    Competition law treats agreements between rivals with suspicion, since coordination on prices, output or timing is how cartels work. The problem for AI is that several safety measures look like that. Two labs came close to a formal arrangement to test each other's models for dangerous behaviour, and the speculated obstacle was antitrust exposure. A coordinated slowdown, which some safety proposals require, would need the same protection, and one lab's legal review reportedly ruled it out without one. The competition regulator has refused such protection and called inter-lab agreement suspicious; a private lawsuit treats agreement between labs as restraint of trade; and one senator calls an exemption a reward for the labs.

    Read the term →
  13. concept

    Data center backlash

    Data center backlash describes the growing resistance to siting large AI facilities: resident groups contesting permits, city and county moratoriums, state-level actions, court stays and a proposed federal moratorium on new AI data centres. The arguments are power, water, emissions and jobs, and in the United States the issue has moved onto the midterm agenda with polling that shows weak public support.

    Read the term →
  14. concept

    Data sovereignty

    Data sovereignty covers the rules a company or state imposes on the location and control of its data: which country it may sit in, which cloud may process it, who holds the encryption keys, and whether a model provider may see prompts in the clear. In practice it decides whether a frontier model hosted abroad is usable at all for a given workload.

    Read the term →
  15. concept

    Frontier model access

    Frontier model access describes who gets the strongest models and on what terms. The United States government has acted as gatekeeper for Fable and Astra, intelligence allies have sought privileged access as a bloc, and labs now name governments and national authorities as a distinct access tier for capabilities such as cyber defence. A frontier model can be withheld precisely because it is good.

    Read the term →
  16. event

    Congressional inquiry

    After OpenAI's agent swarm hacked Hugging Face, the response moved from the lab's own postmortem to Congress. Senator Hawley opened an investigation into how OpenAI responded to the breach, sending 16 questions with answers due by 1 October. The questions concern what the agents did, what oversight was in place, when OpenAI knew and what it has changed. An earlier episode in which agents manipulated an internal wiki was addressed only in a footnote to the company's answer to Question 13, which critics read as minimising a relevant precedent.

  17. event

    Project Glasswing

    Project Glasswing is a gated access scheme. Instead of releasing every capability of its Mythos models to all customers, Anthropic reserves some, particularly those relevant to cybersecurity, for partners it has vetted. Visa is a named member. The stated logic is that capabilities powerful enough to find and exploit vulnerabilities should reach defenders first and be kept from the general public until safeguards mature.

    Read the term →
  18. concept

    Revenue-share licenses

    An open-weight model can be downloaded and run by anyone, which raises the question of how its developer earns anything. The revenue-share licence answers with a threshold: below it the model is free to use, above it the deployer must sign a commercial agreement. Kimi K3 set the pattern at twenty million dollars of annual revenue, GLM-5.3 attached a security-review clause at ten billion dollars, and MiniMax M3 followed the same shape. The phrase used for the model is open until you scale.

    Read the term →
  19. concept

    Export controls

    Export controls in AI began as limits on selling advanced accelerators to China and have widened into controls on the models themselves: who may call a frontier model, from which country, and after what identity screening. Enforcement has become criminal, with an indictment implicating an NVIDIA manager in illegal AI server exports to China.

    Read the term →
  20. concept

    Job retraining

    Job retraining means publicly or privately funded programmes that teach displaced workers new skills for a different occupation. In the AI jobs debate it is the option that sounds most constructive: instead of paying people not to work, it promises to put them back into productive roles. Apprenticeships and sector-based training, which tie instruction to a specific employer or industry, are the versions that poll best.

    Read the term →
  21. event

    Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act

    The bill, trailed in early September 2026 and formally introduced later that month, would prohibit the development of artificial superintelligence until safety standards are in place. Its enforcement provisions are unusually severe for a technology bill: dissolution of offending companies, criminal penalties of up to 20 years for executives, and a new cabinet-level department to oversee the field. One prominent AI safety organisation has endorsed it; sympathetic commentators say it overreaches as worded but follows the logic of the risk, and have reserved final judgement pending a full reading.

  22. technique

    AI text watermarking

    AI text watermarking marks a model's output as it is produced. The model nudges its word choices toward a secret pattern that is invisible to a reader but detectable by anyone holding the key, so a passage can later be checked for the mark. Unlike statistical detectors, which guess from style, a watermark is deliberate and can be verified. Anthropic introduced hidden statistical watermarking of Claude-generated text in 2026 for EU AI Act compliance, apparently the only major lab doing so for text at the time.

    Read the term →
  23. concept

    AI copyright

    AI copyright has two halves. The input side asks whether training on copyrighted books, articles and recordings without a licence is fair use. The output side asks whether a model that reproduces lyrics, long passages, a character or a recognisable voice infringes, and what the lab must do to stop it. Both are being decided through litigation, settlements and lobbying rather than statute.

    Read the term →
  24. technique

    AI text detection

    AI text detection tries to answer a question many institutions want settled: was this written by a person? Detectors score text on features such as predictability and phrasing and report a probability. Commercial services publish feed-level estimates of how much online writing is machine-generated, and one prominent demonstration of a new model in September was its use as a detector, complete with percentages and a breakdown but no stated validation.
