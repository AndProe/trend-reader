Society & philosophy

The wider debates AI raises about work, cognition, culture and how society is organised.

  1. concept

    Singularity debate

    A technological singularity is the point at which AI improves itself faster than humans can follow, producing a rapid and open-ended capability climb. In 2026 several lab leaders have said the industry is already entering self-accelerating development, and the chief executive of the leading chip vendor has made an AGI claim of his own. Named researchers and commentators have countered that progress has stalled, that the scaling-law evidence does not support a hard takeoff, or that people are bandwagoning towards the claim.

  2. concept

    AI slop

    AI slop is what happens when generation becomes free and attention does not. A pitch, a pull request, a report or a piece of code that took seconds to produce lands on a person who must spend minutes or hours evaluating it, and most of it is not worth the time. The defining property is the asymmetry: the sender's cost falls to nothing while the reader's cost stays fixed or rises, so the rational response of recipients is to stop reading.

  3. concept

    Preference cascade

    A preference cascade is a social mechanism: many people hold a view but stay quiet because each believes they are in the minority, until a few visible admissions reveal the true distribution and the rest follow rapidly. In AI the cascade began with a policy commentator admitting he had held back his concerns and became an avalanche after a researcher resigned from Anthropic with a public warning. Within days staff across OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind were saying in public what they had said in private, with extinction estimates from around 10 to 50 percent and calls for coordinated slowdown.

  4. concept

    Cognitive debt

    Technical debt is the cost of shortcuts in code; cognitive debt is the cost of shortcuts in understanding. Each time a person accepts a model's answer without checking it, an act called cognitive surrender, they save minutes now and lose a piece of the understanding they would have built by doing the work. Across a team over a year it produces engineers who ship systems they cannot explain, and who lose system knowledge as AI handles the incidents they once handled.

  5. concept

    No-AI writing policy

    A no-AI writing policy is a stated editorial commitment. The publication declares that its text is human-written, that AI tools may be used to research, gather sources or check spelling, and that any research produced by AI is verified before use. A prominent engineering newsletter adopted this stance publicly on its fifth anniversary and applies the same rule to an essay competition it runs.

  6. concept

    Universal basic income

    Universal basic income (UBI) pays every adult a regular cash floor regardless of work status. Advocates present it as the cleanest answer to AI displacing labour, citing extensive pilot evidence and arguing that retraining cannot keep pace with automation. Opponents, including a well-funded organised opposition, argue it would sever the link between work and income and is politically unacceptable; polling places it among the least popular AI-related policies, and some analysts consider a jobs guarantee the likelier endpoint.

  7. concept

    AI in education

    AI in education is two stories at once. In the classroom, the question is whether students learn more or less with AI, and the emerging answer is that it depends on how it is used: a study reported in 2026 found that using AI as a shortcut to answers degrades learning, while using it as a tutor that explains and questions does not. Outside the classroom, survey evidence shows students changing majors as they read the job market, and schools pausing to rewrite policy.

    Read the term →
  8. concept

    AI consciousness

    AI consciousness asks whether a model experiences anything at all, and if so whether it deserves moral consideration or legal rights. There is no agreed test for the question, and the leading positions range from confident denial to the view that the possibility is real enough to warrant caution in how systems are trained and shut down.
