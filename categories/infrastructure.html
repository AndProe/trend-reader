Compute & infrastructure

The compute, chips and data-centre economics that frontier AI is built and served on.

  1. concept

    Hyperscalers

    The hyperscalers are the handful of companies operating cloud infrastructure at global scale. Their AI businesses are growing 37 to 82 percent a year, they lock up the accelerator pipeline years ahead, and they are the gatekeepers of investment-grade capital for data centre projects, sometimes described as gigascalers to mark the shift to gigawatt campuses. They are also the buyers of behind-the-meter power capacity and the owners of the enterprise tiers, such as Bedrock and Vertex, through which regulated companies consume models.

  2. concept

    Agentic inference workloads

    A chat request is one prompt and one answer. An agent on a task makes dozens or hundreds of model calls, each carrying the whole history so far, pausing while it runs tests or reads files, and sometimes spawning several parallel agents. Most of each request repeats the previous one, so the serving system can reuse cached computation if it keeps the cache available. Agents also consume CPU, because they compile, test and lint between model calls, one reported driver of CPU shortages.

    Read the term →
  3. concept

    Custom silicon

    The largest buyers of AI hardware have concluded that paying Nvidia's margin on every accelerator is too expensive at their scale, and each has designed its own chip. Google's TPUs are the most mature and are now sold to outside buyers, including through special-purpose vehicles that account the sales gross. Amazon has Trainium, Meta has MTIA, and OpenAI's first-generation Jalapeño is reported to compete with Nvidia's Blackwell rather than trail it. Alongside these are purpose-built inference systems from Cerebras, whose wafer-scale CS-4 targets the fastest serving tiers, and Groq.

  4. concept

    On-device inference

    On-device inference is the practice of running an AI model where the work happens rather than in a vendor's data centre. The model's weights are downloaded once and executed on local processors, which can be a workstation GPU, a laptop's neural engine, a phone chip or the onboard computer of a robot. Quality is now close to cloud frontier models for most everyday tasks, with the main cost being longer response times on smaller hardware.

    Read the term →
  5. concept

    Interactivity versus throughput

    An inference server can either serve many users slowly or few users quickly. Batching many requests together keeps the accelerator busy and maximises tokens per GPU, but each user waits longer for each token. Serving fewer requests at once gives each user a fast stream but leaves compute idle. The relationship is a curve, and the numbers are steep: roughly thirty times the aggregate throughput is given up to make one user's stream ten times faster. That is why ultrafast tiers are priced as premium products, and why every hardware comparison must specify the per-user speed at which it was measured.

  6. concept

    GPU access

    GPU access describes the allocation of scarce accelerator capacity. Hyperscalers and the largest labs contract the pipeline years ahead, so most other buyers face shortages, rationing or refusal of capacity at any lease length. Nvidia's allocation decisions sit on the supply side; the demand side is a contest among clouds, neo clouds, labs and enterprises. The scarcity has recently spread from GPUs to CPUs as agent workloads multiply.

  7. metric

    Performance per watt

    Performance per watt is an efficiency ratio: tokens generated, or some measure of intelligence delivered, divided by the power consumed to produce it. It extends a long-standing computing metric, computations per joule, to language models and the hardware that serves them. Chip designers, including OpenAI with its Jalapeño chip, now design for this ratio directly, and hardware comparisons are increasingly argued in tokens per second per megawatt rather than per dollar.

  8. technique

    Smart model routing

    Most requests an AI system receives are easy. Smart model routing exploits that by putting a decision in front of the model call: a small, fast model classifies the request and either answers it or forwards it to a larger, more expensive one. In practice a local model might handle eight of thirty-one incoming emails outright and defer the rest, or a router might send simple agentic jobs to local models and complex ones to a cloud frontier model.

    Read the term →
  9. concept

    Edge AI

    Edge AI means inference happens where the data is: on a laptop, a workstation, a vehicle, a drone or a device, instead of on rented cloud GPUs. It is the deployment and hardware side of the story; on-device inference is the model side. The analogy in use is the move from mainframes to personal computers, with open-weight models as the software that made personal hardware worth buying.

    Read the term →
  10. concept

    Neo clouds

    Neo clouds are the specialist cloud companies that rent out GPUs and serve models without being one of the large general-purpose clouds. Some, such as CoreWeave, build large data centres and lease capacity to labs and enterprises; others, such as the inference providers, host open-weights models and sell tokens at competitive prices. The category has grown fast: one operator reports a 4.2-gigawatt footprint and a backlog above one hundred billion dollars, and non-hyperscaler buyers now account for the majority of new data-centre revenue.

    Read the term →
  11. metric

    CPU pricing

    Server CPUs were the one part of an AI data centre nobody expected to run short. GPUs did the model work, and CPUs sat alongside them doing housekeeping. Agents changed that: every tool call, sandbox, file operation and web request an agent makes runs on ordinary processors, so the amount of CPU work per unit of GPU work has risen sharply. Intel's server CPU average selling prices rose 27 percent year on year while unit volumes fell, and spot availability has since disappeared, with list prices up 10 to 20 percent and six-month lead times.

    Read the term →
  12. concept

    Chinese AI chips

    For several years the working assumption was that Chinese AI would be bottlenecked by access to Nvidia GPUs. That assumption is now in question. Huawei's Ascend accelerators and their successors are being used to train large models end to end, and Chinese serving stacks built around them handle very large volumes, with GLM 5.3 Flash reported serving 100 trillion tokens a day on domestic silicon. The next generation, the Ascend 960DT, has been pulled forward, and cluster claims run to a quarter of a million cards.

    Read the term →
  13. concept

    CUDA moat

    CUDA is the software layer through which almost all AI code talks to Nvidia GPUs. Over nearly two decades it has accumulated libraries, compilers, debugging tools and, most importantly, a habit: every new model, kernel and serving framework is written for CUDA first. The moat is that habit. When a new serving engine release ships, it works on every Nvidia part on day zero, while competing hardware can lag by hours or days and often needs vendor-specific patches.

  14. metric

    Electricity prices

    Electricity prices have become the most visible cost of the AI buildout. Tallies in the vault include a 6.9 percent rise in retail power prices attributed to data-centre demand, household bill increases of 17 to 24 percent in the PJM region of the eastern United States, and warnings that data-centre load could triple natural gas prices. The Ohio bill increases are described as the trigger for the wave of local moratoriums.

  15. concept

    Grid interconnection

    A grid interconnection is the permission and physical works that let a new large load, such as a data centre, or a new generator draw from or feed into the utility grid. Grid operators process requests through a queue with studies of grid impact, and the queue now stretches for years. Fast-track schemes announced to speed it up have, to date, not energised a single megawatt, and Texas has paused the next step of its ERCOT queue as a state-level instrument of control.

    Read the term →
  16. concept

    Ultra-low latency

    Ultra-low latency means a system answers fast enough that the delay does not change the outcome, whether that is a trade executed before the market moves, a game loop that stays smooth or an agent that acts inside a browser race. In trading it has become table stakes rather than the edge. In AI it is the target of persistent-kernel and wafer-scale inference engineering and the basis of the paid fast tiers that vendors now sell.

    Read the term →
  17. metric

    Compute efficiency

    Compute efficiency asks how much intelligence a system produces for each dollar of hardware and electricity it consumes. Improvements come from two directions. On the model side, architectures that activate only a fraction of their parameters per token, better training data, and serving techniques that avoid recomputation all deliver the same quality for less compute. On the chip side, each hardware generation delivers more operations per watt. The two multiply, which is why the cost of a fixed level of capability has fallen so fast.

    Read the term →
  18. concept

    Behind-the-meter power

    A data centre normally buys power from the grid through a utility connection, which requires an interconnection agreement, upgrades to transmission, and participation in regional capacity markets. Behind-the-meter power puts generation on the developer's own side of the meter: gas turbines, reciprocating engines, fuel cells or renewables with storage, sized to run the facility with the grid as backup or not at all. Around 75 gigawatts of such projects have been ordered, and each must pass through several gates, from equipment delivery to permitting and fuel supply, before first power.

  19. product

    Jalapeno chip

    Jalapeno is a custom processor that OpenAI designed for inference, the work of serving answers from a trained model rather than training it. Broadcom built it, the design process used AI-assisted tooling, and the programme went from start to a finished chip in sixteen months. OpenAI reports that on single-token prediction it produces more tokens per megawatt than Nvidia's Blackwell generation, while Nvidia remains its preferred partner.

    Read the term →
  20. technique

    Prefill-decode disaggregation

    Serving a language model has two phases. Prefill reads the whole prompt at once and is compute-heavy; decode produces one token at a time and is limited by memory bandwidth. Running both on the same accelerator wastes capacity, because each phase leaves the other's bottleneck idle. Disaggregation puts prefill on one set of machines and decode on another, handing over the intermediate state, the KV cache, across a fast network. Inference vendors and the leading GPU stack use it as the default for large deployments.

    Read the term →
  21. product

    HBM

    HBM stacks several DRAM dies vertically and connects them to the accelerator through a very wide interface, giving far more bandwidth than ordinary memory modules. Every frontier GPU depends on it, and memory makers have shifted wafer capacity toward it: SK Hynix has committed on the order of $720 billion to sustained AI-driven memory demand. The side effect is a shortage of conventional DRAM and SSDs, with prices roughly quadrupling and a lobbying fight in Washington over production mandates tied to CHIPS Act funding.

    Read the term →
  22. concept

    Ultrafast mode

    Ultrafast mode is a premium tier where the vendor runs its model on hardware tuned for speed rather than throughput and charges more for the result. OpenAI's version, backed by Cerebras systems, runs GPT-5.6 Sol roughly 14 to 15 times faster at added cost; Chinese vendors including Xiaomi and Z.ai sell comparable high-speed tiers built on GPU runtime engineering. Inside OpenAI the tier is reserved mostly for external customers and used internally for incident response.

    Read the term →
  23. product

    Vera Rubin

    Vera Rubin is the platform Nvidia follows Blackwell with. It pairs a new GPU with HBM4 memory, comes in rack-scale configurations such as NVL72, and is claimed to deliver about 5.4 times the performance per megawatt of GB200, with the first agentic benchmark results on the NVL72 rack reporting large gains in performance per dollar. The transition from Blackwell is described as easier than the Hopper-to-Blackwell move, with changes concentrated in network speed and rack power.

    Read the term →
  24. metric

    Energy efficiency

    Energy efficiency measures how much useful model output a system produces for each joule of electricity it draws. In research it is reported as tokens per joule or as GPU energy per benchmark task, and one sustainability framework for edge models treats measured energy as a first-class pillar alongside accuracy and cost. It is the research-side twin of performance per watt, which is the hardware vendor's framing of the same quantity.

    Read the term →
  25. concept

    Object storage

    Object storage is a giant labelled locker in the cloud. Files of any size and type go in under a key, are retrieved by that key, and cost a small amount per gigabyte per month with no capacity planning. Unlike a database it does not query the content; unlike a disk it does not belong to any one machine. AWS S3 established the model in 2006 and every cloud now offers an equivalent.

    Read the term →
  26. concept

    Orbital data centers

    An orbital data center is a cluster of AI processors mounted on satellites rather than housed in a building. The pitch is that space offers near-constant solar power without grid connection, no land acquisition, and no neighbours to object. Starcloud is the startup most associated with the idea, and a space-optimised compute system involving SpaceX and Nvidia has been described in the same vein. The limiting factor is not money but rockets: how much hardware can be lifted and how often.
