Work & engineering culture

How AI is changing engineering organisations, roles and the practice of building software.

  1. concept

    Engineering culture

    Engineering culture is the unwritten operating system of a software organisation: how much autonomy teams have, whether experimentation and A/B testing are normal, how best practice spreads internally, and how much goodwill engineers extend to leadership. It is set from the VP of engineering and CTO seats, which is also where reorganisations under AI originate.

  2. concept

    AI labor displacement

    AI labor displacement is the labour-market effect of automation reaching white-collar work. The evidence is mixed. One tally attributes 113,000 layoffs to AI, 24 percent of announced cuts, while chief executives grow cautious about saying so publicly. Individual cases are stark: a ten-person function reduced to 1.25 people. Yet net-jobs estimates show small aggregate effects so far, and some companies that cut engineers are now re-hiring them.

  3. event

    Project OT

    Project OT was an internal Meta initiative to reorganise engineering around AI. The premise was that with coding agents, teams could be 60 percent smaller and still deliver the same output, so the company planned to shrink teams accordingly. The plan was cancelled. The reported reason is that the productivity gains did not show up where it mattered: the volume of code produced rose, but the number of features shipped stayed flat.

  4. concept

    AI productivity

    AI productivity is the question behind every 3x claim. Careful practitioners have revised what they think it is. One revision from writing: AI did not cut the effort a piece took, it raised the quality reachable at the same effort. Another from engineering: OpenAI's figure of 3.1 agent-workdays per human shift is better read as machine runtime than as better thinking, and since roughly half of long tasks need human intervention, delivered work about doubles rather than triples.

    Read the term →
  5. event

    Meta engineering exodus

    The Meta engineering exodus refers to the departures of experienced Meta engineers over the summer of 2026. The sequence, as reported, began with layoffs in May, continued with an internal plan to reduce teams by around sixty percent on the basis that AI would replace the work, and included reassigning senior engineers to labelling data for the company's models. When the deeper cuts were cancelled the damage was already done: engineers who had been told they were redundant, or moved to work they considered beneath their skills, left for the leading AI laboratories and for competitors.

  6. concept

    Engineering as cost center

    Engineering as cost center describes a shift in how leadership accounts for its developers. In a company where engineering is a profit centre, more engineers mean more product and more revenue, and the question is how to hire and retain them. In a company where engineering is overhead, the question is how to do the same work with fewer people, and AI tools become the justification for a target such as reducing teams by 60 percent.

    Read the term →
  7. concept

    AI fatigue

    AI fatigue is what happens to engineers when AI adoption becomes a mandate rather than a tool. It shows up as burnout and loss of drive under usage quotas, as weariness with a hype cycle that oversells every release, as disgust at the low-quality output that floods review queues, and as fear about what the technology means for a career. Experienced engineering leaders separate these varieties because they call for different answers.

  8. concept

    Org flattening

    Org flattening means fewer managers, fewer layers between the top of an engineering organisation and the people writing code, and wider spans of control for the managers who remain. The trend predates the current AI cycle, but AI has given it new justification: if agents raise individual output, leaders argue that fewer people and fewer coordinators are needed, and executives who frame engineering as a cost centre treat management layers as overhead to cut.

    Read the term →
  9. concept

    IDE decline

    The integrated development environment has been the engineer's primary workspace for decades. Inside OpenAI, IDE usage has been falling since January 2026 as engineers moved their work into Codex threads and, from July, into ChatGPT Work, while load on build, test and deploy systems rose roughly tenfold. The engineer now describes intent, reviews output and manages several agents rather than typing code line by line. Long-run observers of engineering practice list it among the largest shifts of the past five years, and treat it as emerging rather than settled.

  10. concept

    Mercenary culture

    Mercenary culture describes teams whose members no longer believe the company will treat them fairly and therefore treat employment as a purely transactional arrangement. They stay while the pay is high, decline discretionary effort, keep résumés current and leave as soon as a better offer appears. The current reference case is Meta, where layoffs, reassignments of senior engineers to data-labelling work and retention grants aimed at those who stayed left, by the accounts in the vault, a workforce with morale gone and a resignation wave under way.

    Read the term →
  11. concept

    AI lab talent competition

    AI lab talent competition describes a market in which the same few hundred people are bid for by every frontier lab, by AI startups, by private-equity firms building deployment teams and by the trading shops that used to be the top payers. Labs and startups are out-hiring Big Tech for engineers, Anthropic and OpenAI recruit directly from proprietary trading firms, senior researchers leave established labs to found new ones, and Amazon and Meta have been re-hiring engineers they laid off or reassigned.

    Read the term →
  12. concept

    Quantitative trading

    Quantitative trading firms, also called proprietary trading firms or prop shops, trade with their own money rather than clients'. Their systems watch market data, decide whether to act, and send orders, and for years the winning edge was doing that loop faster than anyone else, measured in microseconds. The current era is different: raw speed is largely commoditised, and the edge comes from better models of what will happen next.

  13. concept

    Hands-on executives

    The term describes senior people who returned to building as models improved: directors and VPs prototyping on weekends, founders shipping features and overriding their engineering leaders on the strength of what they built. Its popular name is founder mode. Those who study engineering organisations treat the shift as permanent rather than a phase, and new measures of AI leadership look at what leaders themselves do with the tools, judging, explaining, learning and coordinating, rather than what they sponsor.

    Read the term →
  14. concept

    Data center jobs

    Data center jobs refers to the labour demand generated by building and powering AI facilities: electricians, pipefitters, concrete and steel crews, substation and transmission work, and the operations staff who run the sites afterwards. The net picture the vault carries is roughly one million jobs created against about 200,000 announced AI-related layoffs, although the two figures fall on different workers in different places.

  15. concept

    Shadow AI

    Employees who find AI useful do not wait for procurement. They paste documents into consumer chatbots, run personal subscriptions on work tasks and wire up agents with their own credentials. That is shadow AI. It is usually discovered by security teams as a data-leakage risk and answered with a ban, which stops the reporting rather than the usage.

  16. concept

    AI on the resume

    AI on the resume is the career-side consequence of the shift to agent-driven engineering. Two years ago experience with AI coding tools was a plus; in 2026 experienced engineering leaders describe it as a requirement, and advise anyone who has been sceptical to get practical experience quickly, because hiring managers now screen for it and its absence reads as a signal of disengagement.

    Read the term →
  17. concept

    Closed contributions

    Open-source projects have traditionally accepted code changes from anyone willing to submit them, subject to review. Coding agents changed the arithmetic: a contributor can now generate a plausible-looking pull request in minutes without understanding the project, and popular repositories receive them by the thousand. Reviewing each one costs a maintainer more than the contribution is worth, so a small number of high-profile AI projects have adopted a blunt rule of not accepting external pull requests by policy, while others restrict AI-written submissions. Three projects have done so at the time of writing, and the expectation is that the practice spreads. The Linux kernel, by contrast, allows AI-written contributions.

    Read the term →
  18. concept

    Manager-to-IC moves

    A manager-to-IC move is a director, engineering manager or head of engineering choosing to return to writing code and shipping work directly. Two motives dominate in current accounts: stability, since management layers are the first cut when companies flatten, and skills, since leaders who spent years in meetings want to learn how AI-assisted engineering actually works before they are asked to direct it.

    Read the term →
  19. concept

    Tokenmaxxing

    Tokenmaxxing describes what happens when a company measures AI adoption by consumption. Engineers learn that dashboards track tokens used or prompts sent, and they generate usage to look compliant: running agents on trivial tasks, letting sessions run long, or feeding the model work they would have done faster by hand. The term began as an early warning and is now quoted back as a grievance by engineers who resent being scored on it.

    Read the term →
  20. concept

    Retainer equity grants

    Most technology companies grant equity at hiring and then top it up each year with a refresher. Retainer grants are a departure from that routine: a one-off award made to someone who has handed in their notice, in the hope of changing their mind. During its 2026 engineering exodus Meta began making such grants, a practice new for the company, while the size of its annual refreshers became the single factor determining whether people stayed.
