About Trend Reader

The AI vocabulary, read into one map.

Trend Reader is a reading system, not a news site. It reads what the people building and analysing AI publish, promotes the words that keep recurring into defined terms, and connects each term to the ideas it depends on. What matters is shown by the links between ideas, not by how much was written.

by Andreas Proesch · updated 2026-09-25

Who

Trend Reader is written and maintained by Andreas Proesch, who works on the business side of AI and keeps himself at its technical frontier by reading, every week, what the people building it publish. The site is the result: a living reference of the terms that matter, each explained for a technically literate executive and connected to the ideas it depends on. Definitions and explainers are his own, written from how a term is actually used, never lifted from a vendor. Weekly briefs and research notes are working analysis, not opinion pieces.

Method

Six steps, run every week.

  1. Read

    Every Monday the reader collects the week's writing from the people building and analysing AI: essays, papers, talks and newsletters.

  2. Gate

    Each piece passes a relevance gate written in the vault's own vocabulary; off-topic pieces are set aside, never silently dropped.

  3. Note

    One note per piece: a summary in plain language, the key data points, the terms it uses, and the author's own conclusion.

  4. Define

    A label that keeps recurring becomes a canonical term with a two-sentence definition written from how it is actually used, and is linked to the ideas it depends on. Terms are ideas and technology, never companies.

  5. Report

    An analyst report reads across everything: what different people said, the trends they agree on, what is new, and what deserves deeper analysis.

  6. Research

    One queued topic is researched each week from the underlying notes: the claim stated precisely, the evidence, the contradictions, and what would falsify it.

The weekly brief

A weekly email edition is in preparation: the terms that moved, the report that explains why, and one research note. Until it launches, the Home page is refreshed after every Monday run, and the Research page carries every report in full.
